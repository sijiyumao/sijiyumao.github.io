On Monday morning, Ren Jinhuan returned to the office. He shared his Malaysian snacks and souvenirs with his team members and brought some to his peers in the department. Sure enough, someone brought up that tweet along the way, openly complimenting him on how good he looked on camera and suggesting they could use his photo as a selling point for the next campus recruitment drive. He didn’t go into much detail and simply replied with polite small talk.

Ren Jinhuan worked in the User Growth Department under the E-Commerce Division, led by Wen Yan. A year after graduation, he followed Wen Yan from the Social Media Division to the E-Commerce division. At the time, the department was in urgent need of talent, and seeing that his work performance was on par with those who had been there for three or four years, Wen Yan promoted him to team leader. Later, he participated in a major company-wide project, earned an “S” performance rating, and was promoted to senior team leader the following year.

At the end of the team meeting, Ren Jinhuan kept two team leaders behind: one named Xin Cheng and the other named Jiang Yao. Both were the same age as Ren Jinhuan but held him in high regard. Ren Jinhuan asked if there had been any new feedback from the CEO’s briefing the previous week. The department held a weekly update meeting with Boss Fang every Wednesday regarding overseas operations; since Ren Jinhuan had been on a business trip the previous week, he had asked Wen Yan to take Xin Cheng and Jiang Yao to the meeting.

Jiang Yao, who was known for speaking his mind, said, “After Yan-ge finished the report, I got the feeling that Mr. Fang wasn’t very satisfied because overseas user retention hasn’t shown any improvement.” Since the overseas project launched, the number of existing users retained each month had indeed fallen short of expectations. The operations team had tried many approaches, but to little effect.

Xin Cheng thought for a moment and shared his thoughts: “I think the CEO’s concern might not be limited to retention. Currently, most of our new overseas users are being driven in through social media, and we aren’t targeting the right audience. The executives are aware of this, so he might be suggesting that there’s a problem with our growth model.”

Ren Jinhuan agreed with him. The overseas e-commerce business had actually always been integrated into Aladdin’s domestic e-commerce app. A long time ago, CEO Fang had suggested creating a standalone platform to handle the overseas segment, but Wen Yan had argued at the time that development would consume too many resources and that independent operations after launch would be costly, and that it would be better to let the domestic platform drive growth. While Wen Yan’s reasoning made sense, it wasn’t a sustainable long-term solution. When CEO Fang later asked him about the development progress, he replied that they were still discussing the proposal with the product team, so CEO Fang never brought up the matter again.

This was actually a warning sign. When senior executives at a large company proposed an idea, it was often the result of careful consideration. As a subordinate, the wisest course of action was to act swiftly. If the idea is raised a second time, it indicates that it’s an order, not a suggestion. Ren Jinhuan had once hinted to Wen Yan that he should consider developing an overseas app, but to no avail, so he gave up on the idea as well. This, too, presented a dilemma—when your direct supervisor’s ideas conflict with those of his superior, you have no choice but to side with your supervisor; otherwise, you’re overstepping your bounds. Ren Jinhuan knew that the reason CEO Fang hadn’t brought up the matter again wasn’t because he’d abandoned the idea, but because he’d given up on Wen Yan.

Thinking about this, Ren Jinhuan had also wondered whether Wen Yan was the right leader for him. Back when the E-Commerce Division was still in its “barren period,” he had followed Wen Yan over because he knew the group was short-staffed; by joining, he would stand out from the crowd and be able to rise through the ranks faster. He was driven purely by self-interest, and the results proved that he had made the right bet. Wen Yan had indeed recognized his talent and promoted him, but their management styles were incompatible. Ren Jinhuan was willing to cooperate, but he didn’t feel comfortable doing so.



During his lunch break, Ren Jinhuan took some gifts to visit acquaintances in other departments, eventually arriving at the Strategic Investment Department. The Strategic Investment Department was primarily responsible for Aladdin’s external industrial investments, mergers, and acquisitions. The staff were mostly recruited from Ivy League schools and investment banks. At the same time, this was the department that experienced the greatest contrast in daily life. In their own words, they looked presentable at 10 a.m. but were a mess by 10 p.m., truly embodying the saying “all show and no substance.” Fortunately, it was currently only 1:00 p.m.

There weren’t many people in the Strategic Investment Department, and Ren Jinhuan had gotten to know most of them after visiting a few times. One colleague, knowing the reason for his visit, teased him: “Manager Ren, you’ve been popping by here so often. Isn’t Boss Wen worried you’ll switch departments?”

Ren Jinhuan tossed him a bag of coconut cakes and said cheerfully, “Who knows? Maybe I’m here to poach talent. Want to join our department?”

The colleague quickly made a “NO” gesture with his hand, then pointed to a spot behind him and said, “Shi Lu is over there.”

Sure enough, Ren Jinhuan spotted a striking figure at a desk by the window with thick, even black hair, though it was a bit disheveled at the crown right now. He placed the two bottles of TF perfume on the desk that Shi Lu had asked him to buy for her before his business trip, finally giving her a temporary reprieve from the grueling work on her economic efficiency model.

Shi Lu and Ren Jinhuan met when they were competitors in debate during university. Although they attended different universities, they faced off several times in the East China university debate circuit, with Ren Jinhuan currently holding a narrow lead of one win. At one point, people on the campus network tried to pair them up, prompting Shi Lu to change her signature for a while to: “First, I love making money; second, I love math; third, I love straight guys.” After graduation, the two accidentally discovered they were both working at Aladdin, and so their relationship shifted from “chance enemies” to “partners in information.”

Shi Lu led him to a secluded corner of the break room and said, “I have two pieces of news related to you. First, Boss Wen is indeed in a tight spot. The newly established overseas supply chain team at the Shenzhen branch will be officially announced next month, and headquarters plans to pull someone from Boss Wen’s team to head it.”

Ren Jinhuan had had a premonition about this when the unit was first established. Although it had over three hundred staff members, most were informal employees such as outsourced workers and third-party contractors. The role would mainly involve managing people. Moreover, being transferred from headquarters to a branch office was, from ancient times, considered a ‘demotion’—that is, a promotion in name only but a demotion in reality—and it would be no easy task to return to Beijing in the future.

“But don’t worry, Wen Yan should have your back when the time comes,” Shi Lu said. “However, the second matter has a lot to do with you.”

Ren Jinhuan asked what it was. Shi Lu asked if he’d checked the intranet forum. He’d been in meetings all morning and hadn’t had a moment to look, so Shi Lu sent him a series of screenshots: “This is a trending post from this morning. Someone reposted that article from Today Post.”

Ren Jinhuan scrolled through them one by one. It all started with a poster asking, “Is this a sponsored post our company paid for? Such a drastic change from Today’s usual style…” Soon, someone replied in the comments, saying it was clearly a department head trying to promote their own protégé. No names were mentioned, but the reference was clearly to Wen Yan and himself. Then others began questioning the speed of his promotion, asking how he could have made it into management just two years after graduation. Of course, some people joked, “Aren’t the envious people in this thread just those old-timers who failed to get promoted and who’ve been rotting in the same ranks for ages? They’re just jealous of the younger generation…” Someone even commented, “Good looks really are priceless. Rumours directed at ordinary people are nothing short of a disaster, but when they’re about attractive men and women, you guys actually end up imbuing them with a tragic beauty.”

Eventually, the tone of the discussion shifted toward the conflict between veterans and newcomers: the former were labeled “old hands” who were just coasting through their careers and manipulating others to pad their seniority, while the latter were dismissed as “greenhorns” who didn’t understand the business, yet were arrogant and conceited.

“After the number of replies broke 100, it was quickly deleted, probably by the Security Management Department.”

The tweet had indeed blown up, Ren Jinhuan thought to himself. It seemed he’d have to stabilize Wen Yan’s trust in him for the time being while also finding a new ship to board as soon as possible.

Just then, Shi Lu said to him mysteriously, “I have some breaking news, something I managed to pry out of my boss this morning.” She flashed a smug smile, as if the information she was about to share was incredibly valuable. Shi Lu had striking, elegant features, a classic beauty, and was both flexible and capable at her job; Ren Jinhuan considered her a smart person.

“I’ll give you a 20% discount on the perfume,” Ren Jinhuan said tactfully.

“The head of overseas production and logistics is coming next Monday.”

“Next Monday?” Ren Jinhuan exclaimed in surprise. Thinking back to their awkward encounter at the bar the day before yesterday, he felt his previous plans had gone down the drain, plunging from what he’d thought would be a perfect start to a hellishly difficult situation. He hadn’t yet figured out how to salvage the situation.

Seeing the grave expression on his face, Shi Lu asked what was wrong. Ren Jinhuan simply said he’d been swamped with work lately, but this was truly a tough problem. So, feeling uncertain, he tentatively asked, “If you had a misunderstanding with a very important person right at your first meeting, and you accidentally…‘insulted’ them, how would you salvage the relationship?” He chose his words carefully, using the term “insult” to describe his three-hundred-yuan prank.

“Is the other person a man or a woman?” Shi Lu asked.

“Does it make a difference?”

“Of course. If it’s a woman, let her insult you back—give her the satisfaction of getting the last word. If it’s a man, keep insulting him to stir up his competitive spirit.” Back in her debating days, Shi Lu was particularly famous for her witty one-liners. Everyone assumed she had a colorful romantic history, but only Ren Jinhuan knew it was all theory, never put into practice. After hearing this, he clapped lightly and laughed: “That’s a high-risk, low-return move. If I were your boss, I wouldn’t approve a proposal with such a low RAROC[^1].”

Shi Lu paid him no mind and began unwrapping the perfumes. This time, Ren Jinhuan had picked out the Tobacco & Agarwood: Strong Edition and Italian Cypress for her. Shi Lu was an avid TF perfume enthusiast, particularly fond of woody notes. Ren Jinhuan suddenly recalled the scent on Jin Xiangtang that night. Although he couldn’t recreate the exact fragrance, he still remembered that feeling, so he asked Shi Lu, “Does TF have any agarwood fragrance with a rounded, cool, and slightly metallic sensation, like that of pearls?”

When Shi Lu said she hadn’t smelled it before, Ren Jinhuan let the matter drop. In truth, he couldn’t guarantee the accuracy of his description either. It was just that the scent of the perfume evoked a vague sense of familiarity, and scenes flashed through his mind—scenes that seemed to have happened, yet also seemed not to have happened—such as a snowy night filled with rising heat, the heady scent of alcohol, a cramped, confined space, a dim, yellowish light from a makeshift bulb, and—long, warm fingers, and breaths laced with desire.

Just like that fleeting olfactory memory, these vague fragments were hard to trace, leaving only the regrets of colourful clouds dispersed, and beautiful dreams left unfulfilled.
