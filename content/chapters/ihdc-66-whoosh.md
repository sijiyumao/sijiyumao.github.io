Dark Raven?

Upon seeing this hero, both the commentators and the audience looked surprised.

Although it was a well known fact that Ling Suyu preferred sustained-damage marksmen, heroes like Cat Spirit, Desert Hunter, and Candy Witch all had mobility-related skills.

As for Dark Raven, while it wasn’t accurate to say she had no mobility, her only means of movement came from her ultimate. Without it, her survival capabilities were extremely weak, which made one wonder, “Since she dies so easily anyway, why not just use a burst-damage marksman who can take down enemies instantly?”

So, with Guardian Spirit, a powerful burst-damage marksman in this patch, not on the ban list, and Netherworld Shadow, another burst-damage marksman that even had mobility, also not banned, Ling Suyu’s pick of Dark Raven gave off the air of a true pro who’d “use leaves to hit people even when stones are available”—which, in XY’s view, came across as condescension.

After both commentators paused for a moment, they explained Ling Suyu’s choice as follows.

Remi: “You can tell Feather really loves sustained-damage marksmen!”

Liang Fan: “Sustained-damage marksmen are played differently from burst-damage marksmen. Feather used Candy Witch in the last game—maybe he didn’t want to disrupt his rhythm?”

However, the audience had a different take.

At that very moment, the livestream’s bullet comments and the comments below displayed:

【Feather has clearly played Guardian Spirit before—has he just not practiced it this season?】

【So does that mean he’s practiced Dark Raven?】

【I suddenly recalled casual player who’s been maining Dark Raven this season…】

【What a coincidence, I was thinking the same thing.】

【What are you guys talking about?】

【I suggest searching the forum using the keywords: “pro players’ alt accounts.”】

As a result, before the second match between Fight and XY had even begun, a post titled “Which two pro players’ alt accounts are these?” on the official Glorious forum quickly shot to the top of the homepage.

Fight was completely unaware of this, with all their attention focused on XY’s final two picks.

They still had to pick a top laner and a mid laner. Given their usual preference for high HP and high defense, as well as the unusually squishy composition they’d picked so far—which was a departure from their norm—their choices would likely be…

“Imperial Knight and Deepsea Faerie!” Commentator Remi announced XY’s picks.

Fight wasn’t surprised at all.

Like Skeletal Empress and Black-Armoured Scythe, Imperial Knight was a tanky top laner with high health and defense, but he lacked crowd control and leaned more toward offense than defense.

Well, it wasn’t as if he had absolutely no crowd control—his second ability, “Barbed Hook,” let him use his weapon to pull enemies in front of him towards himself. Although opponents could still use their abilities and talents during this process, meaning they weren’t truly CC’d, if they ate “Barbed Hook” without any movement skills or dashes, the effect was practically the same as being CC’d.

Deepsea Faerie as a mid-laner needed no introduction—she had both crowd control and healing abilities, strong survivability, and was extremely durable, making her the top pick for XY’s mid-laner.

Generally, when facing Deepsea Faerie, players would choose to counter her with Vampire Progenitor.

Other mid-lane heroes would get caught in close-range skirmishes with Deepsea Faerie, get CC’d, and be worn down until they die. Only Vampire Progenitor who, like Deepsea Faerie, had a healing ability but dealt higher damage without CC, could outplay her in close combat, forcing her to back off and retreat under her tower.

But this time, Tang Yu’an glanced at Fight’s lineup and smiled with satisfaction: “We’ve got enough tankiness—let’s go for high damage.”

“Alright!” Dou Jiayan happily locked in Phoenix Soul.

They weren’t sure how XY felt after they snatched three consecutive picks from them, but in any case, they were now highly motivated and quite satisfied with their roster—they didn’t seem intimidated in the slightest.

Top lane hero Black-Armoured Scythe was one of Mo Xiaoyun’s preferred picks, and was also one of the champions he played most often and excelled at.

Mid lane hero Phoenix Soul could be considered Dou Jiayan’s signature champion. Although it wasn’t the champion he played best, he simply loved it. When someone loves a champion enough, they’re less likely to make foolish plays with it, ensuring consistent performance.

The marksman hero, Dark Raven, was the champion Ling Suyu has practiced the most this season. He was currently so fond of her that she’d even surpassed Desert Hunter in terms of favour—after all, with teammates providing protection and no need to worry about escaping, her damage output was higher than Desert Hunter’s.

The support hero, Skeletal Empress, was Tang Yu’an’s signature pick. In his hands, even when built as a support, she applied no less pressure to the enemy than Mo Xiaoyun’s Skeletal Empress.

Finally, the jungler, Blade Soul.

“This is probably the first time Yumao has seen Yu He play Blade Soul, right?” Tang Yu’an asked uncertainly.

Ling Suyu gave a soft “Mm-hmm.”

That was indeed the case.

It was unclear which heroes Yu He preferred when solo queuing, but when they played as a five-man squad, given his pick order—which usually prevented him from getting Twin Demonblades or Tempest Blade—he tended to pick Double Phantom, or at the very least, Venomous Scorpion.

“Well, you’re in for a treat.” Tang Yu’an said, “When Yu He plays Blade Soul, he doesn’t aim to win the match; he aims to carry the team.”

“Huh?” Ling Suyu was taken aback. “Is Yu He’s Blade Soul that strong? Then why didn’t he use it in previous matches?”

Before Tang Yu’an could speak, Yu He himself replied, “It’s not that strong—it’s just a bit egotistical.”

“Poison[^1]?” Ling Suyu was even more confused. “Blade Soul doesn’t have poison.”

Yu He: “……”

For a moment, Yu He suspected Ling Suyu was doing this on purpose, but he had no proof.

Tang Yu’an chuckled and explained, “It’s the ‘lone’ in ‘lone wolf.’ Yu He’s Blade Soul has a mind of its own.”

Ling Suyu: ??

Although he knew that every member of Fight possessed extraordinary skills—Mo Xiaoyun could take on three opponents a level higher than him, storm enemy towers at will, and absorb damage effortlessly, never giving a second thought to enemy towers or the damage output of the enemy’s dual carries—

Dou Jiayan had the abilities to either drag down four teammates when he’s off his game, or  carry four when he was feeling it, being the one most likely to die, yet also the one who dealt the most damage.

Yu He was a poser from start to finish: in-game, he was a solitary, aloof solo laner; out of the game, he frequently spouted pretentious remarks that left his teammates speechless and opponents in tears.

But no one could have imagined he’d evolve to the point where the heroes under his control would develop a sense of self-awareness… Like hell!

Fortunately, Ling Suyu was an atheist with a lively mind; he could step back from the situation and snap back in just as quickly. After making a 180-degree mental turn, he managed—half through understanding, half through guesswork—to grasp Tang Yu’an’s meaning: “He doesn’t completely follow commands?”

Tang Yu’an pondered for a moment: “I guess you could see it that way.”

Ling Suyu got it: “So it’s like me—no matter what I use, they[^2] all have their own minds.”

Yu He: “……”

Tang Yu’an: “……”

That day, Tang Yu’an began to wonder for the first time if he’d been spoiling Ling Suyu a bit too much.

But… it also didn’t seem to be that big of a deal.

Although Ling Suyu certainly marched to the beat of his own drum, it was mostly within Tang Yu’an’s expectations—something he could keep up with and direct the rest of the team to follow.

He’d been fully prepared for this ever since he decided to recruit Ling Suyu.

Conclusion: It was fine to keep spoiling him.

Still, he decided to set the record straight to avoid any misunderstandings on Ling Suyu’s part: “Yu He will still follow orders, but his Blade Soul is more geared toward sustained damage rather than dishing out a wave of damage before dying. So, in this match, he won’t be taking damage for any teammates—that’s what I meant.”

“So that’s it—I get it now.” Ling Suyu thought about how he’d insisted on playing as a marksman in solo queue, and it suddenly clicked.

It must’ve resulted from Yu He’s solo-queueing. Twin Demonblades and Tempest Blade were constantly on the ban list and wouldn’t come off. He probably tried playing as an assassin like Double Phantom in the jungle, but found his teammates unreliable and couldn’t carry the game, so in a fit of rage, he locked in Blade Soul.

Since his teammates were unreliable, he wouldn’t count on them anymore.

Since he wasn’t counting on his teammates, there was no way he’d ever take a hit for them.

His assassin could finish off low-health enemies his teammates couldn’t, take down towers his teammates couldn’t, and even die for his teammates, but his Blade Soul would secure victory no matter what, even if it meant stepping over his teammates’ corpses!

Ling Suyu had originally thought that Yu He didn’t use Blade Soul because he didn’t like or wasn’t good at playing warriors, but it turned out he had been holding back his edge for the team’s sake—transforming from an attacker into a protector?

Even though he looked like such a cold-hearted young man, deep down, he was just as gentle as the rest of Fight.

Ling Suyu smiled: “It’s fine. Go ahead and carry the team—you won’t steal my spotlight.”

Tang Yu’an raised an eyebrow, and Yu He did the same.

After a moment, Yu He snorted: “I can’t just pretend I didn’t hear that. Let’s settle this once and for all.”

Dou Jiayan, looking completely baffled: “ “He-He, who are you going to settle what with? Yumao? Why?”

Yu He: “I suddenly miss the feeling of getting MVP.”

Dou Jiayan: “I want it too! This round’s MVP is definitely mine!”

Yu He: “Then you’re my opponent too.”

Dou Jiayan: “Let’s settle this once and for all!”

Tang Yu’an couldn’t stop laughing as he listened to the chuunibyou-esque remarks from these two youths, neither of whom were even 20 yet.

Ling Suyu had already tuned out their words and was silently waiting for the match to begin.

He always let his skills do the talking.

Only Mo Xiaoyun frowned, looking worried: “Don’t do anything reckless. This is a competitive match—as long as we win, it doesn’t matter who gets the MVP.”

“It’s fine; they know their limits,” Tang Yu’an replied, adding teasingly, “Who knows, maybe I’ll be the MVP~”

And so, amid the intense rivalry and trash talk between Fight, the second match between Fight and XY began.

“It’s so surprising that XY went with Cat Spirit and Spider Queen!” said Remi. “I knew they had a deep hero pool, but I never expected they’d practiced this combo!”

“I think they were inspired by Fight using this duo to defeat igame in the group stage,” Liang Fan speculated. “SMG also used this duo in today’s match, which might give people the false impression that the duo has been buffed, but it hasn’t. So, ultimately, it comes down to Fight’s influence. The performance of the quarterfinalist teams in the group stages was bound to attract attention. I suspect that after the group stages, more teams than just SMG and XY have been practicing this hero duo.”

Both commentators assumed that XY had been practicing Cat Spirit and Spider Queen, and in fact, they had.

However, contrary to their belief that this duo was particularly strong, their primary motivation was to counter Ling Suyu.

The reasoning behind the decision to practice this lineup went as follows: “Feather’s Cat Spirit is very agile. And not just Cat Spirit—any marksman with mobility in his hands is as slippery as an eel, making them very hard to counter, so if we can ban them, we should.”

Q: “What if we ban Cat Spirit and he picks Desert Hunter instead?”

A: “Then we’ll ban Desert Hunter too.”

Q: “What if we ban Desert Hunter and he picks Candy Witch instead?”

A: “Then we’ll ban Candy Witch too.”

Q: “So are we just going to let the bug-like mid laner Phoenix Soul and the devilish Twin Demonblades run wild?”

A: “Let Cat Spirit out. Marksman, practice Cat Spirit—if you face Fight, snatch the pick whenever you can.”

Marksman: “Huh?”

Although Candy Witch was a mage, her playstyle leaned more toward that of a marksman. Many mid-laners struggled to adapt to her, the cost of mastering her was too high, and the risks in the late game were significant. It was better to focus on mastering heroes that had been strong in recent patches.

Desert Hunter was too hard to get going—even “Glorious’s Number One Desert Hunter” Ling Suyu lost to igame while playing him, so there was absolutely no point in practicing him.

After much deliberation, the only champion they could master to steal kills from Fight was Cat Spirit.

However, this was a last resort—it was no different from “inflicting a thousand wounds on the enemy while losing eight hundred of your own.”

Unlike SMG, who loved trying new things, XY would rather not use this lineup if possible.

But…

“We can’t counter Candy Witch—we have to ban her. We can’t counter Twin Demonblades—we have to ban him. Although we don’t know if Fight will pick Beast Master, if they do, we’re done for. We can’t take that risk. In that case, let’s go with Cat Spirit.”

Of course, XY didn’t pick Cat Spirit simply because they were confident they could win the game with it; their reasoning was—

“If we pick Cat Spirit, Feather will definitely pick Desert Hunter. Desert Hunter is unstoppable in the late game, but deals almost no damage early on. As long as we don’t let them gain an economic advantage, we’ll have a chance!”

Yes, XY picked Cat Spirit based on the assumption that Ling Suyu would pick Desert Hunter. By the time they selected Cat Spirit, they’d already figured out how to counter Desert Hunter.

igame really set a great example: they suppressed Fight early on to secure an economic advantage, then kept the pressure on until they won the match, never giving Desert Hunter a chance to get going.

If everyone had previously been terrified of Ling Suyu’s Desert Hunter, now, with igame setting an example, they felt a little more confident when facing his Desert Hunter.

But no one could have imagined that a sudden twist would occur—Fight picked Dark Raven!

“Dark Raven?!”

“Damn! Another sustained-damage marksman!”

That wasn’t the point, though. The real issue was—

“If they’re running Dark Raven, we won’t be able to pressure them early on, right?”

“Not necessarily. We’ve got Spider Queen.”

“But their support’s insane and picked a top laner?”

“Uh…” A moment of silence.

In the end, just before the match officially began, XY’s coach only had time to offer a perfunctory word of encouragement: “Bot lane, you’ve got this! Push them back!”

To XY’s bot lane players, this sounded no different from “Good luck on your way to the grave.”

《Glorious Heroes》 was a very fair game.

The lower a hero’s damage was in the early game, the higher it was in the late game—and vice versa.

Examples of the former included Desert Hunter; examples of the latter included Candy Witch.

The weaker a hero’s survivability, the higher their damage—and vice versa.

Examples of the former included Guardian Spirit; examples of the latter included Netherworld Shadow.

Survival ability depended on skills: the more dashes, the more shields, and the more passive health regeneration, the stronger the survival ability.

In other words, heroes with dashes and shields would inevitably have their damage nerfed.

Cat Spirit had one movement ability, while Desert Hunter had two. Although both relied on basic attacks for damage, Cat Spirit’s early-game basic attack damage was higher than Desert Hunter’s, making them well-balanced.

As for Dark Raven, she had no movement abilities before her ultimate, and her ultimate was geared more toward survival than damage, so her early-game damage was higher than Cat Spirit’s, making her well-balanced.

Cat Spirit’s first skill, “Spiritual Mark,” marked an enemy, and each subsequent attack against that enemy dealt additional damage.

Dark Raven’s first skill, “Raven Feathers,” buffed herself, causing each basic attack to deal an extra hit.

Both were buff-type skills; although the buff mechanics differed, they were essentially on par.

However, Cat Spirit’s second skill, “Shadow Steps,” allows her to quickly dash a short distance while phasing through obstacles.

Dark Raven’s second skill, “Poison Feather,” caused feathers fired for a period after activation to be poisoned, providing armor penetration and damage over time.

With just this second skill alone, Cat Spirit’s damage output was already outclassed.

Now let’s look at the third skills.

Cat Spirit’s third skill was a passive ability called “Shadow Strike,” which instantly dealt a certain amount of damage when three basic attacks hit the same target.

Dark Raven’s third skill was an active ability called “Feather Retraction.” Each basic attack had a certain chance to drop a black feather; when activated, all dropped feathers returned to Dark Raven and instantly dealt damage to enemy targets they passed through.

Cat Spirit’s third skill boosted her basic attack damage, while Dark Raven’s third skill dealt damage based on probability and the player’s skill.

If you were incredibly lucky, Dark Raven could use “Feather Retraction” before Cat Spirit landed all three basic attacks, and the black feathers scattered across the ground could take Cat Spirit down.

And even if you weren’t a top-tier player, Dark Raven wouldn’t be at a disadvantage in a 1v1 against Cat Spirit using just her first and second abilities, even without this skill.

So, the moment XY saw Dark Raven, they knew there was no way Cat Spirit could hold down Fight’s bottom lane. But what could they do? The team composition had already been locked in—they had to play!

If they couldn’t apply pressure, they’d just play it safe and farm until their ultimates were ready. While Cat Spirit’s ultimate dealt heavy single-target damage, Dark Raven’s was an AoE ability that was more of a survival tool. When these two marksmen traded blows with their ultimates active, it was really hard to say who had the upper hand.

However, they never expected that Fight wouldn’t let them farm in peace!

As soon as Ling Suyu hit the lane, he charged straight for XY’s bottom lane first tower.

XY’s bottom lane: ?

What are you doing? Don’t come over here!

The two minion waves converged at Dark Raven’s feet, forcing XY’s bottom laner back to the tower. XY’s marksman put his first skill point into his passive, landing his first auto-attack on an enemy minion.

As for Ling Suyu, he spent his first skill point on his first ability, “Raven Feathers,” then decisively locked onto the enemy Cat Spirit, firing a barrage of black feathers—whoosh!

Dark Raven was a bird spirit with the beautiful, youthful face of a human girl. Beneath her elaborate gown woven from black feathers, “hands” resembling black wings were revealed. As she flapped her wings, the black feathers immediately took on a life of their own, sweeping toward their target in an eerie yet graceful arc.

Under the enhanced effect of her first skill, “Raven Feathers,” every time a feather was fired, another immediately appeared and followed the first along a different trajectory.

In an instant, Cat Spirit’s health bar seemed to have been pierced, and health points began spurting out.

Oh no!

XY’s marksman realized the target was him, not a minion, and decisively retreated under the tower. The support cast a shield at just the right moment to protect him, but more than half of his health bar still mysteriously vanished.

The two retreated into the tower, thinking they were safe, but Fight exploded into action. Tang Yu’an’s Skeletal Empress used a Flash followed by her first ability, vanishing from her spot before reappearing above the pair, then swinging her massive bone hammer down with a thunderous crash!

The two were sent flying into the air, completely incapacitated.

Almost simultaneously, Ling Suyu’s Dark Raven followed up with another Flash, continuing to flap its elegant black wings—whoosh, whoosh, whoosh!

【System】 Dark Raven, first blood!

【System】 The enemy Cat Spirit has been slain!

The two had retreated too far back, and Tang Yu’an had pushed too deep—there was no way for him to escape alive.

Well then, the support might as well go down with them.

Ling Suyu locked onto Spider Queen—whoosh, whoosh, whoosh!

【System】 Dark Raven, double kill! Peerless!

【System】The enemy Spider Queen has been slain!

Spider Queen’s health and defense relied entirely on gear; her base stats were very low, so killing her was even easier for Ling Suyu than taking down Cat Spirit.

After securing the double kill, Ling Suyu turned around coolly and left the tower.

As she died, the enemy support Spider Queen cast a Web of Constraint that ensnared Tang Yu’an’s Skeletal Empress. Although Tang Yu’an blocked it with his shield, as long as he attacked, he could still claim the kill.

And so—

【System】 Spider Queen, one kill!

【System】 Ally Skeletal Empress has been slain!

Fight started the game with a 1-for-2 trade: their support died, and the enemy support secured the kill. Although it was a case of “hurting the enemy a thousand while losing eight hundred ourselves,” they still came out ahead.

“Holy crap! That was brutal! Fight’s playing super aggressively this game!” Remi was stunned by Fight’s “I’ll fight my way through even if it kills me” attitude right from the start and couldn’t help but wonder, “Is Feather angry because his Cat Spirit was stolen?”

Even Liang Fan, who’s usually level-headed, said, “When Feather plays sustained-damage marksmen, with the exception of Candy Witch, he usually plays it safe early on. Why is he being so aggressive this game? It looks like XY’s strategy of trying to steal Cat Spirit to pressure the bot lane has backfired.”

But the truth was—

Ling Suyu: “The enemy isn’t a burst-damage type, so we can play a bit more aggressively early on.”

Tang Yu’an: “I was thinking the same thing. Why don’t we get a kill right at the start?”

Ling Suyu: “Sure.”

Tang Yu’an: “Are you serious? I’ll only go in if you can kill.”

Ling Suyu: “If you dare to go in, I can kill.” He paused for a moment. “Don’t worry—no one knows Cat Spirit better than I do.”

Tang Yu’an chuckled: “Alright, with you saying that, I’m reassured. Even if I die, it’s not a loss~”

And just like that, the two of them charged in, and Ling Suyu actually secured a kill—a double kill, no less!

However, this wasn’t their original plan…

Tang Yu’an: “Ugh, the enemy retreated too far back, but we got a double kill, so we’re not at a loss.”

Ling Suyu: “Be more careful next time. If they’re too deep in their base, don’t go in—otherwise you won’t get any experience.”

Tang Yu’an: “It’s fine, once I respawn, the tower can’t kill me.” He paused. “Actually, I did it on purpose to give you a chance to take the kill all by yourself. So, were you happy?”

Ling Suyu: “……”

Other teammates: “……”

Ling Suyu: “Uh-huh, thanks.” As he said this, he suddenly recalled Yu He’s catchphrase: “Thanks for the hospitality.”

Tang Yu’an: “……”

Yu He: “……”

The commentators couldn’t hear the real-time voice chat on Fight’s channel, so naturally they had no idea that the team wasn’t fighting so fiercely because they were angry—they were forced to be aggressive due to an unexpected turn of events. However, this made for maximum dramatic effect, and the live comments were delighted:

【Hahaha, the last time Feather was this angry was when he was fighting Ma Mingxi!】

【Feather looks so cool when he’s angry! Feather’s Dark Raven is so cool!】

【Tang Yu’an is a badass too—he sacrificed himself just so Feather could get revenge.】

【Tang Yu’an’s Spider Queen got stolen too! Maybe they got caught up in the heat of the moment!】

【XY must be regretting stealing Fight’s heroes right about now.】

【XY even stole our He-bao’s[^3] Tempest Blade! Can’t wait to see He-bao get revenge with Blade Soul!】
