Every Monday, Ren Jinhuan woke up an hour early, and today was no exception. First, he called a café near the office to reserve a table for two for lunch, requesting a quiet, dimly lit spot. He then ordered a light meal for two and asked the delivery person to bring it to the Aladdin headquarters building by 11:45 a.m. While eating breakfast, he replied to a few business messages, helped Wen Yan prepare the materials for Monday’s report, and drafted a weekly to-do list to send to the team chat. Finally, he picked out a white shirt with fine gray stripes and ironed it until it was neat and crisp.

He was a rather meticulous person. His approach to dressing mirrored his approach to life. He preferred a neat and orderly aesthetic, with balance and harmony always serving as his ideal. He made a conscious effort to avoid a lifestyle marked by eccentricity or impulsiveness. Of course, this didn’t mean he disliked such people; on the contrary, he actually admired them to some extent. Shi Lu, for instance, was precisely that type of person. But everyone’s comfort zone was different. Ren Jinhuan’s ideal life was predictable and plannable; if any random sparks were to disrupt his routine, he would manage to cope, but he’d find it bothersome and exhausting.

Shi Lu once remarked that he lacked genuine passion, refusing to embrace the spontaneity and disarray of life, and always placed the importance of maintaining a respectable public image above all else. Ren Jinhuan had responded to her at the time: “If you wear yourself outwards to the point of looking disheveled and reveal that you’re fighting tooth and nail, isn’t that just putting yourself in a position where others can easily take advantage of you?” His innate aversion to conflict had allowed peacefulness to become second nature to him, much like the fourth debater position he most often chose on the debate stage. Gauging the judges’ preferences, aligning with the broader perspective, delivering closing arguments, and elevating the discussion’s significance—this was the role he excelled at and found reassuring.



\-

When Ren Jinhuan stepped out of his apartment, he noticed that the tenant across the hall on the same floor was having the place cleaned; the real estate agent was taking photos, and all the furniture had been cleared out. It seemed the tenants had moved out long ago. He happened to know this agent, who explained that the tenants had given notice two weeks earlier and were now eager to list the apartment.

Ren Jinhuan remembered that a couple had lived across the hall. Although they’d been there for two years, he rarely saw them and had only occasionally spoken to them when they’d mistakenly picked up the wrong package. The agent said, “There’s nothing we can do. Rents have gone up again this year, and aside from people in the tech industry, very few from other sectors rent in this complex.”

That was certainly true. In recent years, many IT companies had relocated to this area and, in an effort to attract talent, had rolled out housing subsidies and other incentives. Local real estate agents had shrewdly followed suit by raising rents, causing prices to skyrocket overnight. Even so, the rental market remained in high demand, and Ren Jinhuan figured he’d have a new neighbor before long.

Upon arriving at the office, Ren Jinhuan took the elevator from the parking garage straight to the 7th floor of Tower A to join Wen Yan for the weekly Monday Aladdin management morning meeting. After the 90-minute meeting, Wen Yan walked with him through Towers B and C, discussing upcoming strategic plans along the way and asking him to prepare a proposal evaluation in advance. The two parted ways at the skybridge corridor. Ren Jinhuan then took the service corridor to the 2nd floor of Tower H, where he held a one-hour weekly meeting with his team to review the progress and challenges of each project. Finally, he left the main office building and headed to the conference room in the company’s in-house library for his biweekly one-on-one with HR.

Almost every Monday followed this pattern: meetings always filled the first workday. On his walk from Tower A to H, Ren Jinhuan would encounter an average of 30 familiar colleagues, two-thirds of whom he had shared a meal with, and ten of whom were fellow managers. Of those ten, he considered five worth cultivating deeper relationships with, but only two were of significant value and required long-term relationship maintenance.

At 11:45 a.m., Ren Jinhuan picked up the lunch he’d ordered that morning right on time and happened to run into Shi Lu, who was wearing workout clothes. Unless she had a deadline, Shi Lu always went to the company gym for aerobics on Monday afternoons. The two walked together for a while, and on a path through the botanical garden, Shi Lu said cheerfully, “The 11th spot on the company’s ‘Hottest Male Executives’ list is about to change hands.”

On the “Slacking-Off” section of Aladdin’s internal forum, there was a long-running popular thread where employees voted to rank the male executives by looks. However, the top 10 spots on the list were fixed and, in a show of good judgment, were reserved for bigwigs like Director Lin and CEO Fang. Thus, the actual ranking began at number 11.

Although Ren Jinhuan had guessed the answer, he still played along and asked who it was. Shi Lu didn’t answer directly, but said, “This morning, HR held a welcome party. I tagged along with my boss and met the new head of Overseas Production & Logistics.”

Ren Jinhuan smiled. “Then tell me about the intel you got.” So Shi Lu began recounting Jin Xiangtang’s background and history. Although Ren Jinhuan already knew all this, she added one extra detail: “I also caught a whiff of FM’s Extraordinary Vetiver on him.”

Ren Jinhuan slowed his pace, looking puzzled. “Are you sure?”

“Of course. If you don’t believe me, you can go smell it yourself.”

Ren Jinhuan remained silent. He had smelled the perfume Shi Lu mentioned before and could confirm it wasn’t the scent he’d noticed when he first met Jin Xiangtang that night. He guessed that Jin Xiangtang might have switched perfumes due to the professional setting. After a moment of silence, Ren Jinhuan asked if there was any other news. Shi Lu replied lazily, “I also found out Jin Xiangtang’s zodiac sign and MBTI type. If you accompany me to the movies on Wednesday night, I’ll tell you.”

Ren Jinhuan said with a sigh, “I have plans that night, but I don’t really want to know any of that anyway. I’ll make my own judgment based on my own logic.”

Shi Lu felt her fun-seeking attempt had fallen flat and accused him of being too much of a realist. Ren Jinhuan replied, “When I have to make a decision, I rely on a comprehensive comparison of the big picture and my own discernment to arrive at a one-in-a-million answer, rather than basing it on generic results like beauty rankings, zodiac signs, or personality tests.”

“And there’s your flaw. You only value pretty official endorsements and worldly recognition, while looking down on the value and experience behind ordinary things.” Shi Lu refused to back down, “Take these ‘best-looking’ lists, for example, which you think are just for entertainment. They’re actually quite valuable as a reference for some single HR and administrative staff. You’ll understand once you’re qualified to join the ranks of senior management.” Her tone was sharp and full of innuendo, which for a moment made Ren Jinhuan feel as if he were back on the debate stage of years past. He didn’t press the argument further but ended the conversation amicably, saying he’d consider her suggestion.

Shi Lu glanced at her phone and saw it was almost noon, so she didn’t linger—after her workout, she still had to meet a blind date.

Ren Jinhuan was somewhat surprised that she was going on a blind date. She raised an eyebrow and said, “What else am I supposed to do? Am I supposed to look for a straight guy at the gym?”

“That’s not what I meant. I thought you disliked blind dates.”

“Approaching the mundane is a lifelong pursuit for any genius,” Shi Lu said with gusto. “Although you won that debate earlier, when it comes to finding a partner, I definitely have you beat.”

Under the blazing sun, the plants in the garden huddled close together, hoping to escape the last wave of heat. Ren Jinhuan stood beneath a camphor tree, scattered patches of light from the green canopy falling on him like an oil painting that had been defaced with tape, its colors faded in several places. Faced with Shi Lu’s challenge, he felt a rare surge of competitiveness. Tilting his head slightly as he pondered, he replied calmly, “That’s not necessarily true.”
