“Can you really not help me, Senior?”

When Ren Jinhuan uttered these words, the final syllable trailed off softly. Earlier, he and Jin Xiangtang had played a game of wordplay in English—neither had given an inch, neither had gained the upper hand, and it had been quite an effort. True, he was currently in a position where he needed to ask for help, but that didn’t mean he had to accept taking a passive role. This was the marvel of the Chinese linguistic codes: softness could overcome hardness, and retreat could be a form of advance. By reminiscing about old ties and recounting shared history, making him lose face would be your cruelty. After all, who didn’t love that soft, melodious Wu dialect? No matter how much Western education he’d absorbed, he’d still have to yield to these five thousand years of gentle breezes and soft rain.

So who exactly had the upper hand here? Ren Jinhuan calculated his advantages.

And what of Jin Xiangtang? His gaze was heavy as he shifted his eyes, inch by inch, away from him and towards the pomegranate tree nearby. Among the fruits, one had ripened ahead of the others, its half-side glowing with the red of the morning sun. It hung precariously from the branch, just like this “junior classmate he met along the way”—sharp-witted yet reserved, a master at playing the goody-two-shoes, yet far from docile. As Jin Xiangtang thought of this, he actually felt a touch of idle amusement. Should he help or not? He half-closed his eyes, smiled suddenly, and turned to leave amid the scent of damp, green grass.

Though silent, it spoke volumes. This was Jin Xiangtang’s message.

Four hours later, at 6:00 p.m., the September evening was, as usual, windless and rainless, with a clear, crisp sky. Ren Jinhuan’s corporate messenger emitted a “ding.” Jin Xiangtang sent his first message: “I’m planning to rent a place nearby and would like your advice. Are you free to join me tomorrow at noon?”

A bright smile shone in Ren Jinhuan’s eyes as he replied, “Sure.”

At 12:30pm the next day, Ren Jinhuan walked around to the back of the building and met Jin Xiangtang in a quiet alley. The other was leaning against a black Panamera, head down as he typed a message, his medium-gray suit draped over his arm. Dappled shadows from the trees shimmered like ripples on water. As Ren Jinhuan approached, Jin relaxed and gestured for him to get in the car.

On the way, Jin Xiangtang asked if he’d eaten yet and suggested they stop to grab some sandwiches or something. Ren Jinhuan said he’d already eaten, so Jin Xiangtang replied, “Then I’ll treat you tonight,” subtly setting up a plan for after work.

Regarding renting an apartment, Jin Xiangtang explained that he lived on the east side of the city, twenty kilometers from the office. With severe morning and evening traffic, he wanted to find a place closer to work. He then asked Ren Jinhuan how long he’d been in Beijing.

“Just two years. To be honest, I’m not yet familiar with many places.”

“I’m not very familiar myself—I haven’t been back in six years. Aside from the smog, everything else has changed quite a bit,” he joked, making Ren Jinhuan chuckle. “Manager Ren, you’re from the south, right? Have you been able to adjust to life here?”

Ren Jinhuan replied, “Overall, it’s been fine. If I had to pick out one thing I haven’t adjusted to, it’s spring, when poplar fluff is floating everywhere—it feels a bit uncomfortable on my face.”

“I can’t do anything about that one, ” Jin Xiangtang said helplessly, then casually quoted two lines of poetry—one from Xu Wei’s “Peach-Leaves Ferry” and the other from Ouyang Xiu’s “Spring in Jade Pavilion”—“The green willows are soft and delicate, yet their charm has nowhere to be displayed. You’re too refined; even they are unable to please you.”

Ren Jinhuan’s eyes shone: “I wouldn’t dare accept that kind of ‘pleasing’—I’m afraid it would be too much trouble.”

Jin Xiangtang continued, “That’s true. Even though you don’t show it on the surface, you give me the impression that you’re someone who’s very hard to please.”

The two chatted casually all the way until the car pulled up in front of a residential complex. A young real estate agent approached with a smile and greeted them. Ren Jinhuan noticed the name on his name tag—it was the same company he’d used when he rented his own place.

The agent was clearly brimming with enthusiasm. From the moment he saw the car, he knew they were high-end clients and was practically brimming with the desire to bring over every high-priced, fully furnished rental in the area. He explained that the complex had been completed less than four years ago, was generally quite new, and was very suitable for young people to live in. He then led them to one of the units, a duplex with excellent use of space and a stylish design. Jin Xiangtang stopped after taking just a few steps in the living room. Ren Jinhuan first asked about the usable area of each room, then tactfully mentioned that they were looking for a more open layout; the ceilings were a bit low, and he was worried it might feel a bit oppressive to live in. So they moved on to the second unit, which was indeed much brighter. This time, Jin Xiangtang took a closer look. Ren Jinhuan heard people talking next door, so he tapped on the wall—it sounded solid, like wood.

The real estate agent hurriedly explained, “This place was previously used as a shared apartment, so a partition was added. It’s pretty common these days. As long as the tenants don’t cause trouble or receive any complaints, it’s perfectly safe.”

Ren Jinhuan glanced back at Jin Xiangtang and, understanding the implication, said, “We’d like to see a few more places.”

“Are you two living together?” The agent noticed that while Ren Jinhuan was doing most of the talking, the other person clearly held the decision-making power. Ren Jinhuan said no, while Jin Xiangtang added that he’d be living alone for the time being. The agent didn’t ask any further questions.

The three of them made their way through several nearby apartment complexes, viewing four or five units within an hour. Each had its pros and cons: some were on floors that were too high, others too low; those on moderate floors had slightly poor soundproofing, while those with good soundproofing lacked natural light. Midway through, they came across a decent one, but Ren Jinhuan felt the price was too high. Although it might not be a big deal for Jin Xiangtang, items with poor value for money were never his top choice. Jin Xiangtang casually asked, “Do you like it?” Ren Jinhuan was a bit taken aback and said, “As long as you think it’s good, that’s fine.”

Back on the streets, Ren Jinhuan went to a small convenience store to buy a few bottles of water, giving one to Jin Xiangtang and one to the real estate agent. As for the results of their apartment hunt, the two had originally planned to choose one from the properties they’d just viewed, but after making a phone call, the agent rushed over and said, “I just got in touch with a colleague, and he recommended another place that absolutely meets both of your requirements. It’s just a ten-minute drive away.”

In the real estate business, commissions varied by property. Ren Jinhuan guessed the agent was referring to a property that needed to be rented out quickly, which would mean a higher profit margin. Since he hadn’t seen Jin Xiangtang show any particular interest earlier, he suggested they go take a look—they still had time anyway. However, when Ren Jinhuan saw the navigation app on the phone the agent handed him, his eyes widened slightly, and an inexplicable sense of tension welled up inside him—it was actually the very same residential complex where he lived.

When a butterfly flapped its wings in the Amazon rainforest, it wouldn’t necessarily trigger a tornado in Texas two weeks later; countless events had to occur in between, all requiring precise alignments at random points. Ren Jinhuan didn’t believe that the couple across the hall moving out two weeks ago was the starting point of this butterfly effect, but as the familiar streets, the familiar security guard, and the familiar floor kept appearing—until the elevator finally stopped on the 10th floor where he returned every day—he had to admit that Murphy’s Law had caught up with him.

As the real estate agent entered the electronic code for Room 1002, Ren Jinhuan stood with his back to his own apartment, Room 1001, feeling a wave of heat wash over him. It was as if the door behind him were the jaws of a tiger; he had never before felt such a sense of danger simply by returning home. Upon entering, apart from the different location of the bedroom balcony, the layout was nearly identical to his own apartment. This scene, both strange and familiar, left him speechless, while the young real estate agent across from him was talking nonstop, explaining every detail to Jin Xiangtang.

“This complex has great greenery and is very safe. There’s even an elementary school on-site, and the residents are all quite well-mannered. Plus, the landlord has moved abroad and signed a multi-year contract with our company, so the lease term is long. What do you think?” the agent asked.

“It looks pretty good,” Jin Xiangtang replied briefly.

“If you’re satisfied, why not sign the lease today? After all, properties in this complex are in high demand—we get so many viewing requests every day that I’m worried we won’t be able to hold this one for you for very long.” Unsurprisingly, the agent began employing “scarcity marketing” tactics.

Jin Xiangtang didn’t give a definite answer. Instead, he returned to the living room, where he saw Ren Jinhuan sitting silently on the sofa, and asked, “Are you tired?”

Ren Jinhuan smiled faintly and said no. He had just searched the community chat group for complaint records from the past six months, but the results were just trivial matters—completely useless. Jin Xiangtang sat down beside him and asked, “What do you think of this place?”

“I actually think the fourth place we just saw wasn’t bad,” Ren Jinhuan said, knowing full well he was lying.

“Didn’t you say it wasn’t good value for the money? Besides, this one’s even a little cheaper,” Jin Xiangtang said, watching him backtrack.

“If you prioritize value for money above all else, you often end up missing out on a lot of intangible value,” Ren Jinhuan said earnestly, inadvertently refuting his own principle of choosing based on value for money.

Jin Xiangtang nodded in agreement but added thoughtfully, “That may be true, but compared to this one, it doesn’t seem to have any obvious advantages.”

“Actually, I’ve heard from many colleagues before that rents in this complex rise quickly and the property management fees are high, so in the long run, it’s not the best choice either.” One of the tricks of sophistry: if you can’t find merit in one option, exaggerate the flaws of the other.

The real estate agent standing nearby quickly objected, “We don’t just raise prices on a whim—tenants have to have stayed for at least a year. Besides, you two are in the IT industry, right? Even after deducting your housing allowance, it’s not that expensive. If you worked for big tech companies like Aladdin, Tuanduo, or Soso, you’d even get a corporate discount—20% off!”

Ren Jinhuan remained silent. Jin Xiangtang asked him, “Do you not like it?” The question was very direct. Ren Jinhuan realized that while the other person had originally intended for him to offer advice, he himself had had other plans, which made him feel a bit embarrassed. But more than the embarrassment, Ren Jinhuan found it harder to handle this unexpected turn of events, so he smiled understandingly and said, “It’s not that I don’t like it; I just think you deserve a better place to live.”

Jin Xiangtang stared at him for a moment, then finally seemed to soften. He told the agent, “We’ll think it over.”

Once they stepped outside the front door, Ren Jinhuan finally breathed a slight sigh of relief, as if he’d narrowly escaped a crisis. Yet today’s string of unexpected events seemed particularly fixated on him. Just then, a deliveryman rode up in the elevator and, upon seeing him, casually remarked, “Mr. Ren, have you come back for your noon break? Here’s your package.” Ren Jinhuan froze in his tracks. As the delivery person handed him the box, he felt as if he were holding a rabbit kicking wildly in his arms—as if it could sense his thoughts and was trying to break free.

The real estate agent asked in surprise, “Does Mr. Ren live here, too?”

The deliveryman nodded toward Room 1001: “Isn’t this the one? I’ve delivered packages here many times. Since you’re here, Mr. Ren, why don’t you just sign for it right now?” He pulled out a ballpoint pen, but Jin Xiangtang took it instead.

Jin Xiangtang gave the pen a few twirls, then tapped it lightly on the delivery slip in front of Ren Jinhuan. Watching him sign his name with an expression as calm as ever, Jin Xiangtang leaned in slightly and said, “How can you be so insincere when asking someone for a favor, Junior[^1]?”
