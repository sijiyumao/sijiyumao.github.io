Ren Jinhuan took that hand—a pair of long, clean, yet powerful hands that exuded the composure of a helmsman—and introduced himself by name.

Jin Xiangtang pondered for a moment and asked, “Revel to the fullest when life is at its best[^1]?” Then he laughed and added, “It’s a pun.”

Ren Jinhuan blinked, and as his gaze shifted, he caught on. A spark of amusement lit up the corners of his eyes. Although his name had no connection to that line from “Ode to Wine,” this literary-minded interpretation was quite amusing. So, returning the favor, he replied offhandedly, “I raise a golden goblet of wine to the Li Tang dynasty,[^2]” adding, “It’s an allusion.”

Upon hearing this, Jin Xiangtang fixed him with an intense gaze, while Vice President Yu, standing nearby, remarked rather earnestly, “Do you young people still exchange couplets when socializing these days?” Everyone burst into laughter. Jin Xiangtang invited Qin Heng to the empty seat next to Clair Zhang, then took the seat on the far end himself, directly across from Ren Jinhuan. He smiled wryly and said, “Exchanging couplets isn’t necessarily the norm, but people going head-to-head with one another is quite common.”

As he spoke, he turned his profile toward Ren Jinhuan, who didn’t look up but continued peeling a mandarin orange.

Once everyone had arrived, the restaurant owner walked in with a female manager. After exchanging a few pleasantries with Vice President Yu, he introduced the restaurant’s signature dishes to the group one by one and instructed the chefs to prepare them as soon as possible. The female manager brought out a new tea set, replaced the previous Baihao Yinzhen with it, and brewed a fresh pot of Zhengshan Xiaozhong. She was known as “Li-jie,[^3]” and had been working there for three years, possessing a gentle yet shrewd demeanor. Both were warm and sociable people who knew how to make small talk.

During the meal, everyone reminisced about their alma maters, and a sense of camaraderie immediately took hold. There were three major circles in professional networking, ranked by importance: alumni networks, former colleagues, and hometown networks. Vice President Yu and Teacher Qin Heng, both present, were graduates of University A, while Shen Feng and Wen Yan had graduated from University B. The two universities were geographically close, separated by just one street, so the group’s conversation flowed with great enthusiasm. Although Clair Zhang completed his bachelor’s, master’s, and doctoral degrees abroad, he graduated from CMU. CMU’s computer science program was highly sought after by major internet companies, and the firm had hired many recent graduates with this background in recent years. Given his standing within the company, Clair Zhang naturally hit it off with them as well. Of course, the one with the greatest advantage here was Jin Xiangtang. Having attended all three universities himself, and being the company’s future star, he was particularly at ease in such social settings.

Ren Jinhuan did not participate in this “alumni gathering.” In fact, his own alma mater was quite prestigious. He’d earned his bachelor’s and master’s degrees in econometrics from S University, which ranked fourth nationally overall and second in its discipline. However, since S University was located in the south, while the internet industry held a greater geographical advantage in the north, the vast majority of the company’s employees came from northern universities, and there were relatively few alumni from the south. Among the alumni he knew well, the one who had done the best was a senior from the Data Architecture Department, who was about to be promoted to the department’s second-in-command. As for his fellow townsman, that was Wen Yan—the two were from the same province and city. However, in a setting like this, Ren Jinhuan chose the safest approach: quietly listening from the sidelines.

Before long, Clair Zhang had exchanged contact information with Jin Xiangtang and later simply started calling him Eric. Seeing that Wen Yan and Clair Zhang’s tea was nearly gone, Ren Jinhuan refilled their cups to about seven-tenths full. Jin Xiangtang noticed the way he poured the tea from the lidded bowl into the sharing cup and asked, “Have you studied the art of tea?”

“Not much,” Ren Jinhuan explained. “My mother understands more. When I was a child, we often had guests at home, so I picked up a few things by watching her.”

The fair cup was still half full, and just as he was about to pour some more for Jin Xiangtang, the other man held out his left hand in a gesture that said, “It’s fine, I’ll do it myself.” There was still some distance between them across the long solid-wood table. As Ren Jinhuan reached across, the cuff of his lightly-coloured shirt rolled up slowly like a wave, revealing a sliver of pale, luminous wrist, like freshly solidified soy wax. Jin Xiangtang took the cup from his hand. As he caught a whiff of the cologne on his cuff, he gazed at him thoughtfully for a moment. After refilling his own cup, he naturally poured Ren Jinhuan’s cup to the brim.

Ren Jinhuan smiled and said thank you, but Jin Xiangtang simply replied, “It’s an extra service; have you not already paid?”

Vice President Yu and the others were engrossed in lively conversation and didn’t hear this exchange. Ren Jinhuan paused for a second, then deliberately adopted an expression of complete bewilderment as he looked at him. Jin Xiangtang turned his head with a smile, choosing not to call out his little bit of cleverness.

Li-jie and her staff served all the dishes one by one, and before long, the conversation at the table returned to its previous rhythm. Just then, as the group was discussing something, Qin Heng suddenly turned to Ren Jinhuan: “Hey, Xiao[^4]-Jin, I seem to recall that you also studied at CMU?”

Wen Yan, sitting next to him, exclaimed in surprise, “Really? How come you’ve never mentioned that before?”

“Yes, I’ve been there. It was an exchange program during my junior year, and I was only there for a year,” Ren Jinhuan replied. He had actually included this experience on his resume and mentioned it during the campus recruitment interview. At the time, both Qin Heng and Wen Yan had been interviewers for the group interview, but he hadn’t talked about it much after officially joining the company. Ren Jinhuan was a bit surprised—he hadn’t expected Teacher Qin Heng to still remember him.

Qin Heng, approaching fifty, had worked at McKinsey for many years and was a well-known veteran and leading figure in the consulting industry. He was humble and friendly, and patient and supportive toward his juniors. Every year, Aladdin conducted anonymous evaluations of its leaders, with ratings provided by their direct reports, assessing professional competence, managerial skills, and sense of responsibility, among other areas. Qin Heng’s scores consistently ranked within the top five.

Upon hearing Ren Jinhuan’s reply, Qin Heng pondered for a moment, then asked Jin Xiangtang, “Xiangtang, weren’t you at CMU back then too? Did you ever run into Xiao Jin?”

Jin Xiangtang held his teacup, his fingers tracing the blue-green surface. The chandelier above was reflected in the tea—a clear, orange-yellow glow, like the moon reflected in water. Tilting his head slightly, he replied, “No, I was working on a collaborative project at a lab at another university at the time.”

Upon hearing that Ren Jinhuan had attended CMU, Clair Zhang became interested and asked him what his major was and what courses he had taken. When Ren Jinhuan mentioned the course “Algorithmic Trading,” it happened to align with Clair Zhang’s field of expertise, so they chatted about related topics for a while.

“Though I didn’t do very well on the final paper for that course, so my overall grade was only an A-,” Ren Jinhuan said with a hint of regret.

Wen Yan offered words of consolation: “An A- isn’t bad at all—the standards for papers abroad are quite high.”

“I happen to know a little about the Algo-Trading course as well. ” Jin Xiangtang adjusted the watch on his left wrist, causing a gleam of silver to flash along the edge of the dial, a gesture that caught Ren Jinhuan’s attention. “The professor is Scott; he’s a great guy, but his assignments are mostly graded by his TA. Even for the final paper, the two of them discuss it together. That TA is known among students for being strict with grades, so Manager Ren, you shouldn’t let it bother you.” He spoke casually, then raised his eyes to look across the table, like a dealer at a gambling table waiting for his opponent to play a card.

But Ren Jinhuan had no intention of meekly “revealing his hand.” In front of so many senior executives from companies that valued alumni connections, shifting the blame for his failure onto others wasn’t a wise move. He weighed his words carefully, then finally lowered his head and said shyly, “In any case, I really should have devoted more time to that course. I was too impatient back then, trying to juggle my coursework and a competition at the same time.” Clair Zhang, having hit it off with him earlier, seized the opportunity to ask what kind of competition it was. Through their back-and-forth, he subtly helped boost his standing at the dinner.

Wen Yan, fearing that Ren Jinhuan might feel uncomfortable with too many questions at this kind of dinner gathering, thoughtfully set the stage for him: “Xiao Jin is among the top performers in my team when it comes to getting things done. If there’s any shortcoming, it might be that he lacks a bit of experience, and thus comes off as younger and a bit naive.” He cast a warm, affectionate glance at this subordinate who was so much to his liking, and the other returned a grateful look.

Then, almost casually, Ren Jinhuan’s gaze drifted toward Jin Xiangtang—a fleeting, ethereal glance that was both lively and attractive, yet shrewd and calculating.

Jin Xiangtang leaned back in his chair, calmly observing the pair of superior and subordinate. A faint smile tugged at the corners of his mouth. It seemed Wen Yan could no longer hold onto his people. A cunning rabbit is often more dangerous than a fox—the latter always inspires caution, while the former is universally loved.

As the meal drew to a close, Li-jie brought out fruit and desserts, including several portions of bowl-steamed cake. The restaurant owner mentioned that Li-jie had made them herself, adding a bit of tea water before steaming to enhance the flavor. Vice President Yu and the others tasted a few bites and agreed that there was indeed a fragrance of tea, though they couldn’t pinpoint exactly which type of tea it was—they could only roughly guess it was something like oolong tea. Li-jie exchanged a glance with the owner and laughed, “It’s not that easy to guess.” But the very next second, Jin Xiangtang spoke the answer: “Oriental Beauty tea from Taiwan.”

Clair Zhang couldn’t help but praise him: “I thought executives like you only dealt with digital technology. It seems I’ve underestimated you.”

Shen Feng joked, “In our line of work, we mostly sell intangible things, so we have to study the finer arts every now and then to better sell our concepts.”

“But I’m afraid I can’t hold a candle to Eric’s tea-tasting skills,” Clair Zhang teased, “How many years have you been drinking tea to reach this level?”

Hearing this, everyone burst into laughter, and the room was filled with a lively atmosphere. Jin Xiangtang was, for once, at a loss for words. Finally, with a helpless smile, he explained, “I have a pretty good memory. Any flavor I’ve tasted or person I’ve met—I only need to experience it once, and I’ll never forget it.”

It was a statement spoken with absolute certainty. Though uttered out of the blue, it suddenly struck Ren Jinhuan right in the heart. He couldn’t help but lift his head again and gaze intently at Jin Xiangtang. Above the ceiling hung a Chinese-style chandelier; its shade was adorned with Zen-inspired patterns, casting a divine glow over everything. A shimmering light glinted off Jin Xiangtang’s shoulders, cascading down his arms—first to his cufflinks, then to his wristwatch, and finally to his hands. The longer he looked, the more it seemed to take on a life of its own—as if he were facing a wordless scripture, devoid of inherent meaning, with everything resting solely in the observer’s heart. At the same time, the boundary-crossing alerts began to sound. Startled, Ren Jinhuan suddenly realized this had already exceeded his “dipping a toe in the water” principle, so he broke off his observation.

Just before the meeting ended, Vice President Yu, seizing the momentum, proposed a six-player Texas Hold’em game for next Wednesday evening, the day before the Mid-Autumn Festival holiday, at the same venue. The restaurant owner suggested that Li-jie could help deal the cards, as she had experience, but Clair Zhang mentioned he’d have to return to the U.S. next week to be with his family and wouldn’t be back until after the holiday.

“Then why not ask Old Liu from the Social Business Division?” Shen Feng suggested, but Vice President Yu replied, “Old Liu is such a terrible player, it’d be no fun to invite him.” He ran through a mental list of options, then suddenly thought of something and asked Ren Jinhuan, “Jinhuan, do you know how to play Texas Hold’em?”

Meanwhile, Teacher Qin Heng had also confirmed the time and said he could make it. Ren Jinhuan didn’t hesitate and said he knew a little.

“Then you’ll come too. That’ll make six of us,” Deputy General Manager Yu said simply and decisively, effectively finalizing the plan.

But the reality was that, of the seven people attending today’s dinner, only six knew how to play Texas Hold’em. Ren Jinhuan wasn’t one of them, and there were only three days left until their next meeting.

Around nine o’clock, the group began to leave one by one. Ren Jinhuan and Wen Yan had arrived together. Before leaving, Ren Jinhuan wanted to wash his hands and asked Wen Yan to wait in the car for a moment. However, as soon as he stepped into the restroom, he ran into the most troublesome person of the evening.

Jin Xiangtang was standing by the sink, having just taken off his frosted gold wristwatch. He spotted Ren Jinhuan in the mirror and casually asked, “Heading home alone?”

Ren Jinhuan walked past him, turned on the faucet next to him, and replied, “No, I’m leaving with Yan-ge.”

Jin Xiangtang smiled. “Manager Ren, you’re clearly the type who’s often looked after by others.”

“How can you tell just by looking?” Ren Jinhuan smiled at his reflection in the mirror. “I haven’t even noticed it myself.”

“If you put it that way, I’m afraid someone might get their feelings hurt—someone who’s poured their heart and soul into you for nothing.”

Ren Jinhuan slowly rubbed his hands together and said cheerfully, “I’m not part of the super-rich class, nor do I hold any high-ranking position. What do I have to offer that would make anyone go to such lengths?”

Jin Xiangtang took a slight step forward and pulled a dry paper towel from beneath the mirror. “And yet you sure know how to cast a spell on people…” He glanced at him. “You seem so young and innocent. Some may reckon that, should they treat you nicely, they may even have a chance.”

Ren Jinhuan shook his hands off and said softly, “I don’t quite understand.” Jin Xiangtang smiled but didn’t reply.

After drying his hands, Ren Jinhuan took out his phone and held it out to Jin Xiangtang. “That incident at the bar earlier was a misunderstanding. I wasn’t in the right frame of mind at the time, so I was a bit on guard. Could we get to know each other again? Want to be friends?” He kept his handsome, clear eyes open and softened his tone slightly—that signature demeanor of his that made it hard to refuse.

But today was different.

Jin Xiangtang glanced at the screen and, feigning a misunderstanding, said, “I know your name. We’ll get in touch through the company’s internal messaging system once you start tomorrow.” He didn’t give him the slightest opportunity to add his personal contact information.

Ren Jinhuan was taken aback for a moment. After thinking it over, he decided to back down for now: “Okay, whatever’s convenient for you. There are plenty of places to eat and drink around the office—I can help recommend a few. There are also some nicely designed tea houses, if you’re interested.”

Jin Xiangtang nodded and smiled. “Actually, I’m not as big a tea drinker as people think I am.”

“Then Director Jin is truly impressive. You seem to be quite knowledgeable about tea and can even rattle off all sorts of details,” Ren Jinhuan flattered him at just the right moment.

Jin Xiangtang looked at him with keen interest: “You’re pretty impressive too.” But he didn’t elaborate.[^5]

Just then, Ren Jinhuan’s phone rang—it was Wen Yan calling. Jin Xiangtang said knowingly, “It’s getting late. Let’s go; otherwise, your Yan-ge must be waiting quite anxiously.”

After Ren Jinhuan got into the car, Wen Yan asked him why he’d been gone so long, and he made up an excuse to brush it off. Once the car started moving, Wen Yan noticed him looking out the window. The night breeze had blown the strands of hair from his forehead slightly out of place, but he seemed in good spirits, so Wen Yan asked, “Are you that happy?”

He rested his hand on the window, propping up his cheek, and said softly, “It was rather lively with so many people present, so I’m happy.” As he spoke, his whole body leaned against the car door, his gaze fixed on the neon lights outside as if he’d forgotten the road they’d just traveled, giving the impression of a dandelion about to drift away.

Wen Yan’s heart skipped a beat. He felt the other was a little too far away, so he rolled up the window. Only after he was safely locked inside did he let out a sigh of relief. Then, unable to resist, he patted him on the head and said, “Stop sticking your head out there, you’ll catch a cold.”

In the dim interior, Ren Jinhuan closed his eyes, overcome by an indescribable sense of loss. He covered his eyes with his palms, blocking out all light, as if sinking into a deep sleep. Then, as he caught the scent of the base notes of perfume on his wrist, a sudden flutter of excitement shot through his heart.

Just moments ago, as they parted ways, Jin Xiangtang had leaned in and whispered, in a voice only the two of them could hear, “The perfume you’re wearing today suits you very well.”

He paused for a moment, then realized what he meant. Before leaving, considering that the person he was meeting wasn’t an acquaintance—and was, moreover, a middle-aged senior figure—he had chosen a safe, foolproof option: a perfume with a jade dragon tea scent.

But why would he say that?

Ren Jinhuan thought to himself, *I haven't done anything wrong, so why is this person criticizing me in such a roundabout manner?*
