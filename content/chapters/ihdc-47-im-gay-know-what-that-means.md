After the debriefing ended, Chen Jie went to the restroom.

Tang Yu’an watched his back as he left the room. Thinking about how he’d suddenly changed the way he addressed him, he hesitated for a moment before following him anyway.

When Chen Jie came out of the restroom, he saw Tang Yu’an standing at the sink, pretending to wash his hands. He couldn’t help but raise an eyebrow: “Is there something you want to say?”

Given that they were close and both skilled at reading people, they could often tell what the other was thinking with just a glance.

Sure enough, as soon as Tang Yu’an looked up, before he even had a chance to speak, Chen Jie already understood: “That was my first time calling you ‘captain.’ Feels a little strange, doesn’t it?”

“Yeah,” Tang Yu’an admitted as he dried his hands with a paper towel. “When we first met, Fight didn’t even exist yet. In your eyes, I was more like an ordinary gaming buddy than the captain of a competitive team, right?”

“Not that ordinary,” Chen Jie said, stepping closer to wash his hands. “You were a gaming buddy with great skills who could help me climb the ranks.”

Tang Yu’an couldn’t help but chuckle, then asked, “So what’s gotten into you all of a sudden? It really does feel strange being called ‘captain’ by you.”

“How should I put this…” Chen Jie finished washing his hands but didn’t leave the restroom right away; instead, he stood by the sink and continued, “Although I told Yumao that if he didn’t perform well in an official match, I’d take his spot back, seeing how he’s performed these past few days, I have to admit that the gap in skill between people really can’t be bridged by hard work alone. You really need some natural talent to play esports.” At this point, he let out a soft sigh, then confided a truth from the bottom of his heart to Tang Yu’an, “With how much he shines, how am I supposed to have the courage to come back?”

Tang Yu’an froze for a moment, words stuck in his throat.

He knew Chen Jie wasn’t saying all this to seek comfort, so he decided to listen on.

Sure enough, Chen Jie didn’t even wait for a response before continuing: “I’ve made up my mind. I’m retiring, becoming Fight’s coach, and I won’t be coming back.”

“You’re completely set on this?” Tang Yu’an asked, letting out a soft ‘Hmm—’, “But what does that have to do with you calling me ‘captain’?”

“It’s nothing much. It’s just that now that I’ve fully embraced my role as a coach, I feel I have to take responsibility for Fight. I can’t spoil you too much or treat you too much like a friend,”

Chen Jie said. “Though most of your decisions are pretty sound, and if you’d retired to become a coach, I definitely wouldn’t have had a role to play, you aren’t always right. Take using Mobile Artillery as support, for example. I thought it was a bit risky at the time, but because I trusted you so much, I didn’t stop you.”

Tang Yu’an: “But we won, didn’t we?”

Chen Jie tilted his head and stared at him.

“Alright, it was a bit risky,” Tang Yu’an said, nervously raising a hand to rub his nose. “Okay, I get what you mean. You’ve made up your mind to be a proper coach, so you want to change our relationship, right? That’s totally fine, just do whatever makes you comfortable. I was just curious about what you were thinking, so I asked. Now that we’ve cleared that up, there’s nothing to worry about.”

“Mm-hmm,” Chen Jie replied, patting Tang Yu’an on the shoulder.

Tang Yu’an was always like that—he’d carefully pick up on every subtle change in each member of Fight and offer them feedback, making everyone feel seen and cared for.

Chen Jie was a straight guy, and a bit of a chauvinist at that, but he had to admit that Tang Yu’an was very charming.

He was always there to look out for others and liven up the atmosphere.

He was especially reliable when it counted.

Although he often ran his mouth, and a lot of what he says is just to liven up the atmosphere and wasn’t true at all, he was actually very sincere with people.

When they first met, Chen Jie didn’t know that Tang Yu’an was gay.

He didn’t even know after they’d started living in the same club.

It wasn’t until one day when he forgot to bring his clothes into the shower and called out to Tang Yu’an to grab them for him. Instead of helping him, Tang Yu’an called out to Dou Jiayan upstairs.

Chen Jie couldn’t help but tease him: “Wow, what’s wrong with asking you to grab my clothes? Have you gotten this lazy already?”

At that moment, Tang Yu’an replied very seriously: “I can get them for you, I don’t mind.

But I don’t know if you’d mind—I’m gay. You know what that means? I like guys.”

Chen Jie was stunned to the spot.

For a split second, he thought Tang Yu’an was joking.

But then he thought about it and realized he really should have figured it out a long time ago.

He’d look away whenever Mo Xiaoyun lifted his shirt to show off his abs.

He’d laugh and dodge when Dou Jiayan lunged to hug him, then say, “Stop it, I don’t like being hugged. Go hug Ah-Jie instead.”

If he hadn’t said anything, no one would have noticed.

But he always quietly kept his distance on his own, and then, when he didn’t want to make his teammates feel uncomfortable, he forced himself to speak up and admit it.

Actually, before meeting Tang Yu’an, Chen Jie didn’t have a very good impression of gay people.

But Tang Yu’an made him change his mind about them, and he’d even started to feel a little… concerned for him?

Chen Jie always reminded Tang Yu’an to be mindful of his boundaries and not let romantic matters affect the game.

But in reality, Chen Jie actually hoped Tang Yu’an would find someone.

Although he didn’t have a partner himself, he figured it must be much harder for Tang Yu’an to find one than it was for him, right?

Despite Tang Yu’an being so handsome he could win the hearts of many girls just by walking down the street.

He couldn’t reciprocate any of their feelings.

Chen Jie sighed, turned the faucet back on after just having turned it off, and splashed cold water on his face.

Tang Yu’an looked at him in confusion: “What’s wrong now? Are you still not quite over it?

There’s more than just Yumao who’s a better marksman than you in the Glorious esports scene. It’s one thing to be jealous of other marksmen, but don’t get jealous of your own teammate. Yumao might seem aloof, but he’s actually very sensitive. He gets anxious easily, so you’ve got to pamper him properly…”

Before he could finish, Chen Jie’s fist slammed into his chest, followed by two words spoken in utter exasperation: “Shut up!”

You’re better off staying single until Fight wins the championship, dammit!

The 15-minute break ended quickly, and the second game between Fight and GQ began.

In this game, Fight went first with bans, and GQ went first with picks.

Fight’s three bans were Beast Master, Realm God, and Tempest Blade—not much different from the previous game.

GQ used their first two ban slots on Phoenix Soul and Twin Demonblades; their third ban slot went to Desert Hunter!

“GQ has banned Feather’s Desert Hunter!” commentator Remi exclaimed excitedly. “They’re the second team after OsO to ban Desert Hunter!”

“Banning a hero specifically to counter a single player is the greatest recognition of Feather’s skill!” Liang Fan added, picking up where Remi left off. “To think that not long ago, Feather was just a substitute for UH, but now he’s a starter for Fight—and even their main damage dealer—it really makes you reflect!”

Remi: “Actually, I’m kind of looking forward to seeing the current Fight team face off against UH. That’s bound to be quite the spectacle!”

Liang Fan: “Hahaha, that match-up would be interesting even in the pre-match segments!

But first, both teams need to advance to the quarterfinals!”

Remi: “Speaking of which, GQ banned Phoenix Soul but didn’t ban Array Sprite in this game.

They also banned the jungler Twin Demonblades—could this be a targeted move against Fight’s jungler?”

Liang Fan: “Twin Demonblades as a jungler is incredibly strong on his own. Since GQ had the first pick in this game, they actually had a chance to snatch him up. Since they banned him, it either means they didn’t plan to use him, or they had other ideas for their first pick.

But either way, it’s a foregone conclusion that they didn’t want to let Fight have that jungler, and Fight’s jungler is also very skilled!”

Remi: “Fight’s jungler, Crane, really likes to solo-push. He’s got that ‘lone wolf’ vibe.”

Liang Fan: “Yes, you can tell he’s obsessed with pushing towers. He’s definitely a jungler with a unique style!”

While the two were commentating, GQ selected their first hero just before the countdown ended—Mobile Artillery!

“Mobile Artillery!” Remi’s eyes lit up. “Do they think Feather will pick Candy Witch, so they’re counter-picking again?”

“Haha, Feather really is the center of their attention! ” Liang Fan wasn’t particularly surprised, but he was still worried for GQ. “Actually, Mobile Artillery doesn’t have much of an advantage as an AD carry this season. If the goal was just to force Fight not to pick Candy Witch, picking Artillery first is a bit risky. Don’t forget—even though Feather has been using Desert Hunter and Candy Witch all season, his go-to heroes in the White Fox Cup were Guardian Spirit and Netherworld Shadow.”

No sooner had he finished speaking than Fight’s team picked two heroes in a row—Skeletal Empress and Candy Witch!

“Huh?” Remi was taken aback. “Even after Mobile Artillery was picked, Fight still went with Candy Witch! Are they planning to win this game in the early game too?”

Immediately after, GQ picked Abyssal Lord as support and Deepsea Faerie as their mid laner.

“Looks like GQ is planning to play it safe and farm for the late game,” Liang Fan said.

Fight then picked Double Phantom as jungler and Vampire Progenitor as mid.

“If the mid laner is Vampire Progenitor, then Candy Witch is definitely the marksman this game! But even though Array Sprite was available, neither team picked it. Looks like we won’t need to ban this meta mid-lane pick next time?” Remi wondered uncertainly.

For their final two picks, GQ selected Blade Soul as the jungler and Black-Armoured Scythe as the top laner.

“Black-Armoured Scythe! They’re definitely planning to farm their way to the late game,”

Liang Fan said. “Looks like this match is going to be a long one!”

Remi: “Now only Fight’s support remains to be picked. Both of their carries are magic damage dealers. If GQ builds full magic resistance, their damage won’t be enough to carry the late game. I wonder what kind of support they’re planning to use.”

No sooner had the words left his mouth than Fight picked their final champion—Dark Angel!

Remi: “Huh?!”

Liang Fan: “Huh?!”

The moment the two commentators saw this champion, they both let out cries of disbelief.

After all, they had both braced themselves for a support champion; they never expected Fight to pick a top laner—and an early-game top laner at that!

“Fight’s using Dark Angel as support?!” Remi exclaimed before realizing, “Wait, is this even a support? Or is it…”

“It must be Skeletal Empress as support,” Liang Fan speculated. Then, as if a thought had just struck him, he suddenly gasped, “Fight must have decided to use Skeletal Empress as support to balance the damage types the moment they saw Mobile Artillery get snatched!

They deliberately picked Skeletal Empress as their first pick to mislead GQ. Sure enough, GQ fell for it and picked Black-Armoured Scythe, who’s a 50-50 matchup against the Skeletal Empress in lane. They never expected Skeletal Empress to be a support! Based on GQ’s pick, Fight selected Dark Angel, who has a longer range, deals more damage, and is stronger in lane. GQ’s top lane is going to get crushed!”

“Wow!” Remi finally caught on after hearing Liang Fan’s commentary and couldn’t help but exclaim, “Fight played that so slyly! Their coach’s zodiac must be a fox!”

Fight’s coach Chen Jie: ? I didn’t do a thing.

Tang Yu’an, who’d directed his teammates to pick Skeletal Empress right from the start to trick the opposing team: Heh—surprised? Unexpected?

And just like that, both teams’ lineups were set:

Fight: Dark Angel, Vampire Progenitor, Candy Witch, Skeletal Empress, Double Phantom; GQ: Black-Armoured Scythe, Deepsea Faerie, Mobile Artillery, Abyssal Lord, Blade Soul; As soon as their lineups were revealed, even before the match began, GQ had already started to despair.

Liang Fan: “Comparing the two, Fight’s lineup has a massive early-game advantage! GQ won’t be able to gain an edge on any of the three lanes—they’re going to have a really tough time in the early game!”

And wasn’t that true?

In a 1v1 battle between Dark Angel and Scythe, the former could literally pin Scythe beneath its own tower.

Not to mention the bottom lane—one Candy Witch would already be enough for GQ to handle, but with the addition of Skeletal Empress, there’s no way GQ’s bottom lane will be able to leave their tower.

Even in the mid lane, Deepsea Faerie, who was known for being a 50-50 matchup against anyone, fared worst against Vampire Progenitor. Vampire Progenitor, like Deepsea Faerie, was a melee-focused mage, but with higher early-game damage, lifesteal on their abilities, and a sustain that was even better than the Deepsea Faerie’s.

Remi: “GQ picked first, but let Fight select a lineup that completely crushes them! I have to say, Fight truly lives up to being a top-8 team—even their hero selection is this meticulous!”

Liang Fan: “I’m really holding my breath for GQ—will this lineup even make it to the late game?”

GQ: Already giving up, don’t cue us.
