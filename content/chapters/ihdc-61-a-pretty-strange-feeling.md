The second game between igame and UH began shortly thereafter.

Since igame had won the previous game, igame went first with bans and UH went first with picks.

However, UH already knew that igame’s mid laner would pick Beast Master, so as soon as igame banned Realm God, UH immediately followed up by banning Beast Master.

igame used their second ban on Deepsea Faerie.

UH used their second ban on Twin Demonblades.

Then, igame used their third ban on Wind Guardian.

Seeing this ban, Tang Yu’an couldn’t help but laugh: “UH is completely being led by the nose by igame. I bet UH will ban Blade Soul next to snatch Tempest Blade, and then igame will snag Guardian Spirit and Abyssal Lord.”

This season, the pick order and number of picks had been standardized. The team with first pick could only select one hero initially. Although UH’s core was their marksman, since they’d left Tempest Blade on the board, they have no choice but to fight for the first pick—otherwise, if igame’s jungler, Ye Xunjie, secured Tempest Blade, it’d be a one-sided slaughter where he carried his team to victory.

The team with the second pick, on the other hand, could select for two positions at once, so they’d inevitably go for a bot lane composition.

The strength of many bot lane compositions stemmed from the synergy between the marksman and the support—just like Guardian Spirit and Abyssal Lord, or Cat Spirit and Spider Queen.

If you picked Guardian Spirit alone, its survival capabilities were too weak; if it couldn’t take down the enemy instantly, it’d be the one taken down.

If you picked Cat Spirit alone, her early-game damage is too low, and her laning ability wasn’t any better than Desert Hunter’s.

Sure enough, after UH banned Blade Soul, they locked Tempest Blade for their first pick with almost no hesitation.

Immediately after, igame locked in Guardian Spirit and Abyssal Lord.

“See? Now this is what you call being a prophet!” Tang Yu’an said proudly.

Chen Jie looked utterly unimpressed: “Anyone could’ve guessed that, right? UH probably figured it out when they saw igame ban Wind Guardian, but they had no choice—they had to take Tempest Blade with their first pick.”

Tang Yu’an ignored him. After his moment of pride, he couldn’t help but sigh: “It’s a good thing we’re not playing against igame on the first day. With this combo, not even Winne—let alone UH—could handle them.”

“In terms of Beast Master, you’d be able to play him, right?” Chen Jie asked suddenly.

Tang Yu’an thought for a moment: “I can play him, sure, but he’s too squishy. He doesn’t have crowd control until his ultimate. Laning is fine, but if the enemy jungler comes to gank, whether Yumao escapes or not, I’m dead for sure. I’d be better off picking Curse Master.”

Ling Suyu blinked—so… was there any hero you couldn't play?

When a pro player says “I can play,” it doesn’t mean the same thing as when a casual player says it.

For a casual player, “I can play” just meant knowing what the abilities were, how to build items, and how to execute the moves.

But for a pro player, “I can play” meant “I can guarantee a certain level of win-rate in a match.”

It didn’t necessarily mean they could carry the team, but at least they wouldn’t hold it back.

Take Ling Suyu himself, for example. As a pro player, he specialized in marksmen, but as the saying goes, “Know yourself and know your enemy, and you will never be defeated in a hundred battles.” Whenever a new champion was released, he played them for a while to familiarize himself with their abilities and mechanics. No matter what role they filled, he made sure he could play them at the level of an average player. That way, when he encountered them in a match, he’d know exactly how to dodge their skills and secure a counter-kill.

Don’t let the fact that Ling Suyu always played marksmen fool you—when he played casual matches and drew heroes from other roles (except for pure supports), he could still carry the team.

But if you asked him if he knew how to play that hero, his answer would be “no,” because he lacked the confidence to continue carrying the team with that hero on the professional stage.

Come to think of it, why did he end up focusing on marksmen again?

Ling Suyu couldn’t help but reflect on his memories from before he went pro.

He was drawn into the game by Glorious’s promotional videos, and because of his social anxiety, he always played solo queue.

At first, he was fairly “accommodating”—he’d let his teammates pick their roles first, and he’d fill in the remaining position.

If his teammates didn’t pick a role, he’d take whatever role was left.

But later, after several consecutive games where the bot lane collapsed and the carry couldn’t carry the team, making it impossible to turn the game around, he started aggressively snatching the carry role!

If you can’t carry, don’t steal my marksman!

Even if you can carry, don’t steal my marksman!

I’m playing marksman no matter what! Do whatever you want!

Ling Suyu didn’t like typing, so every time he joined a game, he’d instantly lock-in marksman, then pretend to be offline. No matter what his teammates said, he wouldn’t reply.

Fortunately, many players weren’t limited to just one role, so when he insisted on playing marksman, they just let him have it.

Occasionally, someone who didn’t take it lying down would pick another marksman to compete with him for gold, or even play a marksman in the jungle.

It didn’t really matter—whether you could carry the team or not came down to your own skills.

In any case, once he focused on marksman, his win rate shot up, going from 50% to nearly 80% in just one season.

Back then, even though marksmen were all late-game champions, their auto-attack damage was high enough that if you gained even a slight economic advantage early-on and got one extra attack speed item, you could take on five opponents alone.

Oh, and at that time, Cat Spirit and Desert Hunter were both meta picks.

Ling Suyu climbed all the way to the Glorious tier by solo-queuing as a marksman, then received a private message from UH, and then…

In his first year as a pro, Cat Spirit and Desert Hunter were constantly on the ban list. At the time, the Mobile Artillery mid-lane playstyle was extremely popular, and the only heroes Ling Suyu could play were Hunter’s Blade and Barrier Sorceress.

In his second year as a pro, Glorious released a new hero, Guardian Spirit, which boosted skill damage. Guardian Spirit instantly became a meta powerhouse. Cat Spirit and Desert Hunter were no longer regulars on the ban list, but they also fell out of favor with players.

That was the year Ling Suyu had his highest win rate, yet he no longer had the opportunity to compete in matches.

In his third year as a pro, Glorious introduced a new mid-lane hero, Realm God. For the first time, they reduced basic attack damage, causing mid-laners to temporarily replace marksmen as the primary damage dealers.

In his fourth year as a pro, Glorious introduced Netherworld Shadow. While reducing skill damage, they once again lowered basic attack damage and increased critical strike damage, completely widening the gap between burst-damage marksmen and sustained-damage marksmen. Overnight, sustained-damage marksmen became obsolete.

In his fifth year as a pro, Glorious successively introduced the new mid-lane champions Phoenix Soul and Beast Master, raising skill damage to an all-time high while nerfing auto-attack damage to an all-time low. Not only were sustained-damage marksmen rendered obsolete, but even burst-damage marksmen found their very existence threatened.

And then came this year.

Ling Suyu watched the match between igame and UH on the screen and pursed his lips.

This year, basic attack damage needed to be adjusted back.

The second game between igame and UH was, to put it mildly, a foregone conclusion.

UH’s bot lane was completely dominated by igame, who were capitalizing on the meta’s current strengths.

UH’s mid laner couldn’t secure Wind Guardian, which would have suppressed Curse Master, nor could he get Deepsea Faerie, which would have allowed him to go toe-to-toe with most heroes. Instead, he was forced to pick Vampire Progenitor, a hero of the same type as Deepsea Faerie.

As a result, he lost lane dominance early on, lacked sufficient damage late game, was virtually nonexistent in team fights, and kept getting instantly killed by igame’s elusive jungler. His gold was even lower than the support’s.

UH’s top laner picked Dark Angel to balance his laning phase economy, hoping to put pressure on igame. Early on, he did indeed force igame’s top laner to stay under his tower, but later, as UH’s mid and bot lanes were successively crushed by igame’s jungler, UH was forced into team fights with igame. Dark Angel excelled at laning but struggled in team fights; the healing from his mark couldn’t keep up with the damage igame was dealing. As soon as he fell, his teammates behind him fell one after another like dominoes.

—UH was wiped out by igame in a team fight.

“Alright, no need to watch anymore. It’s 2-0,” Tang Yu’an had already reached his conclusion. “UH is a team that excels more in the late game than the early game, yet they insisted on picking an early-game composition. I really don’t know what their coach was thinking.”

“Probably because they were afraid igame would push too fast and they wouldn’t make it to the late game,” Chen Jie speculated. “Just like when we faced igame with Desert Hunter. I hate to admit it, but UH’s playstyle is the closest to ours.”

“They probably picked the way they did because they were afraid igame would get Dark Angel,” Mo Xiaoyun guessed. “And that was likely exactly what igame expected.”

“Holy crap! igame is terrifying!” Dou Jiayan had been enjoying the match, but after hearing his teammates’ analyses, he couldn’t help but get goosebumps. “Are they actually using their brains to play this game?”

“What else?” Chen Jie shot him a disdainful look. “Do you play with your butt?”

“Of course I play with my hands! My hands!”

Dou Jiayan continued to marvel at igame’s chain of traps while howling in amazement, unaware that igame was also talking about Fight at that very moment.

“It’s so much more comfortable playing against other teams!” igame’s captain sighed.

“It feels so good to have everything under control!” his teammates echoed.

“It isn’t like Fight, who’s always a step ahead of us,” igame’s captain grumbled, “Whatever we want to pick, they ban; whatever we want to ban, they pick. It’s so infuriating.”

“Yeah, I didn’t even get the chance to play my Beast Master,” the mid laner chimed in, casually taking a dig at their own captain. “So, can we say that Fight’s captain is smarter than ours?”

igame’s captain, Ye Xunjie, twitched the corner of his mouth: “Don’t leave after the match! I want a one-on-one with you!”

Mid-laner: “Sure, as long as you don’t pick jungler, we’ll still be good teammates!”

Tang Yu’an, who was watching the match, suddenly sneezed three times in a row.

Huh?

Once is thinking, twice is cursing, and thrice is missing—who’s missing me?

In the end, the second game between igame and UH lasted 18 minutes. Not only did igame defeat UH once again, but they also won two sweeping victories.

It was unclear whether UH’s players had built up too much pressure before the match, lost their composure after losing the first game, or realized they’d fallen right into igame’s carefully laid trap, but their play was clearly unstable.

In any case, they made significantly more mistakes in the second game, especially their jungler. Despite picking Tempest Blade, a powerful jungler capable of one-shotting enemies, he failed to farm effectively and ended up as a fragile, useless character that even the support could bully at will.

The quarterfinal matches were also BO3s. After igame won two games in a row, UH had absolutely no chance of turning things around.

On the first day, igame earned 2 points, while UH’s score dropped to -2.

The audience was thrilled with this result and flooded the chat with messages expressing their joy:

【Way to go, igame!】

【Huh? Isn’t this UH, last year’s third-place team? It’s been a whole season since I last saw them play this bad!】

【So what if igame treats you like Fight? Do you even deserve to be compared to Fight?】

【The only thing UH fans can brag about now is Jiang Hao’s skills, but it’s one thing for UH to embarrass themselves. Embarrassing their own fans on top of that? That’s brutal!】

【No big deal, it’s only a -2. We’ve still got a match against Fight tomorrow; let’s go for a -4 in one go!】

Fight’s members watched the live comments for a while, then headed off to practice, thoroughly satisfied.

Their archrival team, igame, had defeated UH 2-0, so they had to step up their game and aim to secure 2 points on the very first day.

At 4:00 p.m., the second match of the quarterfinals between YJ and SMG began.

Fight watched the first game while having an early dinner. When they saw SMG’s bot lane pick Cat Spirit and Spider Queen, they nearly spat out their food.

“Wow!” Dou Jiayan couldn’t believe it. “Are [what the hell] out of their minds?”

“What’s your problem?” Tang Yu’an raised an eyebrow. “So it’s okay for Yumao and I to pick this comp, but not other teams?”

Dou Jiayan: “Yumao’s Cat Spirit is really strong! But why on earth would [what the hell] pick Cat Spirit?”

Tang Yu’an: “You’re just looking down on [what the hell]’s marksman, aren’t you?”

“No…” Dou Jiayan instinctively wanted to argue, but upon further reflection, he admitted, “Ugh, you’re right. I really don’t trust his Cat Spirit.”

During the group stage, Ling Suyu carried the entire team with Cat Spirit and Desert Hunter, undoubtedly vindicating these two marksmen, who had both long been criticized as “bottom-tier.”

After the group stage, whether in ranked or casual matches, the pick rates for Cat Spirit and Desert Hunter rose significantly. Yet players in other roles still felt conflicted when they saw these two heroes.

They might have originally said, “Trolls, huh?”

Now they’d say, “Do you think you’re Feather?”

This reaction was perfectly normal, because hero power levels are what they are—just because Ling Suyu’s Cat Spirit was strong doesn’t mean anyone else’s Cat Spirit could be that strong.

Ling Suyu’s error rate and skill hit rate were unmatched; when he played Cat Spirit, he didn't even consider error margins.

But what he could do, most people simply couldn’t.

Ling Suyu’s Cat Spirit uses “wave-like subtle steps” to play hide-and-seek with the jungler.

Most players’ Cat Spirits dashed in the wrong direction, ran into walls with their Flash, were hopeless at everything, and remained the top candidates for dying.

Ling Suyu’s Cat Spirit: zero deaths, carrying four teammates, the main damage dealer, dominating the entire game.

Most players’ Cat Spirit: zero damage, dragging four teammates down, constantly scraping damage off the surface, with a presence as faint as a ghost.

After witnessing Ling Suyu’s Cat Spirit in action, Fight’s members all acknowledged his skill.

But that didn’t mean they approved of Cat Spirit as a pick.

When they saw SMG’s marksman pick Cat Spirit, their reaction was the same as that of casual players who randomly got matched with a Cat Spirit—deep down, they simply didn’t believe anyone other than Ling Suyu could master the hero.

In this match, SMG did indeed lose—but surprisingly, they didn’t go down without a fight.

The game lasted nearly an hour, with both teams suffering team wipes.

At the 11-minute mark, SMG secured the first Dragon, but were wiped out by YJ. After all three dragon legion waves passed without incident, SMG ended up losing a tower instead.

At the 25-minute mark, YJ stole the second Dragon. They could have played it safe and pushed towers, but instead insisted on diving the tower to initiate a team fight. SMG counter-attacked and wiped out YJ, leaving only SMG’s jungler and marksman alive. Both players quickly recovered and cleared minions at a rapid pace, not only holding off three waves of dragon legion but also pushing down one of YJ’s towers.

After respawning, YJ played more cautiously. The two teams traded waves of minions multiple times. Then, at the 37-minute mark, YJ seized an opportunity to take down SMG’s Cat Spirit first, followed by a decisive teamfight that wiped out SMG, and immediately went on to secure the Hero’s Chalice.

YJ’s marksman, Ma Mingxi, picked up the Hero’s Chalice and took down all three of SMG’s bottom lane turrets in one go. At the 39-minute mark, the two teams clashed in front of SMG’s main tower. SMG protected Cat Spirit and wiped out YJ. After Ma Mingxi respawned, he killed Cat Spirit and the jungler, only to be taken down by SMG’s mid laner with a single combo.

The two teams remained deadlocked for a while longer, until YJ’s jungler coordinated with their mid laner to execute a kamikaze flanking maneuver that instantly took down Cat Spirit. YJ’s top laner and support then protected their marksman as they wiped out SMG in one fell swoop, finally seizing the opportunity to completely push down SMG’s main tower.

The match lasted 56 minutes. When it ended, not only did YJ breathe a sigh of relief, but the commentators and viewers did as well—only SMG’s players wore expressions of reluctance.

“That was amazing…” Although SMG lost, Dou Jiayan was blown away by their performance. “Feng Lang’s Cat Spirit was actually pretty good?”

Feng Lang was the real name of SMG’s marksman; his ID is S.MG-WW, apparently an abbreviation for “wind wave[^1],” but the commentators liked to call him Weiwei, and over time, even his fans started calling him Weiwei.

“They’re all pros, how bad could they really be?” Tang Yu’an said. “YJ played way too cautiously in the early game. Even though their bot lane composition was clearly much better than [what the hell]’s, they didn’t dare push.”

“To be fair, if the enemy had been Yumao’s Cat Spirit, I wouldn’t have dared to push either,” Chen Jie said. “Clearly, YJ played against Feng Lang’s Cat Spirit as if it were Yumao’s.”

“YJ is a team that excels in the early game. If they can’t secure an early advantage, they’ll be in serious trouble late in the game.” Tang Yu’an said, “In this game, they were so preoccupied with the enemy’s Cat Spirit that they failed to secure an early lead, forcing the game into the late game before finally winning. Their mistakes clearly increased in the late game, but [what the hell] made plenty of mistakes too. The only reason they managed to pull off a late-game victory was that, fortunately, they were up against [what the hell]. If it had been us, winning within half an hour would have been a conservative estimate.”

Ling Suyu, standing nearby, pursed his lips and said nothing.

Tang Yu’an keenly picked up on his reaction, looked at him, and asked tentatively, “What are you thinking about, Yumao?”

“How should I put this…” Ling Suyu paused to gather his thoughts before continuing, “Watching someone else play Cat Spirit is a pretty strange feeling.”

He clearly played other marksmen too, but when he saw others playing different marksmen, he didn't feel anything special.

Today, while watching Feng Lang play Cat Spirit, he felt miserable the whole time. It was kind of like…

“Don’t ruin my Cat Spirit, aaaah!” ← That kind of feeling.

Several times, when he thought it was time to retreat, Feng Lang’s Cat Spirit didn’t pull back—it charged forward instead. It gave him a heart attack, and he thought to himself, “It’s over. You’re dead.”

And then Feng Lang’s Cat Spirit really did die.

Ling Suyu was truly devastated—what did I do wrong to make you force me to watch my favourite hero fall!

Cat Spirit is supposed to carry the team in style, not lie pitifully on the ground!

He knew this mindset wasn’t right. After all, the Cat Spirit was just a hero in Glorious not his personal possession. So he simply said, “It’s a pretty strange feeling,” and left it at that.

But even without him saying a word, Tang Yu’an could sense exactly what he was thinking: “I get it. It’s probably similar to how I feel when I watch someone else’s Skeletal Empress swing her hammer and fail to stun anyone—it makes me want to jump in and fight for them myself.”

“Oh oh oh, I get it!” Dou Jiayan chimed in. “I played a casual match the day before yesterday. Someone on my team picked Phoenix Soul but refused to trade it to me, so I just had to watch him feed over and over. F*ck, it really sucked! If you can’t f*cking carry, don’t take my Phoenix Soul!”

“Hey, hey, hey, watch your language,” Tang Yu’an reminded him. “You’ve been swearing more and more lately, haven’t you?”

“I picked it up from you guys,” Dou Jiayan blurted out, only to get smacked by Chen Jie.

After the group had a good laugh, Chen Jie said, “Alright, it’s about time. Let’s head to the arena.”

Everyone agreed.

So, before YJ and SMG played their second game, Fight packed up their things, left the hotel, and headed to the competition venue.

Shortly after they arrived at the venue, the second game between YJ and SMG began.

YJ didn’t ban Cat Spirit, but SMG didn’t pick her either.

They picked Candy Witch and Mobile Artillery.

However, unlike Fight, who had their marksman play Candy Witch, SMG ultimately assigned Candy Witch to the mid laner and Mobile Artillery to the marksman.

Given that SMG’s player positions weren’t fixed, YJ spent a long time at the start trying to figure out whether Candy Witch would be played in the mid or bottom lane.

And since Fight had previously played Candy Witch in the bot lane, and SMG had used Cat Spirit—a hero Ling Suyu had played—in the previous game, YJ bet that they were emulating Fight and would pick Candy Witch for the bot lane.

As it turned out, they were wrong.

SMG’s Candy Witch went mid, and Mobile Artillery went bot.

Guessing the wrong lane for a hero wasn’t a big deal—if you could gain an advantage, go for it; if not, switch lanes.

But whether it was because they were still dazed from the previous game or something else, YJ’s mid laner actually tried to steal lane dominance from the dominant Candy Witch. He pushed too deep and was 1v1’d by SMG’s mid laner.

In matches between professional teams, catching up after falling behind in gold was no easy feat.

In the previous game, they could still rely on the fact of Cat Spirit being a more late-game hero to win team fights even while trailing in gold. But in this game, they were dominated by Candy Witch almost the entire time, and the match ended in just 21 minutes—YJ, a team that usually dominated opponents in the early game, was outplayed by SMG in the early stages.

“No way! YJ and SMG are going to a third game?” Dou Jiayan couldn’t believe it. “The first game lasted nearly an hour—how long do they plan to play the third one?”

It was currently around 5:40 p.m. After each match, there was a 15-minute break for both teams to rest and review the game. This meant that the third match between YJ and SMG would begin at 6:00 p.m.

The match between Fight and XY was originally scheduled for 7:00 p.m.

Every team in the quarterfinals was a hot favourite receiving significant attention, so it was impossible for both matches to take place simultaneously.

Therefore, if the third game between YJ and SMG ran straight through until 7:00 p.m., the match between Fight and XY would be postponed.

This was normal, but Dou Jiayan still wanted to finish early and get some rest.

Fortunately, the third game between YJ and SMG didn’t last very long.

In the third game, YJ decisively banned Candy Witch, while SMG once again fielded the bottom lane duo of Cat Spirit and Spider Queen.

But this time, no “miracle” occurred.

YJ played exceptionally aggressively from the start; Ma Mingxi used Flash to apply crowd control before even reaching level 6, instantly taking down Feng Lang’s Cat Spirit with a full skill combo. He leveled up in the blink of an eye, carrying the entire team to victory within 20 minutes.

In the end, YJ defeated S.MG 2-1, earning 1 point.

SMG’s point-total for Day 1 was -1.

Immediately afterward, the match between Fight and XY began.
