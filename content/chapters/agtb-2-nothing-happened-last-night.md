The next morning, Ren Jinhuan was awakened by the commotion downstairs. When he opened his eyes, he realized he was in an unfamiliar location—to be precise, a guest room on the second floor of the bar. His mind was still a blur. He wanted to reach for his phone to check the time, but as he sat up, he realized his upper body was completely naked. This made him tense up instantly; his back stiffened like a worn-out mechanical part. It took him half a minute to try to recall and make sense of the whole situation, but he couldn’t recall anything after the blackout. If he’d been the same as yesterday, this kind of scenario would never have happened, but at this very moment, the situation was simply beyond his control.

Just then, the bathroom door handle turned. Ren Jinhuan belatedly realized that he’d been hearing the sound of running water all along—which meant there was someone else in the room.

He watched, nearly holding his breath, as the other person stepped out. It was a young man, tall and slender, with a lean, athletic build. Fresh out of the shower, he was shirtless, with only a bath towel loosely wrapped around his lower body. The man dried his hair, walked over to the coffee machine on the table, brewed an Americano, and only then slowly turned around to say “Good morning” to Ren Jinhuan.

Ren Jinhuan met his gaze and found him strikingly handsome, with features that gave off a refreshingly open impression. But given the current situation, his first thought was: Had the quality competition of nightclub staff really intensified to this extent?

With these thoughts swirling in his mind, Ren Jinhuan steadied his breathing and returned the greeting as naturally as possible, taking the opportunity to inquire about the events of the previous night.

“I saw you weren’t feeling well, and that guest kept following you, so I just stepped in to help.” His answer was brief and to the point. His voice was pleasant, too.

“So where are my clothes?”

The man gestured for him to look at the nightstand. Sure enough, all his clothes were neatly folded there. “I was afraid you might get up in the middle of the night to throw up, so I took them off for you.” That was a reasonable explanation.

As for his phone, the man walked over to the TV and unplugged the power strip: “You slept for nearly twelve hours. It’s ten o’clock now, so I charged your phone for you.” It was a thoughtful gesture. Ren Jinhuan thanked him, then watched as the man approached the bed. Just as he was about to take the phone, the man suddenly said softly, “Don’t worry, there’s no need to be so on guard. Nothing happened last night.” He paused, raised his eyes, and saw a hint of mischief on the other’s striking face.

A dozen or so unread messages popped up on his phone. Three were from Wen Yan, asking how he’d slept, and a senior from their alma mater, someone he was close with at the company, had also sent a few concerned messages. The rest were more trivial matters, but he didn’t have time to reply to them just yet. He picked up his clothes and put them on one by one while the stranger dried his hair in the bathroom.

Ten minutes later, the man emerged again, this time wearing a dark bathrobe with his hair freshly styled. With his sharp features and piercing eyes, he was indeed a striking figure. He glanced at Ren Jinhuan, took out his phone, and said, “It’s a pleasure to meet you. Let’s add each other as friends so we can stay in touch.” He held out the QR code screen and added, “This is my personal account.”

Ren Jinhuan looked at the QR code in front of him and hesitated slightly. He suspected the man intended to add him as a friend first and then ask for a service fee. However, he had no intention of keeping this person within his social circle. He had a habit of organizing contacts with labels—he was even slightly obsessive about it—and everyone in his address book was sorted into corresponding categories. The problem was, the man standing before him didn’t fit any of his existing labels. Creating a special group just for someone like this would not only seem like overkill to Ren Jinhuan, but he’d also feel it was a waste of phone storage.

Of course, he didn’t let these thoughts show on his face. Ren Jinhuan simply assumed this was one of the man’s ways of making a living. Fortunately, he usually carried cash with him, so he pulled two hundred-yuan bills from his pocket and placed them on the man’s phone screen. “Thank you very much for your help last night, and I’m sorry for holding you up during your work hours. Here’s a little compensation. I rarely go to bars, and we’re in different circles, so I don’t think we’ll have a chance to meet again.”

The man looked at the two large red bills, paused for a moment, then smiled at him. “Are you sure you don’t want to add me? I usually use my work number for business, and only a few close friends and family know my personal number.”

Hearing this, Ren Jinhuan felt a helpless urge to laugh. It seemed as though the man was implying that his personal number was reserved for VIP service—a special offer valid only for today. Perhaps the man really was the top performer at this bar, leading the pack in KPIs; 200 yuan really was a bit of a slap in the face. So, Ren Jinhuan pulled out the only 100-yuan bill he had left and handed it over again: “I’m sorry, this hourly rate might seem a bit low to you, but considering the workload from last night, I think 300 yuan is a fair figure.” I hope you know to stop while you’re ahead.

The man tilted his chin slightly upward, leaning against the wall. He stared intently at Ren Jinhuan’s face for a few seconds before finally accepting the money with a hint of regret: “Well, it’s really a shame that I won’t get to know you.” He picked up a paper cup with a lid, poured in some fresh, hot milk, and handed it to Ren Jinhuan: “I prepared this for you, it’ll be easier on your stomach.”

“Thank you. Have a great weekend.”

“You too.”

As he reached the entryway, the man opened the door for him. Just as Ren Jinhuan was about to step outside, he heard the man say casually, “If something had actually happened last night, would it have been more than three hundred?”

Ren Jinhuan replied with a smile, but without a hint of politesse: “It certainly would have been more, because you’d be facing a massive lawsuit from me.”

Once outside the bar, Ren Jinhuan hailed a designated driver. While waiting, he called Mr. Zhao, the instigator of last night’s events. Mr. Zhao had likely found new entertainment during the latter half of the night and clearly didn’t expect Ren Jinhuan to contact him—after all, he’d already braced himself for being permanently blocked.

“Hey, Teacher Ren, how did you sleep last night?”

“Thanks to you, I haven’t slept this well since I started working. So I’m calling to ask for the recipe for that special cocktail—it works way better than melatonin.” Ren Jinhuan took a sip of milk and found it was just the right temperature.

“Oh, come on, you can’t say that. You’re the one who cast the bait, how can you blame the fish for biting?” Mr. Zhao lay surrounded by a bevy of beauties, looking quite at ease.

Ren Jinhuan heard the commotion across the phone and smiled. “I don’t like to take things to extremes, but I also hate having my boundaries crossed. I’ve already obtained the bar’s surveillance footage, but I don’t want to use it, because I genuinely want to continue doing business with you, Mr. Zhao.”

Just then, the designated driver arrived. As Ren Jinhuan spoke, he got into the car: “Last night, you said something truly unjustified about me. You said I was toying with you and didn’t give you any perks, but I’ve always kept your preferences in mind.” With that, he leaned back leisurely in the back seat, confident of victory. “Aladdin and Sanya’s ‘Hive Flight’ previously had a joint project that secured each leader a chance to test-fly a private jet. I just registered a pass for you under my name. If you have time, you can bring a friend along. Of course, there are also accompanying wine, travel, dining, and accommodation services. I wonder if this might convince you to renew our partnership?”

After hearing all this, Mr. Zhao was practically won over: “Teacher Ren, just how many tricks do you have up your sleeve? I don’t know if I’m lucky or unlucky to know you. Staying in the internet industry really holds you back—you should be in the finance or consulting sectors.” He knew the benefits wouldn’t come for free, so he asked what else the other party wanted to figure out.

“I’ve been thinking it over, and given your extensive connections, surely you could get your hands on a real photo of him?” It was still about last night’s incident.

“Alright, don’t worry. I’ll send the photo right away.”

Ren Jinhuan hung up the phone and leisurely gazed out the window at the street scenery rushing by behind them, feeling quite content. His glass of milk was empty, and his stomach felt much better. Since the person was related to the company’s founder, he could use him as one of his backup plans.

His index finger tapped the edge of the car door rhythmically as he pondered how to build a good relationship with the other party. Just then, his phone beeped with a message notification from Mr. Zhao—an image attachment. He tapped it open with anticipation, but as soon as it loaded, his expression froze. That sense of ease and triumph vanished in an instant. He stared blankly for a few seconds, zoomed in further, and double-checked. It was definitely him. The man in the photo was tall, wearing a black sweater and jeans, standing among a group of foreign professors—young, confident, and exceptionally charismatic.

But he was none other than the stranger he’d just fobbed off with 300 yuan at the bar.

Meanwhile, in a guest room at the bar, Jin Xiangtang was sitting on the edge of the bed, talking on the phone with Director Lin. Director Lin asked him if he was still adjusting to life back in China, if he’d gotten over the jet lag, and invited him over for a meal when he had time. “Your aunt and your cousin really want to see you, and so does your younger cousin. I heard that ever since you came back, he’s been pestering you every day to help him prepare for the children’s programming competition.”

Jin Xiangtang smiled and said, “Sure, I’ll head over with Mom and Dad in a few days.”

“As for the company, it’s just as I described to you before. Old Fang and the others will all be very supportive of you. If you have any ideas, go ahead. I’m looking forward to seeing the results you’ll deliver.”

Jin Xiangtang shifted his gaze to the documents beside him—records of his educational and work history that had fallen out of Ren Jinhuan’s bag the night before. He tossed three hundred yuan on top of them and replied smugly, “Of course. I’m looking forward to it just as much.”
