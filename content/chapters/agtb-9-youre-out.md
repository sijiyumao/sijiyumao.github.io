“So I’m curious—why did you lie to Vice President Yu and the others?”

Jin Xiangtang held a new deck of cards and skillfully performed a fan cut. As the cards stacked up, they resembled a collapsed staircase or a closed accordion. Ren Jinhuan found the motion fascinating.

The two were in a 24-hour café where off-white curtains blocked the view from the hallway, creating an invisible private booth. It was now 9:00 p.m., and the café was mostly filled with couples on dates and college students studying through the night—none of whom would ever imagine that these two well-dressed, elite-looking professionals had come here to stay up all night playing cards.

Ren Jinhuan watched as he placed the shuffled deck in the center of the table and casually picked up the top card, which just so happened to be a joker.

“I don’t consider that lying,” Ren Jinhuan countered gently. “To me, it was simply a preemptive declaration of my future results.”

Jin Xiangtang seemed to grasp his meaning and continued, “To expand your social circle and build connections?”

Ren Jinhuan stirred his orange juice a few times with a straw, the pulp swirled in a winding pattern. He had no intention of answering the question directly and instead tilted his head, saying, “Comes with the industry.”

“What do you mean?”

“Fake IT till you make IT.”[^1] Faced with this little bit of English wordplay, Jin Xiangtang could only smile and sigh: “All right, since you don’t want to talk about it, I won’t push you. But I don’t agree with that motto.”

To their right stood a simple bookshelf filled with books designed in a retro, 20th-century style. Jin Xiangtang caught sight of a theater magazine featuring Arthur Miller’s “Death of a Salesman” on the cover. He said, “I only believe in the authenticity built on true ability; false self-expectations are boulders that drag people to the bottom of the river.”[^2]

Seeing how earnestly he spoke, Ren Jinhuan lowered his gaze slightly and replied sincerely, “You certainly have the credentials to say that. After all, most people aren’t as successful, confident, and renowned at such a young age as you are.” When it came to paying compliments, he was always at ease, making people feel as though they were basking in a spring breeze.

Jin Xiangtang pondered this for a moment and suddenly understood why Wen Yan had been at this subordinate’s mercy. He decided not to press the issue and conceded a point: “As for tomorrow night’s card game, what outcome are you hoping for?”

“Neither a win nor a loss,” Ren Jinhuan replied. Since the participants were all high-ranking, seasoned figures, winning too conspicuously would be tactless, while losing badly might expose his true abilities.

Jin Xiangtang followed up with another question: “Why did you choose me over Wen Yan? Or rather, what made you think I’d help you?” This was perhaps the question that interested him most tonight.

“Because I believed,” Ren Jinhuan said, looking at him with surprising conviction. It was a very simple opening, and he continued, “I believed you could help me. I believed in the absolute power you uphold, and I believed that only you could help me achieve my goal.”

Jin Xiangtang’s expression faltered slightly. The other man’s face was reflected on the wall of the glass in front of him, blending into the light like a dream. He stared intently, then finally flashed an indifferent smile. “It’s a real shame you didn’t say that to Wen Yan. If he’d heard that, he’d probably have been swept off his feet by such ‘sweet talk’ and would have laid down his life for you.”

His response did not make Ren Jinhuan show any signs of frustration; on the contrary, he responded firmly, “You’ve misunderstood me. I’d only say this to you. Although I don’t know you very well yet, I get the sense that you’re someone who loves a challenge.”

He turned the Little Joker in his hand face up and held it out toward Jin Xiangtang. Jokers weren’t used in Texas Hold’em, and Ren Jinhuan cast his gaze upon the card. He gave it a gentle wave from bottom to top, making it resemble a butterfly taking flight from his palm. It never entered the sea of flowers, but it still enchanted one’s eyes. He said, “Don’t you want to see if you can turn the tide with this ‘discarded card’?”

Jin Xiangtang smiled silently. He reshuffled the deck of cards beside him and, using some unknown technique, precisely pulled out the Big Joker, placing it on top of Ren Jinhuan’s card. A sly gleam flashed in his eyes.

“There’s something I need to make clear to you up front: the gambling table is a very dangerous place. Everyone hides their true intentions and schemes to take others’ chips. Things you can’t control will remain precarious and fleeting, even when you hold them in your hands. So, can you really trust me one hundred percent?”

The clock struck ten. Ren Jinhuan, holding the 7 of Clubs and the 10 of Spades, fell into deep thought. For some reason, the words Jin Xiangtang had spoken at the café came flooding back to him. Li-jie had already revealed three cards, and for now, he could only make a pair of 10s. Qin Heng reminded him, “Xiao Jin, it’s your turn to bet.” Only then did he snap out of his reverie. He glanced in Jin Xiangtang’s direction; the other man made a loose fist and tapped his index finger twice with his thumb. So Ren Jinhuan moved some of his chips into the pot, doubling his bet.

This was one of the signals they’d agreed on the night before. At the time, Ren Jinhuan had asked if they might get caught, to which Jin Xiangtang replied, “If we do get caught, wouldn’t that make me your accomplice?” It was an answer that put his mind at ease even more than a firm promise would have.

After the flop and river had been flipped over[^3], Ren Jinhuan won the hand outright. Shen Feng, the head of the Marketing & Branding Department, said, “Jinhuan, you played that well.” Ren Jinhuan smiled and said, “I was just lucky.” In the earlier rounds, everyone played relatively conservatively, mainly to figure out their opponents’ habits. Following Jin Xiangtang’s signal, Ren Jinhuan chose to fold most of the time. Once Vice President Yu and the others had won around half the hands, Jin Xiangtang let him start winning, a move that both saved face and allowed him to quietly make a profit.

Ren Jinhuan was firmly convinced that choosing Jin Xiangtang was the only correct decision. However, the fact that he won when told to win and lost when told to lose—effectively handing the puppet’s strings to Jin Xiangtang and allowing him to pull them—was, upon closer reflection, undeniably risky.

Shortly after, Vice President Yu won two consecutive hands in spectacular fashion. Wen Yan praised him, saying, “Teacher Yu, you truly live up to your reputation as a gold medalist in the International Mathematical Olympiad.” Vice President Yu simply waved his hand. “A true hero doesn’t boast about past glories. Otherwise, I’d be what you lot call ‘middle-aged and out of touch.’” Everyone couldn’t help but laugh, and then they heard Vice President Yu add, “I just know how to crunch a few more numbers, but Teacher Qin excels at reading his opponents’ minds—he’s far more skilled than I am.”

Everyone glanced at Qin Heng’s chip count and noticed it was roughly the same as Vice President Yu’s. Qin Heng’s prowess in the poker community was well-known, but what was even more impressive was how he won—calmly, quietly, and without fanfare—just like his demeanor in the workplace. Qin Heng was among the older members of the company’s senior leadership, but he was still one step away from becoming a VP. This was partly due to health issues in recent years and partly because consulting and analysis were considered support roles in the internet industry, unlike departments such as production, operations, and R&D, where results were easier to achieve. Ren Jinhuan had heard that Qin Heng had long wanted to consolidate all the company’s analytical positions into a single department, believing this would make it easier to establish a systematic framework and help young analysts grow faster. However, he had been unable to do so due to conflicts among the various business units.

Qin Heng smiled and offered a modest refusal. At forty-seven, he had noticeable gray hair, but his face was refined and full of vitality; Ren Jinhuan held him in high regard. Later, when the conversation turned to business, Wen Yan mentioned that he should seek more guidance from Teacher Qin Heng. Qin Heng replied that there were many young analysts in the company whose reports were quite good, and he specifically singled out Ren Jinhuan’s previous “Analysis of the Overseas E-commerce Market,” noting that it was written on par with the work of those in the consulting industry. Ren Jinhuan lowered his head slightly, his eyes sparkling with joy hidden beneath a veil of shyness. Jin Xiangtang caught sight of his expression and was suddenly reminded of fawns being fed in a park—just a few cookies were enough to make them crane their necks and gather around you. There was a certain innocence to it.

Just as everyone was continuing to play at the same pace as before, Jin Xiangtang suddenly came on strong, winning several rounds in a row. He took more than half of the others’ chips, especially from Wen Yan. In one hand, with only Wen Yan and Jin Xiangtang left at the table, Wen Yan had put together a strong hand and raised the bet several times over. But Jin Xiangtang played with fierce decisiveness, ultimately going all-in. Wen Yan couldn’t tell if Jin Xiangtang had a better hand, so he was ultimately forced to fold.

As Jin Xiangtang pocketed the chips, Wen Yan revealed his hand and said reluctantly, “I wanted to see yours.” At a poker table, players had the right to refuse to let their opponents view their cards after the hand was over. Jin Xiangtang showed him only one card, but didn’t reveal its rank—only the suit: a spade. Wen Yan’s expression stiffened slightly: “Why didn’t you show the whole hand?” Jin Xiangtang smiled at him: “I was afraid you’d regret it even more if you saw it.”

This was a clear hint: Jin Xiangtang’s hand was definitely not as good as Wen Yan’s. Vice President Yu burst out laughing and patted him on the shoulder: “Xiangtang, pulling a stunt like that on Wen Yan is really unsportsmanlike.”

Perhaps due to this single loss, Wen Yan’s performance faltered afterward, and he was left with almost no chips. Ren Jinhuan, following Jin Xiangtang’s instructions, was currently enjoying a modest lead. Noticing Wen Yan’s serious expression, he began to weigh his own options. Around 11:00 p.m., Ren Jinhuan found himself facing Wen Yan in a hand. Jin Xiangtang signaled “call” with a hand gesture, but after much deliberation, Ren Jinhuan ultimately chose not to follow the cue and folded instead.

He had sensed earlier that Wen Yan was in a bad mood. After all, he was his boss, so he had to help him save face; adding fuel to the fire would only create more trouble. Since he still had a decent stack, losing a portion wouldn’t hurt. However, when the hand ended, an inexplicable silence fell over the table. Ren Jinhuan looked up at Jin Xiangtang, who was staring at him expressionlessly, his deep gaze utterly unruffled. Ren Jinhuan suddenly realized that when Jin Xiangtang showed no emotion, his face was actually cold and sharp.

That was probably where things started to take a turn. After receiving his new cards, Ren Jinhuan took a sip of tea and angled the cup’s handle toward the six o’clock position—a signal indicating a pair of sixes. Yet, right up until the betting round, Jin Xiangtang didn’t give any signal at all.

After Li-jie reminded him once more, Ren Jinhuan had no choice but to call for the time being, only to fold on the turn. Under the soft, diffused lighting, he leaned back in his chair to glance at his accomplice, who gave no response—merely focused on his own hand, playing with ease, yet cold and heartless. It would be a lie to say he wasn’t nervous; this sense of being caught off guard was deeply unsettling. He felt like a lost goat, led to the edge of a cliff and then abandoned.

After a few rounds, Ren Jinhuan basically called once and then folded, minimizing his losses. Deputy General Manager Yu remarked leisurely, “Jinhuan, you need to pay a little more attention. If you keep this up, you’ll lose a month’s salary…” Qin Hengwen chuckled gently, “You shouldn’t drink too much tea while playing cards—‘tea’ (茶, chá) sounds like ‘bad’ (差, chà). You’ve drunk away your luck.” Shen Feng teased, “You should blame Wen Yan—his bad luck has rubbed off on you.”

As the others exchanged a few words, Ren Jinhuan felt his heart racing. He put on a calm front, but deep down he felt somewhat mocked; he racked his brain for a way out but found none. Just as he was at his wits’ end, Jin Xiangtang gave the table a light tap, signaling a “check.”

Ren Jinhuan froze, then was quickly overcome with joy as if he’d been given a new lease on life. Just as before, whatever Jin Xiangtang hinted at, he did. People who’ve weathered the storm often cherish the lifeline that saves them all the more, However, fairness has never been a concept at the gambling table—it is a world of sharp blades and treachery. He had revealed his hand to Jin Xiangtang from the very beginning, so that by the time he went “all in” at the other man’s direction, he was completely unaware that the cliff beneath his feet was already ten thousand zhang high.

A storm was brewing; Jin Xiangtang also went all-in. The cards were revealed—Ren Jinhuan had 7, 8, 9, 10, J, a straight, while Jin Xiangtang had “three 10s” and “a pair of Jacks,” a full house.

The outcome was clear.

Jin Xiangtang smiled at him and said casually, “You’re out.”
