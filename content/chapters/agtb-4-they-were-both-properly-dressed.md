The week flew by. On Sunday evening, Wen Yan arrived at the entrance of Ren Jinhuan’s apartment complex and drove him to a Cantonese restaurant, the same place where the senior executives had previously gathered.

The restaurant was tucked away deep in a cultural alley and had been in business for many years. Both the owner and the staff were from Guangdong, and the authentic Cantonese cuisine they served set the restaurant apart in this northern city.

Vice President Yu from the Research & Development Center had reserved a private room. He was good friends with the owner and was one of their regulars. When Wen Yan led Ren Jinhuan inside, two other people were already there: the heads of the Marketing & Branding Department and the Strategic Investment Department. Wen Yan introduced Ren Jinhuan to the senior executives. Vice President Yu had a favorable impression of him from previous project collaborations. As for the other two, though they had had no prior interactions with him, they’d heard Ren Jinhuan’s name within the company. After all, rising to become a team leader within two years was considered an exceptional and rare achievement.

Vice President Yu was soon to be appointed CTO, and since he was the host of the gathering, his seat was positioned at the head of the table. Shen Feng, head of the Marketing & Branding Department and one of the company’s first ten employees—a true veteran—sat to his right. Clair Zhang, head of the Strategic Investment Department and also Shi Lu’s boss, was a Chinese-American who had joined Aladdin as a consultant. He sat to Vice President Yu’s left. However, a seat remained empty in the center, seemingly reserved for someone in particular. Since Wen Yan and Shen Feng attended the same university, Wen Yan took Ren Jinhuan and sat below Shen.

The executives began discussing their children’s education and the international situation, touching only briefly on work-related topics. When the conversation turned to some new entertainment trends, they would occasionally ask Ren Jinhuan for his opinion. Ren Jinhuan, however, focused most of his attention on Wen Yan. At high-level dinners like this, it was generally understood that the least-senior subordinate would handle the minor service tasks. Wen Yan would discreetly give him a nod, signaling him to ask the waitstaff to refill tea, replenish snacks, and adjust the room temperature.

Twenty minutes later, the conversation turned to digital cameras. Vice President Yu sighed, admitting he was getting old. No matter how expensive the equipment, it couldn’t make him look as young as he did in his prime. Then, changing the subject, he said to the group, “I noticed that Jinhuan looked quite good in that Today article.”

Vice President Yu spoke casually, but Ren Jinhuan couldn’t help but sit up straighter, while Clair Zhang teased, “Of course someone in their twenties looks better than us middle-aged men.” Wen Yan similarly chimed in at just the right moment, “Teacher Yu has a youthful mindset.”

But the conversation didn’t end there. Clair Zhang suddenly turned to Shen Feng across the table and asked, “Mr. Shen, did we purchase that Today article?”

Shen Feng took a sip of tea and replied slowly, “We only commissioned a few outlets to publish it. As for other media outlets…although they usually write plenty of negative pieces, the overseas developments are currently a hot topic, so I imagine they all wanted to jump on the bandwagon. Let’s just consider it a win-win situation. Positive content from Today is notoriously hard to secure in this industry, and Jinhuan performed quite well that day. I suspect it might have been an ‘organic’ piece.” After speaking, he gave Ren Jinhuan a warm smile, and Clair Zhang also cast a meaningful glance his way with a smile.

Ren Jinhuan had once heard quite a famous story about Mr. Shen Feng from the Branding Department—before entering the industry, Shen Feng had worked for many years at television stations and news agencies, where he held a very high position. It was precisely because of these connections that Director Lin had invited him on board early on to oversee public and government relations. Once, when a reporter asked about the origin of the name “Aladdin,” Director Lin explained: “At first, we planned to find a name starting with ‘A,’ but nothing seemed quite right. Then one day, my daughter’s international private elementary school held an English storytelling contest. She told the story of Aladdin and won first-place, so we adopted that name for good luck.” Before the report was published, Shen Feng keenly sensed the potential risks in this story. At the time, to expand its user base, the company’s promotional strategy urgently needed to extend from tier-one cities to lower-tier ones. Therefore, he requested that the phrases “international private elementary school” and “English” be removed, and added a heartwarming twist to the story: the daughter expressed that, because her father was so busy with work, she hoped Aladdin’s lamp would grant her wish for her father to come home from work early every day. This story became the version widely known to the public, one that is still talked about to this day, and allowed Aladdin to thrive on its “down-to-earth,” “sentimental,” and “warm-hearted” brand image.

Ren Jinhuan lowered his eyes slightly and replied, “That remark wasn’t my original idea. Earlier, when the company published a series of entrepreneurial biographies, one article discussed how to build a warm-hearted company. At the time, I thought that that particular line was exceptionally well-put. During the Q&A session that day, I was actually quite nervous. I was afraid I wouldn’t express myself precisely, so I subconsciously thought of that line. My experience with public speaking is nowhere near as extensive as that of my peers; I’d say I probably only managed to convey about 70 percent of the impact of what would have been a perfect 100-point statement.”

He spoke with utter sincerity, and Wen Yan gave him an approving look. Just then, Clair Zhang spoke up: “Then you really do have a keen eye for talent. The article you mentioned happens to be Teacher Shen Feng’s interview transcript.”

“So that’s how it is…” Ren Jinhuan feigned surprise and ignorance, but he knew full well that the biography had been compiled from oral accounts provided by numerous senior executives at the company, and he naturally recognized that his remarks had come from Shen Feng. Adhering to the principle of “a gift for a gift,” since Teacher Shen had deliberately dropped this hint, he naturally had to tactfully and gracefully pick it up.

As for Clair Zhang, Ren Jinhuan had heard from Shi Lu that he was a child prodigy with the maturity of an adult—someone who had become a tenured professor in the U.S. by the age of 30. Seeing the back-and-forth between Teacher Shen and Ren Jinhuan, he graciously stepped in as a mediator, doing both of them a favor with a single remark.

Shen Feng nodded and said, “Actually, a 70 is already pretty good. It’s just that, from an advertising perspective, moving from a 50 to a 70 is easier for users to accept. But going from a 70 to a 90 is a different story—users often develop a rebellious mindset. People, after all, can’t stand it when the masses create idols. So as long as you know where you stand, just take the rest as idle chatter. There’s nothing much to worry about.”

Ren Jinhuan listened silently. It seemed Shen Feng had had that post from Monday morning deleted.

Vice President Yu then clicked his tongue and said, “Old Shen, let’s not bring your professional matters into our lunch today.” As everyone laughed in deference, Wen Yan checked the time and asked, “Is Teacher Qin Heng from the Industrial Research Department coming today?”

Hearing Teacher Qin’s name mentioned, Ren Jinhuan couldn’t help but take notice. “He’ll be here soon. He said there’s a bit of traffic,” Vice President Yu replied, then added a hint of mystery, “Mr. Qin is bringing someone with him today—a very special person.”

The group was puzzled and asked who it was, to which Vice President Yu replied, “You’ll find out when he arrives.”

A moment later, a server knocked on the door, announced that the last two guests had arrived, and ushered them in. Everyone stood up to greet them alongside Vice President Yu, but when Ren Jinhuan turned and saw the figure standing next to Mr. Qin, he froze in surprise.

“Sorry we’re late,” Qin Heng said in a friendly tone. “Let me introduce you to Jin Xiangtang, who’s set to officially join us tomorrow as the Head of Overseas Production & Logistics. He’s here early to get to know everyone.”

The man being introduced greeted everyone warmly, stepping forward to shake hands with each person in turn. When he reached Ren Jinhuan, he first lowered his gaze, revealing a barely perceptible smile, then let his eyes sweep up from his feet to his face, like a soft brush gliding over the surface of blue-and-white porcelain. He extended his hand and said, “Hello. Nice to meet you.”

Ren Jinhuan fixed his gaze on him—yet another unexpected encounter, though this time, they were both fully dressed.
