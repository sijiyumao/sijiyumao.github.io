Ling Suyu’s pentakill comeback not only stunned the commentators and viewers, but even his teammates couldn’t help but gasp in amazement.

“Pentakill! Aaaah!” Dou Jiayan shouted the loudest, his voice drowning out the game’s system sounds. “Yumao’s the best! Long live Yumao!!”

“Just one word: cool,” Yu He said happily as he lay on the ground.

“That play was great! I feel like we’ve got a chance!” Mo Xiaoyun said.

“What do you mean, ‘we’ve got a chance’!” Tang Yu’an retorted. “Let’s just win this in one go!”

Since igame had been wiped out, the three survivors, though low on health, weren’t in a rush to return to base. Instead, they pushed the mid-lane minion wave, took down igame’s first mid-lane tower, and heavily damaged the second tower. They didn’t return to base to replenish their health until the minion wave had completely cleared.

This team fight completely flipped the economic balance between the two sides. Although Tang Yu’an, the support, still had lower gold than the enemy support due to dying too many times in this game, their two carries, Ling Suyu and Dou Jiayan, now had the highest gold in the entire game!

The opposing team’s top-earning jungler, Child of Frost, was still one minor item behind Dou Jiayan—the advantage they’d built through early-game counter-jungling had vanished completely.

Looking at the current gold standings, Fight felt a sense of vindication. Although the match wasn’t over yet, they couldn’t imagine a scenario where they’d lose.

After all, they had Ling Suyu.

After fighting alongside him for several days, no one knew their new carry better than they did. In real life, he was like an innocent little white rabbit who needed everyone’s careful protection, but once in the game, he turned into a wolf.

Before gaining an economic advantage, he was a wolf cub, cleverly biding his time and quietly building up his gold.

But once he’d secured that economic lead, he pounced on the enemy like a hungry wolf, never giving them a moment’s respite!

As his teammates, they only had two things to do.

First, protect him carefully before he’s fully developed, just as you would guard a treasure.

Second, once he was fully developed, follow in his footsteps, transform into wolves alongside him, and pounce on the enemy, pinning them to the ground!

Sure enough, after this comeback against the odds, igame’s gold lead never surpassed Fight’s again, and Fight emerged victorious in several team fights.

Once fully developed, Desert Hunter exuded an intimidating aura that made him nearly unapproachable. His agile movement made him impossible for the enemy jungler to catch, and his terrifying auto-attack damage struck fear into the hearts of the backliners.

Even the top laner who faced him ended up getting solo-killed in a 1v1.

But Fight wouldn’t give Ling Suyu a chance for a 1v1. Not only did Tang Yu’an guard him constantly like a knight, but his other teammates also shielded him at all times.

Together, they formed a powerful and impenetrable shield, carefully protecting him within its confines.

And he, in turn, channeled their protection into power, delivering the most lethal blows to the enemy with the most devastating damage!

Although igame fought desperately to save the game, clearing wave after wave of minions and repeatedly halting Fight’s relentless advance, at the 23-minute mark, Fight, having secured the Divine Sunbird twice in a row, activated the Hero’s Statue. igame managed to steal the Hero’s Statue, only to suffer a team wipe shortly after.

A team wipe at this stage was devastating, because the respawn time was so long—long enough for Fight to push down their main tower in the meantime.

“It’s going to be a decisive push!” ZhiZhi announced, “Fight is definitely going to pull off a decisive push!”

In fact, it wasn’t just her. Even the viewers with the poorest game sense could tell that igame was completely finished in this push.

Fight collectively pushed the mid-lane minions all the way to the enemy’s inner tower, and the tower’s health was wiped out by Desert Hunter’s terrifying auto-attack damage.

The word “WIN” appeared on everyone’s screens on Fight’s side as the commentators’ excited voices and the audience’s cheers rose and fell in unison.

【Fight staged a comeback against the odds and secured the victory in the first round!】

【Feather’s Desert Hunter was the ace of this game!】

“Fight is awesome!! Feather is awesome!!”

“That pentakill was so f*cking cool!!”

“Against Feather’s Desert Hunter, burst-damage marksmen don’t stand a chance!”

Backstage in the viewing room, Chen Jie pumped his fist in celebration, feeling genuine pride and joy at Fight’s victory. Although he couldn’t take to the field himself, he felt more convinced than ever that his decision to retire immediately had been the right one.

He had poured so much emotion and effort into Fight; as long as it meant Fight could win the championship, he’d do anything.

On the other side, the UH starters, who were also watching the match, all had absolutely horrible expressions on their faces.

They opened their mouths slightly, wanting to say something, but couldn’t get a single word out.

—Who let him use Desert Hunter! If he’d just properly stolen Guardian Spirit, would he be playing so miserably?

He indeed picked Desert Hunter, and indeed went up against Guardian Spirit. Not only did he pick it, but he dominated the fight. Not only did he dominate, he completely crushed his opponent!

—Being pampered like this by his teammates, wouldn’t a pentakill be the least he could do

And he really did get a pentakill, even while his team was behind in gold. He used that pentakill to turn the tide against the odds and help Fight secure the victory!

Their words were like a stream of toxic jinxes—they’d actually managed to “jinx” Fight back to life despite their obvious early-game disadvantage, and they’d somehow turned a match that seemed impossible to win into a victory.

And this victory turned every taunt they’d hurled at Ling Suyu into a slap in their own faces. The louder the crowd cheered for Fight, the more it stung.

“Still not willing to admit it?”

Jiang Hao, who had been silent the whole time, suddenly spoke up: “Feather is really strong.”

His teammates stared at him in shock, as if they couldn’t believe he would say such a thing.

Jiang Hao had anticipated their reaction. He sighed inwardly, suppressed a vague sense of restlessness deep within, and spoke the truth: “He’s just as strong as I am; it’s just that our team’s style doesn’t suit him.”

“Our style doesn’t suit him”… That kind of talk sounded like something you’d say to placate a child.

There was no such thing as a style not suiting a player. There were only teammates who didn’t suit a player.

After all, playstyles can be changed through training, but teammates weren’t so easy to replace.

But what could he do? Jiang Hao certainly couldn’t say “his skills aren’t any worse than mine, but you guys aren’t skilled enough to support him”.

UH’s strengths and weaknesses are both all too obvious.

Their strength was that each player was highly skilled, and their gameplay remained consistently above average.

Their weakness was that, aside from Jiang Hao, everyone else lacked team spirit, and their game awareness varied widely—each player had their own ideas.

It wasn’t easy to bring this group together.

Jiang Hao used to quite enjoy the feeling of being admired and supported by this group, but after watching the match between Fight and igame, he had to admit that he was a bit envious of Ling Suyu’s teammates.

A team with exceptional teamwork, where every member voluntarily protected the marksman—what marksman wouldn’t dream of being part of that?

Perhaps because of their unconditional admiration for Jiang Hao, even though his earlier remark sounded like he was placating a child, his teammates accepted it very easily and even added their own thoughts.

“Yeah, Hao-ge has always played burst-damage marksmen, and that’s what we’re used to.”

“He’s only this strong because he had the gold lead—it has nothing to do with which hero he chose. If Hao-ge were given the gold lead, would there even be any room left for that guy to show off?”

“Why even compare him to Hao-ge? How can he possibly measure up? Hao-ge is definitely stronger.” Jungler Cui Rao said this, then suddenly blanked for a moment.

Was that really the case?

At some point, flattering Jiang Hao had become a habit for him.

Jiang Hao was the captain, the strategist, and their damage dealer. They were more than willing to pave the way for him.

But as fellow marksmen, how had they treated Ling Suyu?

Apart from the support, no one was willing to take damage for him, and even the support would stop paying attention to him in the late game.

Because of poor leadership, the team’s rhythm fell apart, and then they turned around and berated Ling Suyu for not keeping up.

Even when the coach pointed this out during the post-game reviews—though the coach also addressed Ling Suyu’s issues and criticized him for not keeping up—they still believed they were right to berate him.

But look at Fight’s match against igame, and look at the teammates on Fight. Every push was timed perfectly; every team fight, whether in terms of timing or angle of engagement, was flawless. Everyone kept up, and Ling Suyu followed without hesitation.

Cui Rao recalled threatening Ling Suyu, saying he’d lock him in the bathroom and beat him up if he dared to feed again, and for some reason, he felt a sudden pang of shame for no apparent reason.

To be fair, whenever he set the right pace, was there ever a time Ling Suyu didn’t follow through?

But when Jiang Hao was on the field, he didn’t have to deal with this problem at all, because Jiang Hao would always remind him when to step in and when to hold back, whereas Ling Suyu would just sit there with a stern, silent expression.

Cui Rao knew that as long as he wanted to play esports, he couldn’t do without Jiang Hao, but Ling Suyu…

Just thinking about that person now actually made his chest feel a little tight.

Compared to him, who was a bit more sensitive to the flow of the game, the rest of UH didn’t think too deeply.

The top laner was a die-hard fan of Jiang Hao, because Jiang Hao never let him down, whereas Ling Suyu routinely backstabbed him.

The mid laner had always looked up to Jiang Hao, because when Jiang Hao was on the field, he could clearly feel that the team was dealing damage, unlike when Ling Suyu was on the field, when they couldn’t kill the enemy no matter what and instead kept getting crushed by them.

Although the support player wasn’t much of a talker, he’d settled on Jiang Hao early on. With Jiang Hao around, he’d simply do whatever Jiang Hao told him to do. Once he got used to it, he couldn’t be bothered to think for himself. In contrast, playing support for Ling Suyu was a real headache for him, because he could never figure out what Ling Suyu was thinking. Since Ling Suyu didn’t say anything, he didn’t ask either, and their bot lane interactions were as awkward as a first blind date with a stranger.

“I…need to use the washroom,” Cui Rao said, suddenly leaving the room.

He couldn’t stand the MVP close-up animation on the screen—it was so glaring it felt like it was going to blind him.

The commentators’ voices were also driving him absolutely crazy.

【The referees have awarded this game’s MVP to Feather! I don’t think there’s much debate here—Feather’s pentakill comeback directly decided the outcome of this match…】

As Cui Rao walked away, ZhiZhi’s voice grew fainter and fainter.

With mixed feelings, he rushed toward the restroom, but he’d forgotten that he was still at the competition venue, and Fight had just finished a match and was taking a break.

So, the moment he stepped into the restroom, unexpectedly—yet not at all surprisingly—he bumped into a familiar figure.

“Uh, sorry.” Ling Suyu bumped into someone as he was coming out and apologized almost instinctively. Only after apologizing did he get a clear look… This person seemed somewhat familiar?

Cui Rao stared blankly at Ling Suyu, whom he’d just bumped into, his eyes wide with disbelief, as if he’d seen a ghost.

Startled by that look, Ling Suyu instinctively took a step back. The unease on his face vanished, replaced by an icy expression.

The two stared at each other like that for a second—or was it two?

It felt as though those brief one or two seconds lasted an eternity.

In the end, Ling Suyu was the first to react, sidestepping Cui Rao and walking out.

Although they had once been teammates, he had already severed ties with UH, so there was absolutely no need to say hello.

He’d assumed the other man felt the same way, that he’d pass by just as he had on the day Ling Suyu left UH, as if he hadn’t seen him, as if Ling Suyu were a ghost.

To his utter surprise, just as he successfully sidestepped Cui Rao and headed out, Cui Rao spoke up: “How’s it going at Fight?”

Ling Suyu paused in his tracks: “Pretty good.”

“Oh, that’s good,” Cui Rao replied casually, then picked a random stall and slipped inside. Once inside, he fidgeted, running his fingers through his hair—what was he doing?!

Seeing that Cui Rao had gone in, Ling Suyu didn’t bother with him anymore. He quickly left the restroom and returned to the competition room.

However, once he sat down, he seemed a bit distracted. Chen Jie had to call his name twice before he heard him, and he instinctively blurted out, “Huh?”

Tang Yu’an keenly picked up on the change in his mood and asked tentatively, “What’s wrong? Is something on your mind?”

Thinking about his recent trip to the restroom, he added, “Did you run into someone while you were in there?”

Ling Suyu nodded, then asked in confusion, “Didn’t UH leave after their match?”

Upon hearing this, Tang Yu’an immediately realized what was going on: “Did you run into someone from UH? Are you hurt?”

“Hurt? No, not at all.” Ling Suyu was momentarily taken aback by the question.

—What do you take UH’s members for?

Although the jungler he’d just run into routinely threatened to beat him up, he’d never actually laid a hand on him.

On the contrary, it was Jiang Hao who would get physical to stand up for his teammates.

At the White Fox Cup, Jiang Hao wasn’t suspended for intentionally picking a fight. It was because, at the hotel where they were staying, some bored jerk in the lobby mocked them for playing esports, saying it was a waste of time. Cui Rao retorted a few times, only to have the guy grab his collar and threaten him. Jiang Hao punched the man to rescue Cui Rao. The man, holding a grudge, reported the incident to the White Fox Cup organizers, and then…

Ling Suyu snapped out of his reverie and only then remembered to answer Tang Yu’an’s question: “It was nothing. We just chatted for a bit, there wasn’t any conflict.”

Tang Yu’an locked eyes with him for a moment, confirming he was telling the truth, and only then did he relax. He furrowed his brow slightly: “I guess they wanted to watch our match against igame before leaving, but the organizers actually allowed them to stay on the same floor as us?”

That was really annoying.

“Where’s Ye Xiran?” Tang Yu’an decided to handle this matter.

Thankfully, there hadn’t been any conflict just now. What if Ling Suyu got bullied by UH again without him knowing, and it affected his in-game performance?

“Ye Xiran has a sponsorship deal to discuss; he’s meeting with a client. Leave this to me. I’ll handle it after we finish the post-game review,” Chen Jie said. “For now, let’s focus on the review.”

Tang Yu’an hesitated for a moment, but eventually gave a soft “Mm-hmm” in agreement with Chen Jie’s plan.

But he was itching to kick the entire UH team out of the venue right then and there.

Actually, he hadn’t really had any strong feelings about UH before.

But now, the more he liked Ling Suyu, the more he hated UH.

A team so clueless about treating a lady with respect (wait, that’s not quite right) simply didn’t deserve to be in the same building as Ling Suyu.

Perhaps sensing Tang Yu’an’s thoughts, and also to put Ling Suyu’s mind at ease, Chen Jie wrapped up the debrief quickly and immediately rushed to speak with the venue staff during the last five minutes of the break to report the issue.

After learning about the “feud” between Ling Suyu and UH, the staff member said they would handle the matter as soon as possible.

But before they could resolve it, the second game began.

In fact, once the match started, Ling Suyu forgot all about this minor incident and focused entirely on the game.

This time, however, facing igame with nearly the same lineup, the “miracle” did not happen again.

At the start of the game, Fight was on high alert for an igame counter-jungle, but igame didn’t come.

Until the 6-minute mark, both teams played exceptionally cautiously, with neither side securing a kill. It was clear that igame’s tactics had shifted.

At the 6-minute mark, igame’s mid laner and jungler coordinated to assist the bot lane, launching a fierce gank.

Dou Jiayan arrived a split second too late, and Yu He was nearly instantly killed. igame tower-dived and killed Tang Yu’an, while Ling Suyu escaped safely under his protection. Dou Jiayan then secured the kill on igame’s support before being ganged up on and killed by the rest of igame’s team.

igame pulled off a 1-for-3 trade, creating the first economic gap between the two teams.

Immediately afterward, igame secured the Divine Sunbird and used it to take down Fight’s bottom lane first tower.

With the first tower down, there was no longer any need to defend the bottom lane. Ling Suyu, just like in the previous game, began farming the jungle and pushing the mid and bottom lane minion waves under his teammates’ protection to boost his gold as quickly as possible.

However, igame had clearly discussed their tactics during the post-game review. They completely ignored him and focused on ganking the top and mid lanes, killing Mo Xiaoyun and Dou Jiayan twice each, then dominating the jungle to take down the defenseless Yu He.

Fight’s mid and top lane first towers were also quickly pushed down, further widening the economic gap between the two teams.

To prevent further ganks, Fight was forced to group up and move as a team, but at the 10-minute mark, the Dragon God spawned.

This time, they sent Yu He alone to check it. He entered the pit at the right moment and used his Smite at the right time, but failed to secure the Dragon God and fell with a look of frustration on his face.

The rest of Fight had no choice but to tank three waves of dragon legion attacks.

The downside was that Fight lost two more towers.

The upside was that Ling Suyu’s gold finally caught up thanks to those three waves of dragon legion, allowing him to barely keep up with the enemy marksman.

However, at the 16-minute mark, Yu He was killed while solo pushing the top lane. Fight thought they could seize the opportunity to take down the second Divine Sunbird, but although they managed to secure it, they suffered a 1-for-3 exchange, with only Ling Suyu escaping on low health thanks to two dashes and a Flash.

On Fight’s side, everyone except Ling Suyu was down, so naturally, no one was left to take  the Divine Sunbird.

igame seized the opportunity to take down two more of Fight’s towers.

This play directly caused Ling Suyu’s gold to fall behind the enemy marksman once again, and he was unable to catch up for the rest of the game.

In the end, after 21 minutes of play, igame evened the points with a decisive economic advantage.

Tang Yu’an knew they lost this game because igame had adjusted their tactics. Although they had initially collapsed their bot lane, they subsequently focused on targeting their economically disadvantaged top and mid lanes, denying them any opportunity to protect Ling Suyu and let him farm—and they were powerless to stop it.

—It had nothing to do with Ling Suyu’s mindset.

However, as soon as the match ended, he immediately asked Chen Jie, “Did we clear out the UH players?”

“They’ve been chased into another building. Don’t worry, even if you guys were dancing in the hallway right now, UH’s players wouldn’t hear a thing.” Chen Jie replied, then suddenly turned to Ling Suyu and, without warning, said, “Let’s go with Cat Spirit for the next game.”

Ling Suyu was taken aback for a moment.

“That’s what I was thinking, too,” Tang Yu’an said, raising a hand to rest it on Ling Suyu’s head and softening his voice. “Don’t overthink it. It’s not that you played Desert Hunter poorly, it’s just that igame is too strong. They’re so strong that we couldn’t hold out long enough for you to get going, so we have to change up our lineup. Desert Hunter is too late-game, and compared to Cat Spirit, he relies more heavily on his ultimate. If we want to beat igame, we really do need to use Cat Spirit.”

“Yeah, that’s exactly it.” Chen Jie nodded in agreement with Tang Yu’an’s explanation and added, “igame is exceptionally strong this season, maybe even stronger than YJ. They’re truly ready to win the championship, so we have to take them seriously.”

Ling Suyu pursed his lips and said nothing.

Even though they said that, he knew this match would end in defeat, and it was inextricably linked to his choice of Desert Hunter as his hero.

Although he was confident he could pull off another comeback if his teammates held out just a little longer, igame wouldn’t have given him that chance.

When he took off his headset, he’d already braced himself for criticism, and had even drafted a lengthy self-reflection in his mind, but in the end…

Why were all the Fight members so gentle?

It wasn’t just Tang Yu’an—the others were the same.

“Feather, you’re not blaming yourself again, are you? No way, no way!” Dou Jiayan exclaimed in surprise. “I died eight times and I haven’t even said anything yet!”

Chen Jie smacked him right on the head: “You’ve actually got the nerve to say that! I was seriously starting to suspect you were a mole sent by the enemy team!”

“I screwed us over this round,” Mo Xiaoyun said self-reproachfully. “I shouldn’t have initiated that last team fight.”

“I didn’t screw us over, I did my best,” Yu He said, sticking out his chin. “I think.”

Tang Yu’an couldn’t help but chuckle as he withdrew his hand from Ling Suyu’s head: “Victory is everyone’s glory, and defeat is everyone’s fault—not a single person can escape blame. Instead of beating ourselves up now, let’s review the game thoroughly, learn from our mistakes, and win it back in the next match!”

Dou Jiayan: “Mm-hmm!”

Yu He: “Okay.”

Mo Xiaoyun: “You’re right.”

Ling Suyu blinked, watching Fight who, far from being downcast after losing the match, seemed unusually hyped up. The corners of his lips curled up involuntarily. Then, he took a deep breath, cleared his mind of all distractions, and said earnestly, “Next game, I’ll carry you! My Cat Spirit is super awesome!”
