It wasn’t actually that big of a deal for members of the same team to drink water from the same cup.

Dou Jiayan often forgot his water bottle, and since he didn’t want to drink the water provided by the league, he’d drink from Chen Jie’s cup.

Yu He also occasionally forgot his water bottle and drank from Chen Jie’s cup.

In the case that Tang Yu’an forgot his water bottle…

Chen Jie: “Get lost.”

First, there was Ling Suyu’s interview footage where he explicitly said he likes “girlfriends”

just like their captain.

On top of that, both of them were incredibly handsome.

Erm, that seemed like the real point here.

When one handsome guy drinks from another handsome guy’s cup, no matter how natural the gesture or how pure his intentions, it’s automatically viewed through a romantic lens.

After hydrating, Tang Yu’an went to the restroom to splash some water on his face.

As he was washing his face, Chen Jie emerged from a stall after using the restroom.

Spotting him, Chen Jie pushed open the doors to the other stalls to make sure no one else was in the restroom besides them. Only then did he speak in a low voice: “The match isn’t over yet. I’m not trying to antagonize you, but you need to be careful to reign it in.”

Tang Yu’an raised an eyebrow, stepping aside from the sink as he replied, “Are you saying, what if Yumao is straight?”

Chen Jie took the spot Tang Yu’an had been standing, washing his hands without turning around as he said, “Don’t get it backwards. The actual ‘what if’ scenario is Yumao actually being gay.”

Tang Yu’an chuckled softly, showing no sign of being discouraged by the remark.

“Don’t worry, I know my limits. I won’t do anything that makes Yumao uncomfortable.”

He couldn’t be 100% certain that Ling Suyu was someone he could pursue.

But he was 100% certain that Ling Suyu didn’t mind his closeness, or even further intimacy.

If he didn’t even have that much intuition, he wouldn’t be a qualified leader.

A competent leader would never make any decisions that would make those under his leadership feel uncomfortable.

This applied to Ling Suyu, and it applied to Chen Jie as well.

Tang Yu’an patted Chen Jie on the shoulder: “I’ve said it before: competition first, personal feelings second. After you’ve made such a huge sacrifice for Fight, how could I possibly let you down? I’ll work even harder, including for your sake, to win the championship for Fight.”

Tang Yu’an’s tone was very serious as he said this.

After hearing this, Chen Jie opened his mouth, but the words he’d originally intended to say died in his throat.

After all, there was no need to say anything.

He let out a breath he’d been holding and murmured ‘m-hmm.’ “I believe in you.”

Tang Yu’an nodded, patted his shoulder once more, and left the restroom first.

Indeed, a competent leader is someone who puts the minds of everyone under his leadership at ease. √ Chen Jie watched his retreating figure, his mind echoing with those earnest and resolute words. He did feel much more at ease, but…

—I won’t do anything that makes Yumao uncomfortable.

Wait a minute?

So was Yumao actually comfortable with the way Tang Yu’an had been gradually pushing boundaries?

After following Tang Yu’an back to the competition room, Chen Jie immediately gathered the entire team for a quick debrief of the match they’d just played.

Although, just like in previous matches, Ling Suyu had secured an economic advantage early on and practically carried the entire team single-handedly, Dou Jiayan had also managed to stay alive under Ling Suyu’s shadow without making any major mistakes.

But the problems were also glaringly obvious.

“Yanzi, you didn’t even land your ultimate,” Chen Jie couldn’t help but frown. “OsO held off the first wave of the dragon legion but couldn’t hold the second. If they’d held both waves, their marksman would’ve leveled up and gained damage, and on our side we wouldn’t have been able to win team fights relying solely on Yumao. You’ve got to step up.”

“Haah, I know, but I hadn’t even developed yet,” Dou Jiayan said, sounding aggrieved. “By the time they were fully leveled up, I should’ve been there too. Once I got my skill damage boost and critical strike chance, I’d have blown them away!”

“That’s not the problem,” Tang Yu’an said. “The problem is—Yanzi, you’re being targeted.”

“Huh?” Dou Jiayan looked completely bewildered. “Why? Why would they target me instead of Yumao?”

Chen Jie picked up where Tang Yu’an left off: “That was our first game using Witch and Artillery. The enemy didn’t catch on at first, but now that they’re reviewing the match, they’ve definitely spotted the problem—and it’s not just OsO; other teams have probably noticed it too. Yumao’s Witch is very strong, but your Artillery has major issues. Originally, they’d been focusing on our bottom lane to stifle Yumao’s economy, but now that they’ve realized they can’t stop Yumao, with your obvious weakness, guess how they’ll play against us if we use this comp again?”

Dou Jiayan finally caught on: “Crush my mid lane?”

“Right,” Chen Jie nodded. “So, until you’ve improved your Mobile Artillery, we can’t use this lineup anymore.”

“Then what composition should we use next game?” Dou Jiayan grew anxious. “Should we have Feather pick Cat Spirit?”

“We won’t need that against OsO for now. For the next game, we’ll choose one of two strategies.” Tang Yu’an held up two fingers, then lowered one. “Either both carries pick mages and focus on the early game, betting we can push through by then, or… I’ll take Mobile Artillery.”

Ling Suyu: ?!

Other teammates: !!

Soon, the 15-minute break ended, and the players from Fight and OsO returned to their computers.

The computers at the venue were provided by the organizers, but the players had brought their own keyboards and mice to plug in.

After all, different keyboards and mice felt different, which could have a certain impact on a player’s performance.

Although Ling Suyu wasn’t particularly picky about keyboards and mice, using the ones he was accustomed to gave him a greater sense of security. The psychological impact outweighed the difference in feel.

Once both teams were ready, the second game officially began.

Unlike the first game, where the ban order was determined by dice roll, in the second game, the winner of the previous game got to choose the ban slot first, while the loser got to pick their hero first.

For the first ban slot, Fight’s team banned Twin Demonblades.

Then it was OsO’s turn; without a moment’s hesitation, they immediately banned Desert Hunter.

“Alright, OsO has once again given their first ban to Desert Hunter!” Ah-Mu commented. “It looks like Desert Hunter won’t be making an appearance in today’s match between Fight and OsO, that’s a bit of a shame!”

For the second ban, Fight didn’t hesitate much either, banning Tempest Blade.

“Huh? Fight has banned two junglers in a row, are they that worried OsO will snag a jungler?

Doesn’t that amount to acknowledging OsO’s jungler’s skill?” ZhiZhi asked in surprise.

Actually, not really. From Fight’s perspective, OsO’s jungler was just average. Not that his skills were poor; after all, even the worst pro players weren’t bad.

It was just that, so far, none of OsO’s five positions had given Fight the same unstoppable sense of pressure that Feather had.

The reason for not giving them these two agile, high-damage junglers was mainly because they suspected they might target the mid lane in this game, and they wanted to minimize the possibility of them tower-diving and instantly taking out their mid laner.

Then, OsO’s second ban slot went to Harp Demon.

“OsO has banned a mid laner!” Ah-Mu commented. “I wonder who they’ll ban with their last slot—will they ban Feather’s Candy Witch?”

“Feather’s Candy Witch is way too scary—if it were me, I’d definitely ban her!” ZhiZhi said.

“Not many players practice Witch or Artillery, so it’s unlikely OsO will pick them. That’s why I’m guessing their final ban slot is reserved for Candy Witch!”

Just as she said that, Fight banned Beast Master.

Then, after a somewhat lengthy hesitation, OsO used their final ban slot on Realm God.

“Huh?!” ZhiZhi, caught off guard, let out a cry of disbelief.

Ah-Mu also exclaimed in surprise, “They’re not banning Witch? OsO actually didn’t ban Witch! Could it be that they already have a strategy to counter this lineup?”

【OsO has first pick this game. Let’s see who they’ll choose!】

Under the watchful eyes of the commentators, Fight, and all the viewers watching the livestream, OsO selected their first champion—Blade Soul.

“It’s Blade Soul! But is this for the jungle or top lane?” Ah-Mu wasn’t sure—after all, OsO had used Blade Soul in the top lane during the spring qualifiers and even defeated XY, one of the former quarterfinalists.

Then, on Fight’s side, the system granted them the right to pick two heroes in a row. They first locked in Black-Armoured Scythe for top lane, and a moment later, locked in Phoenix Soul for mid lane.

Actually, Mo Xiaoyun’s main pick was typically Skeletal Empress. This champion could tank damage, provide crowd control, resurrect allies, and deal damage, making her a dominant top laner in this season’s meta.

In the past, whenever he had the chance to pick Skeletal Empress, he would always take her, but in this game— Tang Yu’an: “We’ve got enough damage. Pick someone who can tank better, like Black-Armoured Scythe.”

Mo Xiaoyun: “Alright.”

So they locked in Black-Armoured Scythe without hesitation.

As for how they chose Phoenix Soul— Dou Jiayan: “Give me Phoenix Soul! I’m gonna carry this game!”

Tang Yu’an: “You’re not carrying anyone. Pick something with crowd control—Wind Guardian.”

Dou Jiayan: “Fine, but what if the enemy has been practicing with Phoenix Soul?”

Tang Yu’an, after a moment’s thought: “Phoenix Soul is fine too.”

Dou Jiayan: “Yay—”

Seeing that Fight had locked in Phoenix Soul, both the commentators and OsO were surprised.

Ah-Mu: “Huh? Is Fight not using Witch or Artillery this round?”

ZhiZhi: “OsO didn’t ban them, and Fight didn’t pick them either. Maybe both sides have other plans?”

OsO’s coach: “Phoenix Soul… let’s go with Deepsea Faerie. Since the opponent isn’t picking Artillery, let’s give Blade Soul to the jungler. We’ll finalize the bot lane composition first and lock in Netherworld Shadow and Mandara Vines.”

And thus, OsO locked in Netherworld Shadow and Mandara Vines.

Then it was Fight’s turn again. Without a moment’s hesitation, Fight instantly locked in Candy Witch and Mobile Artillery!

“Huh?!” the commentators exclaimed in unison, “After Fight picked Phoenix Soul, they locked in Witch and Artillery?!”

“Things are taking a strange turn!” Ah-Mu’s expression gradually turned Ah-Mused. “The last time I encountered a situation where I had no idea which hero was meant for whom was when I was commentating on an SMG match. But with SMG, not only do they mix up hero positions, but the players’ roles often get mixed up too—the jungler goes mid, the mid laner goes bot. I wonder what’s going on with Fight this time?”

Seeing Fight’s lineup, the commentators and viewers were simply surprised and curious, but OsO was completely flustered and at a loss.

“Damn! So who’s their mid laner? Phoenix Soul? Or Mobile Artillery? Even Candy Witch could go mid…”

“If Phoenix Soul goes mid, they’d have to pick Deepsea Faerie, but if it’s Mobile Artillery or Candy Witch going mid, they’d have no choice but to use Wind Guardian.”

“Not a single one of those heroes looks like a support! Even though Fight’s captain often acts strange, having a hero pool this deep is just ridiculous!”

“Forget it, let’s play it safe, go Deepsea Faerie and Venomous Scorpion. They’re low-risk picks!”

In the end, the lineups chosen by both teams were:

Fight: Black-Armoured Scythe, Phoenix Soul, Candy Witch, Mobile Artillery, Double Phantom; OsO: Blade Soul, Deepsea Faerie, Netherworld Shadow, Mandara Vines, Venomous Scorpion; Then, under everyone’s curious gaze, both teams assigned the heroes to their respective players.

On Fight’s side, Dou Jiayan ended up with Phoenix Soul, Ling Suyu with Candy Witch, and Tang Yuan with Mobile Artillery.

“Mobile Artillery as support!” It wasn’t until both teams entered the game with this lineup that commentator Ah-Mu dared to confirm, “Fight’s mid laner and marksman are both magic-damage dealers. To balance the damage types, Fight’s support picked an AD! This lineup is quite squishy, and OsO’s composition is specifically designed to counter squishy teams! I’m really curious to see what kind of sparks will fly between these two teams in this match!”

Tang Yu'an’s hero pool had always been a mystery.

Fight’s team members were already used to seeing him play support with champions meant for other roles.

After training together for a few days, Ling Suyu had also gained a general understanding of Tang Yu'an’s hero pool.

But as far as he could recall, Tang Yu'an had played top lane, mid lane, and even jungle support. He’d just never played marksman.

What happened to not being able to play marksman?

Ling Suyu held back for a while, but finally couldn’t resist asking, “How come you can even play marksman?”

He didn’t realize his tone carried a hint of accusation, as if he were saying—did you lie to me before?

Picking up on that implication, Tang Yu’an couldn’t help but chuckle. “What a coincidence, this is the only marksman I know how to play.”

Ling Suyu blinked, still sceptical.

Tang Yu’an explained, “I told you before, right? Before I went pro, I played every position.

Even though I’m not great at playing marksmen, what if I ended up with teammates who didn’t know how to play one? So, to climb the ranks, no matter how bad I was at it, I forced myself to practice one—just this one, Mobile Artillery. If he gets banned, it’s game over.

Luckily, he’s not a particularly strong hero, less likely to get banned compared to Blade Soul.”

Blade Soul was a jungler who was often played in the top or mid lanes.

As a jungler, his damage wasn’t high enough.

As a top laner, his defense wasn’t high enough.

But on the flip side— As a jungler, he had excellent survival skills.

As a top laner, he had excellent damage output.

So, for the average player, Blade Soul couldn’t cut through tanks and often got taken down by a mage’s combo in a single hit.

But for those who could truly master Blade Soul, they could tower-dive and instantly take down squishies, clear five enemies with one ultimate, farm efficiently in-lane, dominate the jungle, and carry the entire team once they’d built an economic advantage.

Many people didn’t take Blade Soul seriously at first. After all, he wasn’t exactly a meta-dominant hero.

But once they’d reached high-tier matches and were traumatized by someone who’d truly mastered Blade Soul, even knowing the hero’s pick rate wasn’t that high, they’d ban him every single game.

As for Mobile Artillery, whether this hero could utterly crush the enemy depended more on gold than on skill. If played poorly, the hero won’t make an impact all-game; if played well, it’s just so-so—so no one ever banned him.

Ling Suyu nodded, accepting Tang Yu’an’s explanation, but immediately began to worry:

“Did you practice him lately?”

“Nope,” Tang Yu’an said casually.

Ling Suyu: ?!

“But the heroes in Glorious are all similar, only the specific skills are different. Isn’t positioning and stuff still up to the player’s own skill?” Tang Yu’an said. After a brief pause, he added with a smile, “Besides, you’re here, aren’t you?”

Ling Suyu was taken aback.

“I’m the support, you’re the carry. Whether or not we win this game depends entirely on you.

As for me, I’m here to make you shine even brighter. When I play a tank, I take the damage for you; when I play a damage dealer, I help wear down the enemy. Just as my role is support, I’ll do everything in my power to support you and give my absolute best to help you shine. So, as long as you don’t fall apart, I won’t either. Yumao, I believe in you—do you believe in yourself?”

Tang Yu’an’s words grew increasingly earnest and emotional as he spoke.

At first, his tone had clearly been playful, but by the end, it had turned into a sincere question.

By this point, Chen Jie had already taken off his headset and stepped back from the conversation, but the rest of the Fight teammates could all hear what he was saying, and they all wore expressions that said “We’ve had enough.”

“You’re so…” Mo Xiaoyun couldn’t help but complain.

However, he had barely uttered two words when he heard Ling Suyu go, “Mmm.”

Then, perhaps feeling that a one-syllable response to Tang Yu’an was a bit dismissive, Ling Suyu hesitated for a moment before saying earnestly, “I believe in myself. I can carry.”

—I can carry.

Those words had always been buried deep in Ling Suyu’s heart.

In every match, he felt he could carry the team.

Even though he thought it sounded a bit far-fetched when Dou Jiayan said it, he felt the same way himself.

Give me Cat Spirit, and I’ll carry the team.

Give me Desert Hunter, and I’ll carry the team.

No matter what I play, as long as my teammates aren’t too bad, I can carry the team!

—This was likely the pride of a professional player, an affirmation of his own abilities and

self-confidence.

It might have been arrogance, but so what?

His goal was the championship. If he didn’t even have the confidence to win even a single match when he was on-field, what right did he have to reach for such heights?

It wasn’t just now; before joining Fight, even back when he was still with UH, Ling Suyu had always thought this way.

He believed that as long as his UH teammates played to their actual potential, he could carry them to victory.

But he couldn’t bring himself to say it.

Because he knew that once he did, he’d face his teammates’ scepticism and mockery.

And because of that experience at UH, even after joining Fight, he still couldn’t bring himself to say those words—afraid that his teammates in Fight would think he was arrogant, conceited, or overestimating his own abilities.

So, when he heard Dou Jiayan say those words so effortlessly, he found it hard to believe— yet he also felt a touch of envy.

He never expected that at that very moment— Tang Yu’an: “Feather, I believe in you. Do you believe in yourself?”

Ling Suyu: “Mm.”

Ling Suyu: “I believe in myself. I can carry.”

He said it out loud.

Those words, he spoke them for the first time.

After saying them, his heartbeat suddenly became a bit erratic. The vibrations from his heartbeat traveled up his arms all the way to the palms of his hands, possibly from nervousness or excitement.

Once he said those words, Tang Yu’an let out a laugh: “That’s the spirit! That’s exactly the kind of confidence and grit we need from Fight’s center!”

Right after that, Dou Jiayan chimed in: “Awesome! I feel like we’ve got this round in the bag!”

Yu He: “I don’t know why, but when Yanzi says the same thing, it sounds like the foreshadowing of failure. When Yumao says it, however, it’s incredibly convincing.”

Mo Xiaoyun, who had originally planned to take a dig at Tang Yu’an, gave a resigned smile upon hearing Ling Suyu’s response: “Fine, let’s just win this quickly so we can go eat!”

Dou Jiayan: “Let’s do this! Fighting!”
