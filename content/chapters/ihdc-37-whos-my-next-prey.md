After the reporter and the photographer accompanying her politely bid farewell to the Fight members in the lobby, they left the club building.

Before leaving, the reporter gave Ling Suyu a “good luck” gesture before glancing in Tang Yu’an’s direction.

Although it was clearly a gesture meant to encourage him for the Spring Seasonals, that extra glance, combined with the content of the interview, couldn’t help but encourage people to read more into it.

—It was as if she were encouraging Ling Suyu to find his “girlfriend” as soon as possible.

Ling Suyu nodded blankly.

Then, the moment the group left, his face turned red as a persimmon.

“Yumao [^1]…” Chen Jie had barely called out his name when Ling Suyu shot up from the sofa and rushed to the bathroom.

He needed to splash some cold water on his face to calm down!

Chen Jie watched his back, pondered for a moment, then glanced at Tang Yu’an, who hadn’t stopped laughing the whole time, and couldn’t resist elbowing him.

“What?” Tang Yu’an rubbed his shoulder, having been caught off guard by the sudden attack, and shot him an accusing look.

“Yumao said ‘a girl like you.’ You’re not even a girl, so what are you getting all excited for?”

Chen Jie glared at him.

“Hey, hey, hey, did I even say anything?” Tang Yu’an protested, looking wronged.

Chen Jie: “You’ve been laughing nonstop this whole time.”

Tang Yu’an: “Can’t I laugh because I find Yumao cute? You’re overreacting; I just chuckled a few times. It’s not like I did anything I shouldn’t have.”

Chen Jie: “I’m warning you: the Spring Season is about to start. Don’t go messing around, and don’t get any crazy ideas. If you want to pursue Yumao, wait until after the Spring Seasonals are over.”

“I haven’t even tried pursuing him yet, it’s Yumao who’s been captivated by the inherent charm I radiate…”

Before Tang Yu’an could finish his sentence, Chen Jie punched him. Laughing, he dodged the blow: “All right, all right, my bad. I won’t say another word. Hey, watch your hand!”

In the bathroom, Ling Suyu splashed cold water on his face to cool down, but hesitated to step out, afraid he’d have to face his teammates’ teasing the moment he did.

But no things could be avoided forever; you can’t hide from people your whole life.

Finally, after taking a deep breath, he pretended to be calm as he left the bathroom.

As soon as he stepped into the living room, he saw Tang Yu’an sitting on the sofa, waving at him as if nothing had happened: “Come on, Yumao, let’s eat—good thing the interview didn’t take long, or I’d have starved to death.”

“Aren’t you the one that set the time?” Chen Jie grumbled from the side. “You knew full well we were supposed to eat around 12, but you insisted on scheduling it for 11.”

“What could I do? They insisted on interviewing Yumao,” Tang Yu’an replied as he opened his lunch box and casually handed it to Ling Suyu. “Any earlier and he wouldn’t have woken up yet; any later and it would interfere with practice. This timing worked best.”

Ling Suyu took his lunch from Tang Yu’an. Seeing that neither of them intended to bring up the interview, he silently breathed a sigh of relief, then noticed a detail in their conversation:

“Huh? Did you know yesterday that there would be an interview today?”

“Yeah. ” Tang Yu’an replied, “Ye Xiran told me during the break yesterday. I didn’t want it to affect your YJ match, so I didn’t mention it to you. But after the match, I forgot all about it. It wasn’t until I was lying in bed that I remembered, so I had to wait until you woke up today to tell you. I couldn’t exactly go into your room last night and drag you out of bed to tell you, could I?”

Ling Suyu: “……”

For something like this, please do drag me out of bed to tell me!

Soon, the members of Fight woke up one by one and came downstairs to have lunch with Ling Suyu.

They had no idea that while they were still asleep, Ling Suyu had given an official interview, and Ling Suyu wasn’t particularly eager for them to find out either.

Fortunately, neither Tang Yu’an nor Chen Jie brought it up, so the day passed without incident amid their routine training.

However, once the interview video was edited, it was bound to be released.

That evening, the league’s official account posted the interview with Ling Suyu across various platforms.

At first, seeing how sparing Ling Suyu was with his words, the live comments went like this:

【Feather is so aloof.】

【With a personality like that, won’t Feather have a hard time getting along with his

new teammates?】

Then, when the reporter asked how his teammates at Fight compared to the ones at UH, Ling Suyu: “I’m with Fight right now, so I only have teammates from Fight and opponents from UH.”

【Great answer!】

【Pretty cool!】

【Hahaha, that’s Feather for you! I wonder how the UH players will react when they

see this interview?】

Then, the reporter asked if Fight was confident they could win the championship.

Ling Suyu replied very seriously with just two words: “I am.”

His expression was so serious and his tone so firm, it was as if he were taking an oath.

【Haha, why do I suddenly think Feather is kind of cute?】

【Maybe Feather is just looks cold on the surface?】

【You guys think he’s cold, but really, he’s just socially anxious.】

【Damn, socially anxious, so real!】

Finally, the reporter asked Ling Suyu whether or not he had a girlfriend, and what his ideal type was.

Ling Suyu suddenly became very talkative, giving a detailed, lengthy description, and then very naturally blurted out, “Someone like the captain.”

Live comments:

【Holy crap! I’m exploding, turning into fireworks!】

【I’m really watching this interview for free?】

【Talkative, opinionated, decisive, that’s Tang Yu’an to a T! I ship it guys!】

As a result, after this interview video of Ling Suyu was released, its views surpassed those of the previous video in just half an hour, and continued to multiply in the following half-hour!

Ling Suyu, who had already gained some attention outside the esports community due to UH’s “announcement blame-shifting incident,” was already in the spotlight. Now, his popularity skyrocketed at the speed of light.

Thanks to this video, anticipation for the upcoming Spring Seasonals continues to soar.

【On how I started following esports because I got hooked on a ship.】

【The main characters are dishing out sweet moments themselves—is there anything

better than this? Of course I’m shipping them! AnYu is a lock, go for the Spring Seasonals!】

【Oh my gosh, a marksman and a support? Even better!】

【We’ve gotta start a CP super topic [^2] for them, right?】

【Looks like there’s already one—it’s called “Feathered Words For A Rest”[^3]】

【What kind of cult is “Feathered Words”? It’s definitely AnYu! An “iceberg uke” is

just too alluring!】

【There’s already a super topic with the name “AnYu”, and I think there’s also one

called “Feathered Words Bring Joy[^4].” Which one is it, girls?】

【Whoa! Are you guys that fast? Whatever, I’ll just follow them all!】

And just like that, overnight, not only were the ship super topics for Tang Yu’an and Ling Suyu created, but several of them popped up at once. Among them, “AnYu” garnered the most attention and quickly became the main hub for their fans.

Meanwhile, neither Tang Yu’an nor Ling Suyu had any idea this was happening.

Ling Suyu simply didn’t check Weibo very often to begin with.

As for Tang Yu’an, he hadn’t expected the interview video to go this viral, and with the Spring Seasonals about to begin, he didn’t want to waste his energy on anything unrelated to the championship.

However, he did spend a few minutes browsing the forums before bed.

That’s when he came across this post:

《Stop Overhyping Feather, Don’t Go Too Far》

【Feather isn’t that bad, but he’s not as good as you guys make him out to be either.

He’s lost more games than he’s won when he’s played. Aren’t you afraid of crashing and burning during the Spring Seasonals if you keep hyping him like this?】

【Is anyone actually hyping him? I haven’t checked Weibo, so I don’t know what’s

going on. What are they hyping him for? Zero deaths and Losers’ MVPs?】

【Pretty much. They’re hyping him up as if he has the skill to carry Fight. If Fight’s

marksman hadn’t been injured, would Feather even have gotten the starter’s position?】

【There’s no need to be so harsh on Feather. Although his team has lost more games

than they’ve won when he’s played, anyone who’s watched the matches in full knows his teammates had serious issues. He was just taking all the blame.】

【I agree with the OP’s point, but how can you convince people by only stating the

results without analyzing the process? Here’s my take: Although UH keeps portraying Feather as someone who’s afraid to die, I actually think his playstyle is pretty aggressive. If you’ve watched his games, you’ll see that his teammates have always been the ones adjusting to his rhythm. Take the bot-lane laning phase, for example: in most games, the support initiates the teamfight and the main damage-dealer follows up. But in Feather’s games, he usually engages first, and the support follows-up once they see him. However, Fight’s support is the team captain. If he demands that Feather coordinate with him but Feather refuses, Fight’s record this season probably wouldn’t be very good.】

【In response to the post above: I was originally going to disagree with the main post,

but after reading the comment above, I’ve been convinced. It’s true that Feather’s playstyle is pretty aggressive; I’m not sure if Fight can handle it.】

【Wait a minute, isn’t that because UH’s support sucks? UH’s support has a really low

sense of presence; it often feels like I could do just as well. A support is supposed to set the pace—what’s the point of a pro support who just follows the marksman’s lead?】

【He wasn’t that bad. When Jiang Hao was on-field, UH’s support played decently. At

the very least, he picked good timing for team fights.】

【That’s because Jiang Hao is their captain. How could the support not initiate a team

fight when he tells him to?】

【Right, Jiang Hao’s captain. So when Feather was on the field, did UH have no one

calling the shots?】

【I think they had the mid laner act as a temporary commander. In any case, it

definitely wasn’t Feather since he was a substitute.】

【No wonder UH played so poorly in the White Fox Cup. The lack of a commander

was a factor after all.】

Reading this, Tang Yu’an couldn’t help but raise an eyebrow—are you guys just figuring this out now?

He’d clicked on this thread to see who was spouting nonsense this time, only to find that most people here were actually pretty level-headed. There was no mindless bashing, and a few were even offering rational analyses. But this analysis… was way too late. How long had it been since the White Fox Cup ended? Are they just now realizing that UH’s performance was the captain’s fault?

No, he’d still overestimated them.

They’d only identified the captain as one of the factors; they hadn’t figured out that he was the main one.

Later on, they even started talking about “team chemistry.”

For professional players, team chemistry was certainly important—only by understanding your teammates’ playstyles could you coordinate better.

But Ling Suyu had been with UH for so many years. Even if he didn’t play often, surely he should’ve at least known which heroes his teammates excelled at, or whether they specialized in prolonged skirmishes or burst damage?

Even if he didn’t know, shouldn't the coach have told him before he went on field?

And even if the coach didn’t tell him, having fought so many matches during the White Fox Cup, he’d have had plenty of time to familiarize himself with the situation and adjust.

In short, in Tang Yu’an’s view, while “chemistry” was important, UH’s performance at the White Fox Cup had absolutely nothing to do with it.

Anyone who bothered to bring that up was definitely an amateur.

So, after scrolling through a few more posts, Tang Yu’an decisively chose to log out.

He shouldn’t have been browsing the forum—spending too much time on that stuff lowered one’s IQ.

The same went for Weibo.

He also didn’t plan on giving any response.

As Ling Suyu said, esports is played with your hands, not your mouth.

Winning a match spoke louder than ten thousand words.

So he didn’t know that, shortly after he left the thread, someone clicked in and posted this:

【Just a quick whisper before I’m gone—everyone in this thread, you’re going to be

proven wrong. Don’t ask me how I know.】

Then, right after that, someone else snuck in and posted this:

【Even though everyone on Fight is a jerk, your analysis is all bullsh*t! That’s all,

signing off!】

The person who posted the first reply was Linghu Gaomin, SMG’s captain and mid-laner.

The person who posted the second reply was Ma Mingxi, YJ’s team darling and marksman.

Having both been soundly defeated by Fight in succession, they were now extremely sensitive to any information related to Fight or Feather. Upon seeing this thread, they couldn’t help but click in immediately.

They had originally hoped to find some top players who could analyze Feather’s weaknesses, but when they saw a bunch of analyses concluding that “Feather is no good,”

they couldn’t help but leave a quick reply before walking away with a look of disgust.

They were fools to think they’d find top players on the forum!

They might as well just listen to their coaches’ advice and just ban sustained-damage marksmen!

Soon, on January 10, the annual Glorious Heroes Professional League (often shortened to BPL) Spring Seasonals began!

First up were the four-day qualifiers.

To ensure that teams with championship potential wouldn’t face each other before the group stage, the Spring qualifiers were grouped around the top eight teams from the last Summer Season.

The top eight teams from last season’s Summer Seasonals were:

1. Winne 2. YJ 3. UH 4. Fight 5. igame 6. S.MG 7. XY 8. Big.C All participating teams were randomly assigned to groups led by these eight teams, with only 16 teams advancing in the end, or in other words, two from each group.

Due to the large number of teams participating in the qualifiers, each round consisted of only one match, a.k.a. the best-of-one (BO1) format.

Fight’s group consisted of five teams, meaning they had to play four matches.

To advance, they would need to win at least three of those matches.

Before the match began, Chen Jie gathered and organized information on the other four teams in their group, then briefly outlined their plan: “We have to win the qualifiers, but we can’t reveal all our cards right from the start. Feather, you can keep your Cat Spirit hidden for now—as long as no team bans Hunter, you’ll play Hunter.”

“Got it,” Ling Suyu agreed immediately.

Although Cat Spirit was his main champion, as long as it was a sustained-damage marksman with mobility, it didn’t matter much which one he played. He enjoyed playing them all.

And so, in Fight’s first qualifier match, which also marked Ling Suyu’s debut as Fight’s starting marksman and his first official match with the team, under the watchful eyes of fans and industry insiders alike, Fight selected the very first marksman he’d use during the Spring Seasonals: Desert Hunter!

On screen, the Hunter, wearing a cowboy hat, clad only in a tattered leather vest, and wielding a pair of pistols, fired a shot at the screen, then tilted his head to blow away the smoke billowing from the muzzles. In his deep, slightly hoarse voice, he delivered his line:

“So, who’s my next prey?”
