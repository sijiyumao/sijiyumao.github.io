“The most comfortable relationships between people often lie in keeping things light and superficial. Language and behavior are inevitably ambiguous; while this quality adds a certain charm to social interaction, it also tempts people to get to the bottom of things and explore their origins, even if doing so doesn’t necessarily deepen the relationship.”

As Ren Jinhuan read this sentence, the plane broke through the clouds and began its descent. He said to Wen Yan, who was seated next to him, “This writer has a pretty interesting way with words. ”

Wen Yan forced his eyelids open, took the phone from him, and frowned. “These days, media outlets always love inviting pretentious media critics to elevate their narratives.”

His immediate superior scoffed, but it didn’t dampen Ren Jinhuan’s spirits. The corners of his eyes crinkled as he smiled, taking in the sunset-washed clouds outside his window. “But this person hasn’t said anything wrong. I quite like it.”

During Kuala Lumpur’s August, the sun blazed fiercely and nature ran wild. Boweinian Square was filled entirely with the frenzy of its shopping festival. It was here that “Aladdin,” the leading domestic social media platform, launched its first overseas livestream three years after entering the e-commerce sector. Fifty million in sales within an hour—a staggering figure that sent shockwaves throughout the entire internet industry. Media outlets, which had been lying in wait for some time, quickly slotted the news into their pre-prepared stories. Within a minute of the official announcement, they burst forth like birds scrambling to escape their cages.

Ren Jinhuan, one of the representatives for Aladdin’s overseas expansion project, had risen to the position of senior manager just two years after his graduation. Coupled with his strikingly good looks, he naturally won the favor of major writers, including that of those at “Today Post,” an outlet known for its sharp writing style, the very one Wen Yan had accused of elevating their narratives.

Scrolling downwards on his phone, he happened to come across a section about himself.

“Whether this young rising star will become one of Aladdin’s next generation of leaders remains to be seen, but clearly, today’s media landscape is particularly ‘fond’ of him: A reporter on the scene asked why Aladdin, which started as a social media platform, is venturing into new e-commerce—is this a last-ditch effort following slow user growth?

He replied that new e-commerce is essentially a form of ‘gentle, gradual social interaction’; only the right balance of distance and boundaries can sustain buyer-seller relationships. This is a human need that traditional e-commerce struggles to meet. Aladdin has been in operation for ten years now, and its many users have helped build its mature enterprise. We have a responsibility to create more value for our users’ lives. If we focus solely on growth, we cannot fulfill this mission—this is a process of self-renewal in response to modern society…

He spoke at a moderate pace, without any abrupt pauses that might suggest falling into thought. It was hard to tell whether his remarks were improvised or carefully scripted. While the label of “elite” was clearly attached to him, his sharp edges were rarely apparent. Polite yet not aloof, approachable yet never overstepping boundaries, embodying a perfect balance. There is no doubt in my mind that he is an outstanding practitioner of “gentle social interaction.”

The description, just over 300 characters long and accompanied by a photo, took up two mobile screens. Compared to previous posts, Today’s wording was exceptionally gentle, yet Ren Jinhuan, on the contrary, lacked his earlier sense of leisure.

He wasn’t the only one interviewed, and there were certainly many with far more seniority than him. The fact that he was given such preferential treatment was bound to invite some criticism. Moreover, with recent rumors circulating within the company regarding the department led by Wen Yan, this article seemed somewhat ill-timed.

After getting off the plane, Wen Yan drove Ren Jinhuan back to his place. Upon arriving, Wen Yan offered to walk him up the stairs, but Ren Jinhuan politely declined. It was the last day of his business trip, and Wen Yan had flown all the way to Malaysia just to pick him up for the return trip—Ren Jinhuan already felt bad enough about that. He simply wanted to keep their relationship as a strictly professional one between superior and subordinate with no ulterior motives, but Wen Yan seemed to be testing his boundaries.

Ren Jinhuan was a natural-born peacemaker who disliked straining relationships, especially with his boss. He promptly handed Wen Yan a children’s Superman costume, saying, “This is a gift for Steven.”

Steven was Wen Yan’s son from his previous marriage, and Ren Jinhuan’s deliberate mention of him clearly had the desired effect on the man in front of him. This was the essence of Ren Jinhuan’s thoughtfulness: he always offered a way out, always left room for maneuver, and never overstepped—these were the principles he upheld in his interactions with others.

Wen Yan also realized how impatient he’d been a moment ago; the hand that had been reaching for his seatbelt now slipped unnaturally onto the costume’s packaging. Ren Jinhuan said nothing. He gave Wen Yan time, intending to wait for him to make the final gesture of farewell before getting out of the car himself. This would allow Wen Yan to regain the psychological upper hand and smooth over the earlier awkwardness.

However, Wen Yan sighed at that moment. Ren Jinhuan knew this meant he wanted him to stay a little longer, so he took the opportunity to ask if he hadn’t slept well on the plane.

“While you were away on business, there were quite a few things going on within the company. Compared to that, I feel most at ease when you’re by my side.”

“Yan-ge[^1], you’ve taught me a lot as well,” Ren Jinhuan said with a smile. “When I first started, you’d send my proposals back seven or eight times every time I submitted them. To now say that you feel at ease means all your hard work wasn’t in vain.”

“Even so, you were still the best among that batch of new graduates. Everyone else’s proposals had to be revised up to twenty times.”

Wen Yan recalled the scene from two years ago, and a look of relief flashed across his face. However, it was fleeting, vanishing almost as soon as it appeared. He changed the subject: “The person Boss Fang personally recruited is about to arrive. They’ll be in charge of overseas product operations and will be collaborating with our team. I’ll find an opportunity to organize a group dinner.”

Ren Jinhuan nodded and asked about the newcomer’s background, but Wen Yan shook his head, saying no information had leaked. Even the several HR contacts he knew were keeping their lips sealed this time. “But they’ve probably worked at a major tech company overseas,” Wen Yan added.

In fact, rumors about this had already been circulating internally. Overseas product operations were set to be a key focus area in the future. Currently, a team had been temporarily re-assigned from the domestic business unit to handle it. Every senior leader, Wen Yan included, was eager to secure a piece of this lucrative pie, but the board’s decision was to recruit an experienced professional from outside the company.

Rumor had it that the company had actually extended an offer to this incoming executive a year ago, but he had delayed accepting it until recently, after the CEO personally reached out several times to finally secure his commitment.

Ren Jinhuan didn’t ask many questions; he simply chatted about amusing food anecdotes from Malaysia, making sure to inquire about the other man’s digestive health. By the end, Wen Yan seemed thoroughly satisfied and told him to go home and get some early rest. Only then did Ren Jinhuan feel confident enough to get out of the car.

However, just as he was about to open the car door, Wen Yan reached out first and pressed the interior door handle, trapping Ren Jinhuan in a confined space for those two seconds. He was slightly taken aback; whether intentional or not, this encroached on his personal space. Yet after getting out of the car, he remained composed and thanked him.

“This Sunday, Professor Yu from the R&D department is hosting a small dinner gathering. The overseas infrastructure project we worked on previously turned out quite well. I’ll bring you along to meet him—there will be other executives there as well.” Before leaving, Wen Yan extended a generous invitation. Satisfied to see a compliant smile appear on that clear, beautiful face, he drove off. Glancing in the rearview mirror, he saw him standing there watching him go, and a sense of contentment welled up inside him.

Only after the car had rounded the corner at the end of the road and disappeared completely did Ren Jinhuan turn back. But instead of heading into the apartment building, he went to the underground parking garage—ever since he’d stepped off the plane, his phone had been buzzing with messages. He took out his car keys, pressed the clutch, and casually replied to a message, saying he’d be there in twenty minutes. A white Audi pulled out of the complex, heading in the exact opposite direction from Wen Yan.

Maintaining interpersonal relationships often required a tremendous amount of energy. In recent years, personal content creators had begun calling for people to focus on their inner selves and abandon meaningless socializing. However, Ren Jinhuan believed that very few people could consistently transform solely personal investment into tangible value. The vast majority of profit opportunities in modern life stemmed from differences in information, and these were almost entirely hidden within those interpersonal relationships that appeared casual but were, in fact, carefully balanced.

Wen Yan didn’t know the background of this new figure, but that didn’t mean Ren Jinhuan couldn’t find out.

The car finally stopped in front of a trendy bar. As soon as he walked in, the metallic sounds of future house music mixed with the scent of e-cigarettes created a psychedelic summer vibe. In the early evening, the place gradually filled with people. Ren Jinhuan looked around and finally spotted the person waiting for him at one of the booths.

The man’s surname was Zhao. He’d once been a project manager in the investment banking division of a well-known securities firm, but had since branched out on his own to start a small investment brokerage firm. He enjoyed attending all kinds of auto and yacht shows and had some connections to the entertainment industry. In his mid-thirties, the richness of his private life seemed to be keeping pace with his expanding waistline.

The moment Ren Jinhuan appeared, Mr. Zhao’s previously bored expression instantly gave way to a beaming smile. He rushed over eagerly, like a stainless-steel kettle hissing after the water had come to a boil, exclaiming, “Director Ren, I’ve been waiting for you as eagerly as one waits for the moon.”

The man seemed to have just come from another dinner party, still reeking of the heavy perfumes and cosmetics typical of the nightlife scene. Ren Jinhuan didn’t seem to mind, replying, “Mr. Zhao, you flatter me. I haven’t been promoted to director yet. I just got off the plane and came straight here to see you without resting, isn’t that sincerity enough?”

Mr. Zhao had already prepared a filet mignon and a specialty cocktail for him. “People always aim for the top, you’ve got to wear your hat a little higher. I’m just congratulating you in advance. Furthermore, it won’t be long now, will it, Teacher Ren?” He reverted to his previous form of address, following the current trend across all industries of addressing others as “teacher.” He obligingly carved up the steak for Ren Jinhuan, then chatted about stocks and the cryptocurrency scene, ranging from baijiu and real estate to healthcare and new energy.

Ren Jinhuan didn’t interrupt, occasionally chiming in with a few comments. In truth, the main reason he’d come to see him was because the other man claimed to have uncovered some information about the mysterious figure about to join the company. The company’s organizational structure was about to undergo major changes, and there was another reason why Wen Yan hadn’t secured the overseas production and logistics division: the CEO and other senior executives had some reservations about Wen Yan’s performance.

“Serving the ruler is like serving a tiger” wasn’t just a trope in palace intrigue dramas; it rang especially true in a fast-paced industry like internet development. Before the axe fell, he needed to secure an escape route. Thus, shortly after the rumors began circulating, Ren Jinhuan had reached out to this Mr. Zhao sitting across from him.

Ren Jinhuan was well aware of Mr. Zhao’s personal conduct and moral character. He’d left his brokerage firm years ago primarily because of a sex scandal at work, having been reported and expelled, but on the other hand, the man certainly had a knack for networking and gathering intelligence. Ren Jinhuan had met him at a tech expo; Mr. Zhao had approached him first, and they’d gone out for dinner a few times afterward, during which Ren Jinhuan had extracted some stock insider information, netting himself a few small profits. As for the other man’s ulterior motives, Ren Jinhuan was, of course, well aware of them, but he felt that as long as the person provided value, he could keep him within his social circle. He didn’t care about such people’s character, even labelling them “information sources” in his contacts list.

Today, however, the man was simply talking too much. Ren Jinhuan had been handling wrap-up tasks on the final day of his business trip, and since he wasn’t used to sleeping on planes, he hadn’t slept for nearly forty hours. He’d been keeping himself going on coffee until now, but drowsiness had finally overtaken him.

The bar’s music style had switched to R&B. Seizing a lull in the conversation, Ren Jinhuan raised his half-empty glass and smiled. “Mr. Zhao, aren’t you planning to join the dance floor?” A native of Jiangsu and Zhejiang, his eyes always seemed to hold a subtle blend of tenderness and intellectual grace. Perhaps because he was tired now, that tenderness and grace resembled the morning mist and waters of the southern mountains, with a hint of words left unsaid. As he smiled and let his gaze drift over nonchalantly, it left his companion utterly spellbound.

Mr. Zhao’s heart fluttered. He pulled a document out of his briefcase and said, chewing on his steak, “This information was really hard to dig up, but since it was a job for you, Teacher Ren, I’d go through fire and water to obtain it.”

Ren Jinhuan opened the file as Mr. Zhao gave a vague introduction: “His surname is Jin, full name Jin Xiangtang, and his English name is Eric. He majored in mathematics as an undergraduate at University A and minored in economics at University B. He earned his master’s degree in computer science at CMU in the U.S. After graduation, he first joined Facebook, then moved to Amazon. You have to admit, his resume is quite impressive. He’s got experience in both social media and e-commerce, which perfectly aligns with your company’s business. And the kicker is, he’s only 28 years old.”

Ren Jinhuan was slightly surprised. He’d assumed the candidate would have at least eight years of experience, like Wen Yan, but it turned out he was only two years older than himself. The file was just two pages long and didn’t include a photo. Just as Ren Jinhuan was about to search for him on LinkedIn and related platforms, Mr. Zhao interrupted: “Everything you can find online, as well as the stuff you can’t find, are all right here. I just couldn’t get a real photo of him.” He then began nitpicking about the finance industry, but this was simply a “hook.” Ren Jinhuan could tell he was holding back pieces of key information, so he played along and finished the rest of the wine with him.

“I’ve got another exclusive scoop. Even if this person ends up joining your company, I guarantee very few of your staff will know about this.” Mr. Zhao, emboldened by the alcohol, sat down next to Ren Jinhuan, resting one hand on his left shoulder, his sycophantic gaze practically glued to Ren’s face. Ren Jinhuan glanced at the hand, his expression barely changing. He tilted his head slightly and raised the corners of his eyebrows, signaling that he was all ears.

“This person’s relationship with your Aladdin’s chairman isn’t just any ordinary one.” Mr. Zhao paused, then lowered his voice. “He’s actually the nephew of Director Lin’s wife.”

So that was the connection, Ren Jinhuan realized. Having obtained the information he sought, he had no desire to continue playing along with the man. Without giving anything away, he gently moved Mr. Zhao’s hand away, thanked him for the hospitality, and said he’d see him later. Mr. Zhao’s expression immediately darkened. In the past, Ren Jinhuan had always been happy to put on a friendly face and let him lead the conversation. But this time, after having gone out of his way to gather information for him and waited eagerly all evening, Mr. Zhao wasn’t about to let him leave so easily: “It’s only nine o’clock, it’s not even that late… Let’s go sing some karaoke in a bit, order some barbecue and beer, and then head back. It won’t be too late to head back after that.”

Ren Jinhuan mainly wanted to go home and rest. He could hold his liquor fairly well but, perhaps because he hadn’t slept well, he now felt dizzy and nauseous, so he suggested they make plans for another time. However, as soon as he stood up, the alcohol suddenly hit him. The severe dizziness caused his vision to blur, and the music and voices around him appeared as if echoing from a cheap microphone—at times near, other times far, and distorted.

Mr. Zhao stepped in front of him and said smugly, “Teacher Ren, oh, Teacher Ren, why do you just love to mess with us common folk like this? When it suits you, you play dumb; when it doesn’t, you start drawing boundaries. You’ve strung me along time and time again—you’ve got to let me have a little fun too.”

Usually, when Mr. Zhao texted him to meet up, about seven or eight out of ten times nothing came of it. Every time he thought the conversation had died, Ren Jinhuan would always find the perfect moment to reply, and when they met, he’d gone out of his way to curry favor time and time again. Yet in the end, every blow landed on cotton. At first, Mr. Zhao thought it was a difference in social circles, that Ren Jinhuan might simply lack worldly savvy, but later he figured it out: Ren Jinhuan was all too well-versed in these ways, and he was clearly using himself as bait.

Mr. Zhao put his arm around him: “If you want to take a nap, I’ll arrange a room. Shall we rest together?”

Ren Jinhuan struggled to keep his composure. It would be a lie to say he wasn’t angry, but he still managed to control his emotions, steady his mind, and smile at him: “Mr. Zhao, as a businessman, you naturally understand the principle of leaving some leeway in your dealings with others. To do business with you, I need to get to know you better. You’re not the only one who knows how to dig up information. Some things are better handled behind the scenes than out in the open. After all, I certainly don’t want to lose a friend like you.” Mr. Zhao was taken aback by his words. Ren Jinhuan seized the moment to push him away and headed toward the door on instinct, but he clung to him like a leech, refusing to let go.

A warm white glow cast itself onto the wall, following him like a camera flash. Ahead lay a narrow path, while behind him was incessant pestering. The crowd gradually swayed in the shifting light and shadow, The house singer on stage hummed a nostalgic, tender Cantonese ballad. The scene was surreal and bizarre, as if four layers of walls were blocking him in, leaving no room to breathe. Ren Jinhuan unconsciously stopped in his tracks, the throbbing pain in his head leaving him disoriented.

The lights dimmed, the singer took their bow, and a faint scent of perfume wafted through the air like a rose-scented incense just unsealed, mingling with a cool, pearlescent aroma. It was wondrous, perplexing, yet utterly captivating. Then he heard a voice from beside him asking, “Do you need help again this time?” He slowly turned his head just as the lights went out across the entire venue for a brief moment. In the darkness, his sense of smell became exceptionally keen. The scent enveloped him, as if refusing to let him go, to the point where the exit, tangled crowd, and singing all seemed to vanish. The fourfold walls came crashing down.

Ren Jinhuan couldn’t remember if he’d answered, but he suddenly recalled a late night many years ago when someone seemed to have said to him, “Getting something for free through social maneuvering is often dangerous, and overestimating oneself is also a form of foolishness. Your perception in this regard is, in fact, surprisingly sluggish…”
