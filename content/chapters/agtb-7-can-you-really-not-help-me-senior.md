Ren Jinhuan brought his lunch to the Data Architecture Department on floor D5. He made his way effortlessly through the crowded workstations, found an empty chair, and sat down, quietly waiting for the man holding the stand-up meeting not far away—his senior from the same university, Yang Zheng.

Yang Zheng was a workaholic who led a team of about a hundred people. Getting to where he was today had truly taken a great deal of effort. As usual, this stand-up meeting had dragged on past noon. Just as Yang Zheng was asking the last presenter about project details, he turned his head and spotted Ren Jinhuan. He beamed and waved at him, his smile rippling like spring water into Ren’s eyes, leaving him momentarily dazed. It was the final push before the project launch, but the meeting had revealed risks of delays. Yang Zheng had been feeling somewhat dissatisfied, but at that very moment, that frustration seemed to vanish like a cork pulled out by a corkscrew, tossed aside as if it were nothing.

After the meeting ended, Ren Jinhuan saw how relieved Yang Zheng’s team members were as they hurried toward the cafeteria. He smiled and reminded him, “ “Senior, next time you host a meeting, you’ll have to order lunch for everyone. Otherwise, the younger team members will think you’re pushing them too hard.”

Yang Zheng hurriedly explained, “It’s mainly because upper management is pressuring us hard, and data resources are tight. I had them start on a contingency plan weeks ago, but they still haven’t finished it.” He was a man who paid close attention to work details and was extremely responsible, though his management style was a bit rigid.

Ren Jinhuan sensed the stress in his voice, told him not to worry, and accompanied him to the dining area. When Yang Zheng opened his lunchbox and discovered it was a light, healthy meal suited for fitness, he was taken by surprise. As if reading his mind, Ren Jinhuan said, “You shared a photo of your running records on Moments[^1] last time, and you’ve been consistently among the top few on the calorie-burn leaderboard lately, so I figured you were working out.”

Yang Zheng smiled sheepishly. He had indeed hired a personal trainer recently and hadn’t told anyone, and he hadn’t expected Ren Jinhuan to notice. Yet beneath his embarrassment, he felt a twinge of delight. At the very least, Ren Jinhuan had been paying attention to his updates, which showed through this unique, thoughtful gesture.

With that thought, Yang Zheng looked across the table once again. Ren Jinhuan was mixing salad dressing, moving at a leisurely pace, as if creating a delicate piece of handicraft. Yang Zheng recalled how Ren Jinhuan had eaten the same way during their college days—polite and refined, chewing slowly and quietly, without making a sound—to the point that Yang Zheng, a northerner, had actually felt a bit self-conscious back then. How many years had it been since then? About eight, he thought. Back when Yang Zheng had come to S University for graduate school, his impression of people from the south was that they were petty and spoke in soft, affected voices. Later, he met Ren Jinhuan, who had just started his freshman year. Although he was younger than himself, he was sharp-witted, spoke with great insight, and possessed a beautiful yet unassuming face. When he smiled, he gave off a “first-love” vibe, a quality that perfectly aligned with local cultural aesthetics. This was rare; while people loved new things, it was often just “love at first sight,” whereas someone like Ren Jinhuan was the kind of person you never grew tired of, no matter how long you spent with him.

It was also around that time that Yang Zheng first realized how wonderful the south truly was. People raised amidst its beautiful waters and green mountains were naturally delicate and attractive.

Lost in those distant memories, Yang Zheng found himself eating more slowly without even realizing it, until Ren Jinhuan reminded him. When he looked up, he met his gaze, a puzzled expression, and instantly, that same sense of unease he’d felt all those years ago returned.

“Senior Brother [^2], is the food I bought not to your liking? I’ve noticed you haven’t eaten much.” Ren Jinhuan asked with concern.

Yang Zheng quickly assured him that wasn’t the case, explaining that some work-related matters were weighing on his mind, and he swiftly changed the subject: “Xiao-Jin[^3], why did you suddenly decide to learn Texas Hold’em?”

Ren Jinhuan kept his gaze fixed on him and replied after a two-second pause: “I was a little curious when I heard you mention it earlier, and it seemed like a lot of people at the company played it, so I wanted to learn about what’s currently popular in your poker circle. That way, I’ll have more things in common to talk about with everyone, including you, Senior Brother.” The last sentence was spoken softly, as if merely an afterthought, yet it tickled his heart like a feather. Yang Zheng lowered his head, took a few bites of rice, and said with a smile, “You can talk about anything you want. ” Even if the topic was unfamiliar, he was willing to listen.

Soon after, the two arrived at the café they’d booked in advance. Yang Zheng took out a deck of cards and explained the rules of Texas Hold’em from start to finish, his reasoning clear and his explanation well-structured. He’d often tutored others in the past, so he was genuinely skilled at this.

After listening, Ren Jinhuan got the general idea—the rules weren’t actually that complicated—but after playing a few practice hands with Yang Zheng, he lost all his playing chips. It was rare for Yang Zheng to see his junior look so taken aback, and he felt a sense of control: “Although Texas Hold’em may seem like a game of luck, luck isn’t the dominant factor. It requires calculating probabilities, reading your opponents’ minds, and misleading other players.” He taught him a few more practical tips, noting that the game involved game theory, which was why many of the company’s top executives enjoyed playing it.

“From what I know, Vice President Yu is a truly skilled player, and Mr. Qin Heng and others occasionally share their insights in the Texas Hold’em group. There are some incredibly talented people in this circle who are called ‘omniscient’—they can not only calculate their own hands but also estimate the odds of every opponent at the table.”

As Ren Jinhuan listened to him describe the playing styles of Vice President Yu and the others, he gradually came to a conclusion: given his limited free time, it was simply impossible to become a proficient Texas Hold’em player within three days.

Seeing him frown in silence, Yang Zheng reassured him, “Take it slow, and you’ll get the hang of it after playing for a while.” But Ren Jinhuan didn’t have that much time. The Wednesday night poker game was just two days away. He had already given up on a direct approach and was rapidly mulling over what indirect paths might be available. Just then, Yang Zheng suddenly spoke up, interrupting his train of thought.

“How are things going for you at Wen Yan’s? Our department will be creating some positions in strategy analysis soon; why don’t you come check it out?”

Ren Jinhuan simply replied that things were fine for now and everything was going smoothly, without giving a direct answer. Yang Zheng wanted to press the issue, pointing out that Wen Yan’s team required a lot of overtime and expressing concern that the pressure might be too much for him. If he came over, at least he’d be under Yang Zheng’s watchful eye.

Ren Jinhuan didn’t call him out on his true intentions. Recalling what had happened at the stand-up meeting earlier, he couldn’t help but chuckle: “But Senior Brother, you also made your team members work overtime this afternoon.” This half-joking observation made Yang Zheng feel a bit embarrassed. He really wanted to say, “If it were you, I’d never do that,” but that would have been a bit too blunt—almost like tearing down the veil between them. So gradually, he didn’t know how to continue, and Ren Jinhuan noticed every shift in his expression.

Ren Jinhuan had always been skilled at reading people’s expressions and body language. He’d known about Yang Zheng’s feelings as far back as college. At first, it had indeed been nothing more than ordinary alumni camaraderie, but when exactly had it changed? Ren Jinhuan couldn’t recall a specific incident, but he’d always been sensitive to the emotions and temperaments of those around him. Coupled with the fact that Yang Zheng’s gaze was almost completely unmasked, and that it wasn’t the first time he’d been stared at like that, he quickly pieced together the other man’s secret.

On the night Yang Zheng graduated from graduate school, he called Ren Jinhuan while drunk. His speech was disjointed and halting, but it was all about the old friendship they’d shared over those two years. Ren Jinhuan was preparing for his final exam at the time; he worked through his problems while calmly listening to Yang Zheng talk for three hours. In Yang Zheng’s account on the other end of the line, he heard how Yang Zheng saw him—a figure so unfamiliar it was as if shrouded in a veil, hazy and indistinct, imbued by Yang Zheng with layer upon layer of allure.

People always seem to dare to reveal their true feelings only at the moment of parting, but behind this “bravery” lay cover-ups and consolations for their cowardice—an unconscious cruelty toward genuine emotions, as well as simple self-pity. Yang Zheng had never truly seen him for who he was, and he had never given Yang Zheng the chance to do so.

Ren Jinhuan didn’t hang up that phone call until Yang Zheng had fallen asleep. At the time, he had replied with just one sentence—a rare display of sincerity: “Senior Brother, you think too highly of me.”

After leaving the café, Yang Zheng walked Ren Jinhuan to the base of Building F, where his office was located, and told him to contact him anytime if he needed to transfer departments. Just as Ren Jinhuan was about to nod, he suddenly heard someone call out to him from behind: “Good afternoon, Manager Ren.” He turned around and saw Jin Xiangtang, dressed in a business suit, walking toward him with a lively, energetic air. Ren Jinhuan silently studied him, his eyes unconsciously taking on a hint of admiration; he thought the pair of black leather shoes on Jin Xiangtang’s feet looked quite handsome.

Yang Zheng asked who he was, and Ren Jinhuan introduced the two. Upon hearing that he was the new head of Overseas Production & Logistics, Yang Zheng was surprised. Everyone had previously assumed the position would be held by someone in their thirties or older with years of experience, but the man in question turned out to be unexpectedly young. Jin Xiangtang didn’t notice the subtle expression on Yang Zheng’s face; instead, he shook hands with him in a dignified and composed manner, while catching a glimpse of the playing cards in Yang Zheng’s left hand.

Yang Zheng had to leave for a meeting, so he bid Ren Jinhuan farewell. Before leaving, he asked hopefully, “Xiao-Jin, I have two tickets to the Qilin Society’s production of *The Underwater Garden*. You mentioned previously that you wanted to see it but missed your chance. Would you like to go with me this time?”

Ren Jinhuan noted that when Yang Zheng said “previously,” he must have been referring to a post he’d shared on his social media feed last year. However, Shi Lu had snagged the two extra tickets back then, so he had actually already seen the play—and found the performance more disappointing than he’d expected. Considering that Yang Zheng had indeed helped him out today, he decided to go along with his suggestion.

Jin Xiangtang watched Yang Zheng’s retreating figure, then turned to Ren Jinhuan and gave him a meaningful look. “I just noticed an interesting pattern.”

“Tell me about it,” Ren Jinhuan said, curious to know what he meant.

Jin Xiangtang smiled at him: “Why is it that every time I see you, there’s a different person fawning over you?”

That was certainly a tricky question. Ren Jinhuan was taken aback for a moment, but then recalled the other person’s veiled remarks from last night and replied with a subtle dig of his own. “I never would have thought that the first issue Director Jin would be concerned with after taking office would be my personal social life, rather than business development.”

“Then may I take up a bit of your personal social time right now? ” Jin Xiangtang seamlessly picked up on his remark and continued, “I just started today and am not yet familiar with the office layout. Manager Ren, would you be willing to serve as my mentor for the time being?”

Ren Jinhuan tilted his head slightly to look at him. Having just experienced the other man’s “sweet talk” firsthand, and now faced with this task of guiding him—which fell outside his official duties—he put on a smile filled with grace and composure: “With pleasure.”[^4]

Aladdin’s headquarters was located between the Third and Fourth Ring Roads[^5]. It’s said that the company originally purchased the entire plot of land and commissioned an architectural firm to design it specifically in the style of a university campus. In addition to eight main office buildings, it provided employees with extra amenities such as karaoke rooms, video game rooms, gyms, libraries, and more. They’d also attracted businesses to open 24-hour unmanned supermarkets, coffee shops, and bubble tea stores on the premises. But the most appealing feature—and the one most highly praised by the entire tech industry—was Aladdin’s in-house cafeteria. After all, “food is the foundation of life,” and thanks to the trend of hyper-competition within the administrative sector, weekly meal plans had to undergo professional nutritional evaluation and analysis before being served. On the professional social media apps, Aladdin was nicknamed the “Lamp Factory”—sometimes called the “industry’s dark lamp” when things went wrong, and the “industry’s beacon” when they didn’t—but you’d always see comments like this: “As long as there are no layoffs, I’m willing to keep competing for the Lamp Factory’s meals for another day.”

As they walked, Ren Jinhuan introduced each feature to Jin Xiangtang one by one. They eventually arrived at the central plaza, where workers had already refilled the fountain pool with fresh water. After finishing the tour, Ren Jinhuan happened to glance at the reflection in the pool—blue skies and white clouds, a scene uniquely beautiful here—and he sincerely praised, “Director Jin, you’ve really put a lot of thought into your outfit today.” He was never stingy with praise for things or people that appealed to his aesthetic sensibilities.

Jin Xiangtang didn’t shy away from the compliment either, replying casually, “It is my first day, after all. I had to put on a bit of a show.” He seemed completely at ease.

The two walked halfway around the fountain. Just then, they happened to see colleagues from the Marketing & Branding Department filming a promotional video for new graduates on the main path, so they chose a side path leading to the tree-lined walkway in the botanical garden. The winding path led to a secluded spot; the further they went, the fewer people there were. They even spotted a couple sitting close together on a bench—it had the feel of a “couples’ path” on a college campus. Ren Jinhuan discreetly turned a corner, and finally, the scenery opened up.

“How was your first day on the job, Director Jin?”

“Not bad. Everyone’s just as warm and welcoming as you, Manager Ren.”

“The pace here is fast, likely different from overseas. I hope that even after you’ve been here for a while, Director Jin, you’ll still be able to ‘Always Day One.’”

“Always Day One” was a philosophy championed by Amazon, Jin Xiangtang’s former employer, meaning to maintain innovation like a startup. Translated into local terms, it was similar to “staying true to one’s original aspirations.” Ren Jinhuan tilted his head to look at him, his expression innocent and sincere: “I look forward to the day Director Jin grows into an ‘old bird.’”

“Old bird” referred not only to a “veteran in the workplace,” but also carried the derogatory connotation of a “cunning old fox.” Faced with this double entendre, Jin Xiangtang couldn’t help but stop in his tracks. He looked at the “tour guide” beside him and chuckled, “Do all the mentors at our company have such a silver tongue as Manager Ren?”

“I just happened to have stayed in the debating circles,” Ren Jinhuan replied calmly and unhurriedly.

“No wonder,” Jin Xiangtang changed the subject, using a double entendre of his own. “But rather than pinning your hopes on me, Manager Ren, perhaps you should ‘check’ yourself.”

“Check” was a poker term meaning to fold. Ren Jinhuan was momentarily taken aback, but quickly realized that Jin Xiangtang had seen through the lie he’d told the night before. Jin Xiangtang plucked a leaf from a tree, twirled it between his fingers a few times, and said leisurely, “I actually admire you—you’re really bold, daring to make such a bold claim in front of Vice President Yu and the others.”

Ren Jinhuan remained silent for a while, his gaze cast downwards, as though Jin Xiangtang’s words had stirred a sense of unease within him. The two were standing at the exit of the botanical garden. It was September, and the two rows of pomegranate trees were already bearing small, unripe fruits, while vast stretches of green lawn stretched out all around them. In the afternoon, the gardeners turned on the circular sprinklers, and several streams of water sprayed out in circles toward them. Just then, Jin Xiangtang, quick on his feet, gently pulled Ren Jinhuan to his side, but half of Ren Jinhuan’s hair and shoulder still got wet.

“So do I have absolutely no chance of winning?” Ren Jinhuan whispered.

Jin Xiangtang turned to look at him, finding something inexplicably stubborn in the way he was struggling to survive. “Either you’re a poker prodigy who can tap into your talent in the time ahead, or you possess such incredible luck that it trumps all meticulous calculations and strategic play. Or, perhaps, you’ll be lucky enough to find an omniscient player who’ll team up with you to help you cheat.”

“Option one is clearly not me; option two is something you wouldn’t even find in a novel.”

Jin Xiangtang had anticipated as much, so he only commented on the third option: “You could go find your Yan-ge. Although he doesn’t look like a top-tier player, he’ll at least help you.” With that, he stepped out, intending to head straight back to the office building. It was exactly 2:00 p.m.

Just then, however, Ren Jinhuan spoke up from behind him. “I don’t plan on going to Yan-ge.”

Jin Xiangtang turned around and watched as he slowly walked up to him. He lifted his face—which could indeed be called handsome—and looked at Jin Xiangtang with a mix of cautious probing and hopeful pleading. His long, slender eyelashes resembled an unfurled peacock’s fan, still dotted with droplets of water he hadn’t had time to wipe away. The slightly-damp right side of his cheek gave him an air of innate gentleness. Jin Xiangtang seemed to have anticipated what he was about to say, yet he was still somewhat surprised—

He had anticipated the first part: “Can you really not help me?”

What took him by surprise was the way he addressed him. He didn’t call him “Director Jin,” but “Senior.”[^6]
